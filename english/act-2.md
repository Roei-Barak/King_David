# A PSALM OF DAVID

# ACT II

## The King, His House, and the Threshing Floor

> Scenes 33–36 follow the interpretive reading Roei selected from the lessons of Rabbi Eyal Vered. It is one reading among others and is not presented to the audience as consensus. See `source-notes.md`.

---

## Scene 32 — The Envoys

*Jerusalem. The king's court. The table of Act One is still there, and the seat that was filled. The silence is broken by men coming in from the road.*

*DAVID's ENVOYS — sent to comfort Hanun, the new king of Ammon, on the death of his father. Half of each man's beard has been shaved off. Their robes have been cut away at the hips. They are trying to cover themselves with their hands, and with each other.*

*(DAVID sees them from across the court. He stops his attendants with a gesture: no one is to stare. He goes to them himself.)*

**DAVID**
I sent you to carry kindness. His father was kind to me when I had nowhere to stand.

**ENVOY**
*(not lifting his head)* His princes said to him: Do you think David honors your father? He has sent spies, to search out the city. So they —

*(He cannot finish.)*

**DAVID**
I see what they did.

*(He takes off his own outer robe and puts it round the man.)*

"Stay at Jericho until your beards have grown. Then come back."

**ENVOY**
How are we to go home to our own people like this?

**DAVID**
You are not. That is why I say Jericho. When you come home, you will not have to hide your faces.

*(The ENVOY bows — and chooses to leave by the side, along the wall, among his fellows; not across the open court. DAVID watches them out. JOAB is at his elbow.)*

**JOAB**
Ammon knows what it has done. They have hired Aram — twenty thousand foot, and the kings of the north behind them.

**DAVID**
Take the host. All the fighting men.

*The field before Rabbah. Two enemies, front and rear. JOAB and ABISHAI, with the host between them.*

**JOAB**
Aram in the open; Ammon at the gate. Take the rest and face Ammon. I will take the picked men against Aram.

"If Aram is too strong for me, you shall come and save me. And if the sons of Ammon are too strong for you, I will come and save you."

*(He grips his brother's arm. There is a third brother who is not there.)*

"Be strong — and let us be strong for our people, and for the cities of our God. And the LORD do what is good in His eyes."

*(Battle; Aram breaks; Ammon withdraws behind its wall. Seasons turn. DAVID himself goes out and breaks Aram again at Helam. Then:)*

**A VOICE**
"And it happened, at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go out, that David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel; and they laid waste the sons of Ammon, and besieged Rabbah. But David stayed in Jerusalem."

*The gate of Jerusalem. The host is marching out to the siege. At the gate sits a SCRIBE with a chest. Each man, as he passes, puts a small written tablet into the SCRIBE's hand, and goes on. It is routine; they hardly break step. DAVID and JOAB watch from above.*

**SCRIBE**
Name.

**SOLDIER**
Elhanan, of Bethlehem.

**SCRIBE**
Wife.

**SOLDIER**
I have written it. There.

*(The tablet goes into the chest. Next.)*

**JOAB**
*(to DAVID)* The custom of your house. I thought it soft when you ordered it.

**DAVID**
I have seen what becomes of a woman whose man is neither dead nor come home. No daughter of Israel is to be left chained to an empty road on my account. He writes her free before he goes. If he comes back, let him court her again.

**JOAB**
Most of them think they are paying a tax.

*(Below, a tall soldier has reached the SCRIBE: foreign-born, an officer now. URIAH.)*

**SCRIBE**
Name.

**URIAH**
Uriah. The Hittite.

**SCRIBE**
Wife.

**URIAH**
Bathsheba. Daughter of Eliam.

*(He holds the tablet a moment longer than the others did. Then he gives it.)*

**SCRIBE**
It is in the chest. You know what it is.

**URIAH**
I know what it says.

*(He looks up — and sees the king looking down. The two men from the valley of Elah. URIAH salutes: correct, and no more. He marches out. A sound, very faint: one note from the fall of Goliath.)*

**DAVID**
The shield-bearer.

**JOAB**
One of your thirty. He is the best man I have on a wall.

*(JOAB turns to go.)*

**DAVID**
And I?

**JOAB**
Rabbah is a siege. A siege is digging and waiting. That is my trade. Yours is here.

*(A glance at the house; at the bare height above it.)*

Gather your cedar.

*(He goes down to the host. The gate empties. DAVID is left above a city with no soldiers in it.)*

## Scene 33 — Bathsheba

*The roof of the king's house, toward evening. DAVID alone. On a table: the rolled plan; samples of stone; tallies of cedar, gold, bronze. He has been working at them. He cannot settle.*

**DAVID**
*(to the rolled plan)* "He shall build." And he is not born. Six wives; sons enough to fill a table; and Nathan says: one who shall come.

*(He walks to the parapet. He looks out over the lower city — and stops. The audience sees only his face. Below, somewhere, a woman is bathing: washing herself clean, as the law requires.)*

*(He looks for some time. Then he turns away from the parapet, deliberately, and calls.)*

**DAVID**
Who is the woman?

**ATTENDANT**
"Is that not Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam — the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

*(The name. The faint note again.)*

**DAVID**
Uriah went out with the host.

**ATTENDANT**
With the first companies.

**DAVID**
Then her name is in the scribe's chest. Go down to the gate. Ask it openly, before whoever is there: how does it stand with Bathsheba daughter of Eliam? Bring me the answer aloud. I will have nothing whispered.

*(The ATTENDANT goes. Light: the SCRIBE at his chest, the tablet in his hand, elders of the gate around him.)*

**SCRIBE**
Uriah the Hittite gave his writ at the going out of the host, as every man did. It is whole. It is witnessed. The woman is in her father's house, and free.

*The king's house — by day; the doors open. BATHSHEBA has come — not led: accompanied, by women of her father's house. She is composed. She looks at the king directly.*

**BATHSHEBA**
The king inquired after me at the gate.

**DAVID**
I did.

**BATHSHEBA**
In front of the elders.

**DAVID**
I wanted it heard.

**BATHSHEBA**
It was heard. The whole lower city has it by now. So I have come up to ask, before it grows another head: does the king send for me, or does the king ask?

**DAVID**
I ask. The tablet in that chest makes you free to answer me either way.

*(She considers him.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
My grandfather sits in your council. I have known since I could walk what a king's house is. Why me?

*(DAVID could say several things. He says the true one.)*

**DAVID**
I have been told that a son will be born to me who is not yet born. He is to build what I am not permitted to build.

*(He touches the plan.)*

I have been looking for his mother. I did not know it until this evening.

**BATHSHEBA**
From a roof.

**DAVID**
From a roof.

*(A pause. She does not smile; but she does not look away either.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
Uriah wrote that tablet because the scribe held out the pen. He wrote it like a man paying a tax at a bridge. He means to come home from Rabbah and break it over his knee.

**DAVID**
What he means is his own. What he wrote is written.

**BATHSHEBA**
And if he stands in your gate and says that I am his wife?

**DAVID**
Then he will be told, before witnesses, what he signed.

*(She takes that in. Then, a king's counsellor's granddaughter, she states terms.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
If I come into this house, I come by the front gate. At noon. With my father's name said aloud, and witnesses who can be found again.

**DAVID**
By the front gate. At noon.

*(Noon. The front gate of the king's house. Witnesses. BATHSHEBA daughter of Eliam enters as DAVID's wife. It is done in the light.)*

*Later. DAVID among his tallies. A WOMAN of BATHSHEBA's household.*

**WOMAN**
My mistress sends to the king. She says: "I am with child."

*(DAVID is very still. He looks at the rolled plan. Something like joy; and under it, at once, a thing that must be put in order.)*

**DAVID**
*(to a messenger)* To Joab, at Rabbah: "Send me Uriah the Hittite."

## Scene 34 — "Wash Your Feet"

*The king's house. A clear line runs from its door, down through the city, to the door of a house in the lower town — Uriah's. Servants of the king stand about the gate, as they always do.*

*URIAH comes in from the siege: dust, a soldier's bearing. He stands before DAVID.*

**DAVID**
Is it well with Joab? Is it well with the people? How does the war go?

**URIAH**
The city is still shut, my lord the king. They made two sallies from the water-gate. We hold the springs. Joab says: by the next new moon, or the one after.

*(DAVID hears him out to the end. Then he sends the servants back a little way — not out of sight.)*

**DAVID**
Uriah. You gave a writ at the gate when the host went out.

**URIAH**
Every man gave one.

**DAVID**
Yours has been acted upon. At noon, by the front gate, before witnesses, Bathsheba daughter of Eliam came into this house as my wife.

*(URIAH does not move. Not a muscle.)*

I did not want you to hear it in the camp, from a man who had it from another man. I wanted you to hear it from me.

*(Silence.)*

You stood beside a dead giant once, and asked me for a wife from Israel. I gave my word, and it was kept. You wrote her free with your own hand. There is a house in the lower city with your arms in it and your goods. What was between you and her is finished, and it must be seen to be finished.

*(He gives the order.)*

"Go down to your house, and wash your feet."

*(URIAH salutes. He turns, and goes out. A servant follows him, carrying a gift from the king's table.)*

*(DAVID goes to where he can see the line down through the city. URIAH comes out of the king's door. He stops. He does not go down. He sits — at the door of the king's house, among the king's servants. Night. He lies down there and sleeps, in every man's sight.)*

*Morning. The gate; the servants; URIAH rising from the ground. An ATTENDANT has told DAVID. DAVID comes out.*

**DAVID**
"Have you not come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

*(URIAH answers — and not low. Every servant at the gate hears.)*

**URIAH**
"The Ark, and Israel, and Judah are living in shelters. My lord Joab and the servants of my lord are camped on the open field. And I — shall I go into my house, to eat, and to drink, and to lie with my wife?"

*(The servants look at one another. "My wife.")*

"As you live, and as your soul lives — I will not do this thing."

*(It hangs there, in the king's own gate, in front of the king's own household. DAVID's eyes rest on him. "My lord Joab." "My wife." He does not answer in anger. He does not answer the words at all.)*

**DAVID**
"Stay here today as well. Tomorrow I will send you."

*Evening. The king's table. DAVID has had URIAH brought in to eat and drink before him. The cup is filled, and filled again. URIAH drinks like a soldier ordered to. The servants come and go.*

**DAVID**
They heard you this morning. All of them.

**URIAH**
I spoke as a soldier speaks.

**DAVID**
You stood in the king's gate and said "my lord Joab." And of the king's wife you said "my wife."

*(URIAH turns the cup in his fingers. He does not take anything back. He does not add anything.)*

**DAVID**
I am giving you a second day. Take your arms out of that house. Go down by the street, where it can be seen. Let there be an end.

**URIAH**
*(thick with wine; perfectly steady)* The Ark is in a tent, my lord.

*(He gets up, with care. He bows. He goes out — and at the door, again, he lies down on his pallet among the servants of his lord. He does not go down to his house.)*

*Night. DAVID. The SCRIBE has been sent for, and has come with the chest.*

**SCRIBE**
My lord — he has done it twice, and in the gate. By tomorrow it will be in the lower city: that the king's wife is another man's wife, and that the man said so to the king's face.

**DAVID**
I know what it is.

**SCRIBE**
There is a word for a man who defies the king before the king's servants.

**DAVID**
I know the word.

**SCRIBE**
Then let it be judged. I have the writ. The witnesses of the gate are alive; so are the witnesses of the noon. Sit the elders down tomorrow, in the open. Let the tablet be read out. Let him answer it. And when it has been judged, no one in Israel will be able to say —

**DAVID**
And when it has been judged, he hangs. As a rebel. In front of the host he has served for twenty years.

*(He walks away from the chest.)*

That man knelt in the valley of Elah with both armies looking on, and undid a knot I could not find. He is the best soldier Joab has on a wall. I will not put a rope on him.

**SCRIBE**
My lord —

**DAVID**
He shall have a soldier's death, and a soldier's name. That much I can give him.

**SCRIBE**
*(quietly)* And what will they say of the king?

**DAVID**
Let them say what they like.

*(The SCRIBE looks at him a moment; bows; takes up the chest with the tablet in it, and carries it out, unread.)*

*(DAVID sits down before a blank sheet. He writes. He stops. He writes again.)*

## Scene 35 — The Letter

*Morning. URIAH stands before DAVID, ready for the road. The letter is in the king's hand, sealed.*

**DAVID**
You are going back to Joab.

*(He holds it out. And — though no word of it is spoken to URIAH — we hear what the hand wrote:)*

**DAVID'S VOICE**
"Set Uriah in the front of the fiercest fighting. Then draw back from behind him — that he may be struck down, and die."

*(URIAH looks at the sealed letter. At the king. Whatever he understands, he does not say.)*

**URIAH**
I will carry it.

*(He takes it, salutes, and goes. DAVID's hand is still extended for a moment after the letter has left it. It is a gesture we have seen: a king, sending a man where the enemy will do it. He lowers the hand. He stays facing the empty place.)*

*The siege lines before Rabbah. JOAB breaks the seal; reads. He looks up at URIAH, standing at attention, waiting for orders. He reads it again. He does not ask anything.*

**JOAB**
*(folding it away)* The water-gate. Where their best men are. You will take the point.

**URIAH**
Yes, my lord.

*(The wall. A rush toward the gate; a sally from the city; arrows from above. URIAH is at the front. Behind him the line draws back. He falls. He is not the only one: along the rank, other places stand suddenly empty — men who were only standing where they were told.)*

*(JOAB looks at the gaps. He counts them. Then, to a MESSENGER:)*

**JOAB**
Tell the king everything about the fighting. If his anger rises — if he says: Why did you go so close to the wall? Did you not know they shoot from the wall? — then say:

"Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also."

*Jerusalem. The MESSENGER before DAVID.*

**MESSENGER**
The men got the better of us, and came out against us into the field. We drove them back to the entrance of the gate. Then the archers shot at your servants from the wall. Some of the king's servants are dead.

*(A breath.)*

"And your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead also."

*(Silence. DAVID's face does not change. That is the terrible thing about it.)*

**DAVID**
"Say this to Joab: Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes. The sword devours this one and that one alike. Press your attack on the city, and overthrow it." And hearten him.

*(The MESSENGER goes.)*

*BATHSHEBA's rooms. She has torn her garment. She is sitting on the floor: she is mourning URIAH — "her husband," as all the city calls him. DAVID comes to the door, and waits there.*

**BATHSHEBA**
He was not a wicked man.

**DAVID**
No.

**BATHSHEBA**
He carried a shield for a giant, and then he carried one for you. He ate his bread standing up. I have wept for him this week, and I am not ashamed of it.

**DAVID**
You should not be.

*(She looks up at him.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
Do you know what they are saying in the lower city?

**DAVID**
Tell me.

**BATHSHEBA**
That the king lay with a soldier's wife while the soldier was at the wall. That when she was with child he called the man home and sent him to her bed, to cover it. That the man was too honest to go. And so the king had him put where the arrows were.

*(DAVID has taken a step back, as from a blow.)*

**DAVID**
That is false. Every word of it.

**BATHSHEBA**
I know it is false. I came in at your front gate at noon. But the gate at noon was one day. The story will be told every day. And there is a grave at Rabbah that makes it sound true.

*(He has no answer. She turns her face away, back to her mourning. He stays in the doorway. He does not come in.)*

*(When the mourning is over, DAVID sends, and brings her into his house. She bears a son.)*

**[MUSICAL NOTE: "MAYIM LEDAVID HAMELECH." Because of the weight of Uriah's death, no rhythmic or festive statement of this song belongs here. At most a distant echo, unresolved. Its full placement — and an adaptation fitted to this reading — is deferred to the water from Bethlehem (Scene 49), pending Roei's decision.]**

**A VOICE**
"But the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of the LORD."

## Scene 36 — "You Are the Man"

*The king's hall. DAVID in the seat from which he judges. On the table beside him: the business of the kingdom — and, among it, the SCRIBE's chest. NATHAN is announced, and enters. DAVID is glad to see him.*

**DAVID**
Nathan. Is there a case?

**NATHAN**
There is a case.

*(He tells it as a man brings a matter to his king.)*

"There were two men in one city. One rich; one poor. The rich man had flocks and herds — very many. And the poor man had nothing. Nothing but one little ewe lamb, which he had bought."

*(The word "poor." Something stirs in DAVID's face — an old soreness.)*

"He nourished it. It grew up with him and with his children, together. It would eat from his morsel, and drink from his cup, and lie in his bosom. It was like a daughter to him."

*(DAVID has leaned forward. He once carried lambs.)*

"And a traveler came to the rich man. And he could not bear to take one of his own flock, or of his own herd, to make ready for the wayfarer who had come to him. He took the poor man's lamb — and made it ready for the man who had come."

*(DAVID is on his feet.)*

**DAVID**
I have known a man like that. He had a kingdom. And he reached out and took the one thing a poor man had.

*(He is thinking of Saul, and of a window. His anger is entirely sincere.)*

"As the LORD lives — the man who has done this deserves to die! And the lamb he shall repay four times over: because he did this thing, and because he had no pity."

*(NATHAN has not taken his eyes from him. No light changes. No music.)*

**NATHAN**
"You are the man."

*(DAVID stops. All of him stops.)*

"Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: I anointed you king over Israel. I delivered you out of the hand of Saul. I gave you your master's house; I gave you the house of Israel and of Judah. And if that had been too little, I would have added to you as much again."

"Why have you despised the word of the LORD, to do what is evil in His eyes?"

"Uriah the Hittite you have struck down with the sword. His wife you have taken to be your wife. And him you have killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon."

*(DAVID's eyes go to the chest on the table: the writ; the names of the witnesses. It is all there. His hand moves toward it —)*

"Now, therefore, the sword shall never depart from your house."

*(— and stops.)*

"Thus says the LORD: I am raising up evil against you out of your own house. For you did it in secret. But I will do this thing before all Israel, and before the sun."

*(Silence. DAVID's hand is on the lid of the chest. He could open it. He could lay the tablet in front of the prophet and say: read. He stands so — the length of a long breath.)*

*(He takes his hand away.)*

**DAVID**
"I have sinned against the LORD."

*(Nothing else. No plea; no paper. He comes down from the judgment seat, by himself, and stands on the floor.)*

**NATHAN**
"The LORD, too, has put away your sin. You shall not die."

*(And then the rest — which he does not soften.)*

"But because by this deed you have given the enemies of the LORD cause to pour contempt — the son who is born to you shall surely die."

*(DAVID receives it standing. NATHAN looks at him — with grief; he loves this man — and goes to his house.)*

*(DAVID alone. The seat is behind him. He does not look at the chest again. He begins to speak — low; not yet a song.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 51 — as a developing monologue; melody possible later.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, spoken, after an unaccompanied silence.
**Addressed to:** God alone.
**Physical action:** he leaves the judgment seat; crosses to a basin of water; kneels.
**Why prayer, here:** there is no one else left to whom the thing can be said.
**What changes:** a king who judged becomes a man asking to be made clean. If a melody enters, it enters at the water — never under "You are the man."]**

**DAVID**
"Be gracious to me, God, according to Your kindness.
According to Your great compassion, blot out my rebellions."

*(He reaches the basin.)*

"Against You — You alone — I have sinned,
and done what is evil in Your eyes."

*(He kneels. His hands in the water.)*

"Create in me a clean heart, God,
and renew a steadfast spirit within me.
Do not cast me away from Your presence,
and do not take Your holy spirit from me."

*(The one thing he ever asked. He stays on his knees.)*

## Scene 37 — The Child, and the Rising

*An inner room. A cradle. BATHSHEBA beside it; women with cloths, with a basin — the same basin. The child is very ill. The waiting is heard: breathing; footsteps; water wrung out and changed.*

*In the outer room, DAVID lies on the ground. He has lain there for days. He has not eaten. The ELDERS of his house stand over him.*

**ELDER**
My lord the king. Rise a little.

*(Nothing.)*

A morsel. For the kingdom's sake, if not your own.

*(DAVID does not rise. He will not eat bread with them.)*

**DAVID**
*(into the floor)* "A broken and a crushed heart, God, You will not despise."

*(The ELDERS withdraw. Night. Day. The seventh day. In the inner room, a stillness; then BATHSHEBA makes a sound, once. The women stop moving.)*

*(At the door, the servants gather — whispering. None will go in to him.)*

**SERVANT**
*(a whisper)* While the child was alive we spoke to him and he would not listen. How can we tell him the child is dead? He may do himself some harm.

*(DAVID lifts his head. He sees them whispering. He understands.)*

**DAVID**
"Is the child dead?"

**SERVANT**
"He is dead."

*(DAVID stays where he is, on the ground, a moment longer. Then he gets up.)*

*(He goes to the basin. He washes. He anoints himself. He changes his clothes. He goes out — to the tent of the LORD — and bows down there to the ground. He comes back to his house. He asks; they set bread in front of him. He eats.)*

*(The servants stare.)*

**SERVANT**
What is this thing you have done? "While the child was alive, you fasted and wept. And when the child is dead — you rise, and eat bread?"

**DAVID**
"While the child was still alive, I fasted and I wept. For I said: Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me, and the child may live."

"But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again?"

*(Very simply.)*

"I shall go to him. But he will not come back to me."

*(He puts down the bread. He goes into the inner room.)*

*(BATHSHEBA is sitting by the empty cradle. She has not washed. She does not look at him. He sits down near her — the basin on the floor between them.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
You have washed.

**DAVID**
Yes.

**BATHSHEBA**
You have eaten.

**DAVID**
Yes.

**BATHSHEBA**
I heard them asking you how you could.

**DAVID**
I cannot bring him back. I lay on the floor seven days while there was something to ask for. There is nothing to ask for now. There is only you, and the people out there, and tomorrow.

**BATHSHEBA**
I am not ready to wash.

**DAVID**
Then do not. I will sit here.

*(He does. A long time. He does not speak of God to her; he does not explain anything. His hand lies open on the floor beside the basin. At last — without looking at him — she puts her hand in it.)*

*(Time. The cradle is carried out; later it is carried in again. BATHSHEBA with a newborn. DAVID takes the child into his arms.)*

**BATHSHEBA**
What will you call him?

**DAVID**
Solomon. Peace.

*(NATHAN at the door. It is the first time he has come since that day. DAVID turns to face him, the child in his arms — braced.)*

**NATHAN**
I am sent with a name.

*(He comes and looks at the child.)*

"Jedidiah." Beloved of the LORD. For the LORD's sake.

*(DAVID lets out his breath. BATHSHEBA has come to stand beside him. With his free hand DAVID reaches — not looking — to the table, and finds the rolled plan lying there, and rests his fingers on it.)*

*(The dead child is not forgotten in this room. The living one sleeps.)*

## Scene 38 — Tamar: The First Paralysis

*Years on. The king's sons are grown. AMNON's house: AMNON, the firstborn, lying on his bed, to all appearances ill. DAVID has come to see him — a father at a sickbed.*

**DAVID**
You have eaten nothing. They tell me three days.

**AMNON**
I cannot. Everything they bring turns in my mouth.

*(A beat.)*

"Let my sister Tamar come, and make a couple of cakes in my sight, and I will eat from her hand."

**DAVID**
*(relieved to be asked for something he can give)* Is that all?

*(To a servant.)*

To Tamar, at her house: "Go to your brother Amnon's house, and make food for him." Say that her father asks it.

*(He lays his hand on AMNON's forehead, and goes.)*

*TAMAR comes — the king's daughter, in the long robe with sleeves that the king's unmarried daughters wear. She takes dough; she kneads; she shapes the cakes in his sight; she bakes them. It takes the time it takes. She sets the pan before him.*

**AMNON**
"Send everyone out from me."

*(They go.)*

"Bring the food into the inner room. I will eat from your hand."

*(She takes the cakes and brings them in. He takes hold of her.)*

**AMNON**
"Come. Lie with me, my sister."

**TAMAR**
"No, my brother. Do not force me. Such a thing is not done in Israel. Do not do this vile thing."

*(She is thinking as fast and as clearly as she can; she is fighting for both of them.)*

"And I — where would I carry my shame? And you — you would be as one of the base men in Israel."

"Speak to the king. Please. He will not keep me from you."

*(He will not listen to her voice. Dark.)*

*Light. The same door — from outside. AMNON's voice, changed; the hatred is greater than the love was.*

**AMNON**
*(off)* "Get up. Go."

**TAMAR**
*(off)* "No! This wrong — sending me away — is greater than the other that you did to me."

**AMNON**
*(off; to his servant)* "Put this woman out, away from me. And bolt the door after her."

*(TAMAR is put out. The bolt goes home behind her.)*

*(She stands in the street. She stoops; takes ashes; puts them on her head. She takes hold of the long robe with sleeves, and tears it. She lays her hand on her head. And she walks — through the city, in every person's sight — crying aloud as she goes. She does not hide it. She does not go quietly.)*

*(ABSALOM meets her: her full brother; the most beautiful man in Israel. He sees the robe. He knows at once.)*

**ABSALOM**
"Has Amnon your brother been with you?"

*(She does not need to answer.)*

"Now, my sister — be silent. He is your brother. Do not take this thing to heart."

*(She looks at him. Whatever he intends by it — and he intends a great deal — she has been told to be silent. He brings her into his house. The door closes on the city. She remains standing inside it: we still see her.)*

*The king's house. A SERVANT has told DAVID. DAVID is on his feet — white with anger.*

**DAVID**
I sent her. With my own mouth. I said: your father asks it.

*(He turns on the SERVANT.)*

Call Amnon. Now.

*(The SERVANT turns to go.)*

Bring him to —

*(He has lifted his hand toward the judgment seat — the seat he came down from, on the day Nathan stood here. His eyes are on it. The sentence does not finish.)*

*(The hand stays in the air. The SERVANT waits, half-turned, for the rest of the order. It does not come. DAVID's mouth is open. Nothing.)*

*(A long time. The SERVANT does not dare to move. The hand, at last, comes down — not in a gesture; it simply falls. DAVID has not sat in the seat. He has not sent for his son. The SERVANT is still waiting when the light leaves the king's house.)*

*(It does not leave TAMAR. She is standing in ABSALOM's house, in the torn robe, the ashes on her hair, exactly as she was. Only when the king's side is fully dark does she — by her own choice — sit down.)*

*(ABSALOM sits beside his sister. Across the stage AMNON passes, well again. ABSALOM watches him go by. He does not speak to AMNON — neither bad nor good.)*

**A VOICE**
"And Tamar remained, desolate, in the house of Absalom her brother."

---

## Scene 39 — Amnon: The Second Paralysis

*Two full years. The king's house. ABSALOM before DAVID — easy, smiling, a prince asking a favor.*

**ABSALOM**
Your servant has shearers at Baal-hazor. It is a feast. "Let the king come, and his servants, with your servant."

**DAVID**
"No, my son. Let us not all go, and be a weight upon you."

**ABSALOM**
The king would honor it.

**DAVID**
The king would eat you out of your flocks.

*(He blesses him. ABSALOM does not go.)*

**ABSALOM**
"If not — then let my brother Amnon go with us."

*(A small silence. DAVID looks at his son's open, pleasant face.)*

**DAVID**
"Why should he go with you?"

**ABSALOM**
He is the eldest. If the king cannot come, let the king's firstborn sit at the head of my table. Let all Israel see the king's sons eat together.

*(It is exactly what a father wants to hear. DAVID wants to hear it. He searches the face a moment longer — and finds nothing.)*

**DAVID**
You have not spoken to him in two years.

**ABSALOM**
Then it is time.

*(DAVID gives way.)*

**DAVID**
Let him go. Let all the king's sons go.

*(ABSALOM bows, and goes. DAVID looks after him. His hand has half risen, as if to call him back. It falls.)*

*Baal-hazor — seen only as ABSALOM, aside, with his servants.*

**ABSALOM**
"Watch. When Amnon's heart is merry with wine, and I say to you: Strike Amnon — then kill him. Do not be afraid. Is it not I who command you? Be strong. Be men."

*The king's house. A RUNNER, wild.*

**RUNNER**
"Absalom has struck down all the king's sons! Not one of them is left!"

*(DAVID rises; tears his garments; falls to the ground. All his servants stand by with their clothes torn. JONADAB — the king's nephew, a very clever man — bends over him.)*

**JONADAB**
"Let my lord not say that they have killed all the young men, the king's sons. Amnon alone is dead."

*(Smoothly.)*

"It has been set on Absalom's mouth since the day he forced his sister Tamar."

*(DAVID lifts his head and looks at JONADAB: that this was known; that it was sayable; that two years went by.)*

*(The WATCHMAN cries out. The king's sons come in by the road — on their mules, weeping. DAVID goes to them. He counts them with his eyes, one by one, as he used to count; he holds each. Then the king and all his servants weep, very bitterly.)*

**DAVID**
And Absalom?

**RUNNER**
Fled. To Geshur — to Talmai, his mother's father.

*(DAVID stands. A son of his has murdered a son of his. He is the king; there is one thing for a king to do. He turns to the captain of the guard, and lifts his hand.)*

**DAVID**
Send —

*(The hand in the air. The captain waiting. It is the same hand, the same height, the same silence. He cannot say after whom, or to do what. He could not judge the first son. He cannot now judge the second for doing what he left undone.)*

*(The hand comes down.)*

*(In ABSALOM's house, TAMAR — who has heard the news through the wall — sits as she sat. She does not rejoice. Her brother is gone as well.)*

**A VOICE**
"And David mourned for his son all the days. And Absalom fled to Geshur, and was there three years. And the soul of King David longed to go out to Absalom."

---

## Scene 40 — The Return That Is Not a Meeting

*JOAB, watching the king across a room: DAVID is looking out of a window, north-east, toward Geshur. He has been looking that way for three years.*

*A WOMAN of Tekoa is brought before DAVID in the garments of a mourner. She falls on her face.*

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
"Help, O king!"

**DAVID**
What troubles you?

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
"Truly, I am a widow; my husband is dead. And your servant had two sons. The two of them fought in the field, and there was no one to part them. And the one struck the other, and killed him."

*(DAVID is entirely attentive.)*

"And now the whole clan has risen against your servant. They say: Give up the one who struck his brother, and we will put him to death for the life of the brother he killed — and destroy the heir as well. They would quench my one coal that is left. They would leave my husband neither name nor remnant on the face of the earth."

**DAVID**
Go to your house. I will give orders about you.

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
On me be the guilt, my lord the king, and on my father's house. The king and his throne are innocent.

**DAVID**
If anyone says a word to you, bring him to me. He will not touch you again.

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
Let the king swear it. By the LORD. That the avenger of blood shall not destroy further — that they shall not destroy my son.

**DAVID**
"As the LORD lives — not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground."

*(She has him. She lifts her face.)*

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
Let your servant speak one word more to my lord the king.

**DAVID**
Speak.

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
"Why, then, have you planned such a thing against the people of God? In saying this, the king convicts himself — for the king does not bring home his own banished one."

*(DAVID is very still.)*

"For we must surely die. We are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. Yet God does not take away a life; He devises means, so that the one who is banished may not stay banished from Him."

*(A silence.)*

**DAVID**
Do not hide from me the thing that I am going to ask you.

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
Let my lord the king speak.

**DAVID**
"Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?"

**WOMAN OF TEKOA**
*(almost with relief)* As you live, my lord the king — no one can turn right or left from anything the king says. Your servant Joab commanded me. He put every one of these words in my mouth.

*(JOAB comes forward. DAVID looks at him — not angry.)*

**DAVID**
You could have asked me.

**JOAB**
I have been asking you for three years. You look out of that window.

*(DAVID nearly smiles.)*

**DAVID**
"See, now: I have done this thing. Go. Bring back the young man — Absalom."

*(JOAB bows to the ground. He is going. DAVID's voice stops him.)*

"Let him go round to his own house. My face he shall not see."

**JOAB**
My lord —

**DAVID**
His own house.

*Jerusalem. ABSALOM is home — and is not. A door: the king's. He passes it. He passes it again; the light changes; he passes it again. Two full years. He sends to JOAB; JOAB does not come. He sends again. JOAB does not come.*

**ABSALOM**
*(to his servants)* Joab's field is next to mine. He has barley in it. Set it on fire.

*(A glow. JOAB arrives at a run, furious.)*

**JOAB**
Why have your servants set my field on fire?

**ABSALOM**
Because now you are here.

"Why did I come from Geshur? It would be better for me if I were still there. Now let me see the king's face. And if there is guilt in me — let him kill me."

*(JOAB looks at him: he has heard a sentence very like it before, from another young man, about another king. He goes in to DAVID.)*

*The door opens. ABSALOM enters. He comes before the king and bows with his face to the ground. DAVID raises him — and kisses him.*

*(They stand. The kiss has been given. Each is waiting for the other to say the next thing.)*

**ABSALOM**
Two years I have stood by that door.

**DAVID**
Now you have come in.

**ABSALOM**
Yes.

*(Silence.)*

Is there something the king wishes to ask me?

*(DAVID looks at him. There are five years of things.)*

Ask it. I would answer. I have had a long time to make the answer ready.

**DAVID**
*(with difficulty)* Is your sister well?

*(It is not what ABSALOM expected. For an instant his face is a boy's.)*

**ABSALOM**
She lives in my house. She does not go out. I have a daughter now. I named her Tamar.

**DAVID**
Tamar.

**ABSALOM**
It is the first time I have heard you say the name.

*(DAVID receives it. He opens his mouth to say more — what he should have done; what he could not; that he longed for this son every day of the three years. None of it comes. The mouth closes.)*

*(ABSALOM waits. He gives it a full breath longer than courtesy requires. Then he bows — correctly — and goes out by the door he waited at. DAVID is left with his hand a little way out from his side.)*

---

## Scene 41 — The Gate

*The gate of Jerusalem, early. The road by which people come up to the king for judgment. A chariot stands there, and horses, and fifty men to run before it: ABSALOM's. He himself is on foot, among the people, and has been since before light.*

*(A NORTHERNER, with the dust of days on him, a document in his hand.)*

**ABSALOM**
You have come a long way. Which town?

**NORTHERNER**
Your servant is of Dan. The tribe of Dan.

**ABSALOM**
Dan. That is five days. Sit. Give him water. What is the matter?

**NORTHERNER**
A boundary stone, my lord. My neighbor moved it in my father's time, and his son says it was always so. I have the old deed. I have been at this gate four mornings.

**ABSALOM**
Four.

*(He takes the deed; reads it — really reads it.)*

"See: your claim is good and right."

*(He gives it back.)*

"But there is no one from the king to hear you."

**NORTHERNER**
They say the king is —

**ABSALOM**
The king is my father, and a great man. He has a great deal to carry.

*(Quietly; the man leans in to hear it.)*

I know what it is to wait at a door.

*(A WIDOW approaches, sees who it is, and begins to go down on her knees. ABSALOM is there before she reaches the ground: he puts out his hand, raises her, and kisses her cheek, like a kinsman.)*

**ABSALOM**
No. Not to me. What is your name, mother?

*(She tells him. He repeats it. He will know it tomorrow.)*

**ABSALOM**
*(to those gathering — not a speech; a sigh, overheard)*
"Oh, that someone would make me judge in the land! Every man who had a suit or a cause would come to me — and I would see that he got justice."

*(More come. The same thing, with other faces: the hand out before the bow; the kiss; the name. Morning after morning.)*

**A VOICE**
"So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel."

*The king's house. ABSALOM before DAVID.*

**ABSALOM**
"Let me go, please, and pay my vow, which I vowed to the LORD, in Hebron. For your servant made a vow while I lived at Geshur: If the LORD brings me back to Jerusalem, I will serve the LORD."

*(DAVID is moved. It is the first thing this son has asked him for in years.)*

**DAVID**
"Go in peace."

*(ABSALOM bows, and goes. They are the last words DAVID will ever say to him.)*

*(A ram's horn, far to the south. Then voices, from every side at once:)*

**VOICES**
"Absalom is king in Hebron!"

*(A RUNNER to DAVID.)*

**RUNNER**
Ahithophel has gone to him. From your own council. "The hearts of the men of Israel are after Absalom."

*(DAVID stands up from his seat. JOAB, ABISHAI, the household.)*

**DAVID**
"Rise. Let us flee — or there will be no escape for us from Absalom. Hurry, or he will overtake us quickly, and bring evil down on us, and put the city to the sword."

**JOAB**
Flee? We have the walls. We have the guard. Let him come and break his teeth on Jerusalem.

**DAVID**
On whom?

*(JOAB does not follow.)*

Who will be on the wall, Joab? Judah. And who will be under it? Judah, and Benjamin, and Dan — a man I have never met, with a deed in his hand. I have spent my life keeping Israel from killing Israel. I will not make this city the place where it is done.

**ABISHAI**
He is a rebel.

**DAVID**
He is my son. And they are my people. Both.

*(To the household.)*

Take what can be carried.

*(They gather it. Ten women of his house are left behind, to keep it. The king goes out, and all his household after him. Around the going of the king, Jerusalem empties.)*

---

## Scene 42 — On the Way Out

*The last house of the city. DAVID stands and lets the people pass before him: his servants; the guard; and six hundred men of Gath who followed him years ago, with their families. Their captain: ITTAI the Gittite.*

**DAVID**
"Why should you come with us as well? Go back. Stay with the king" — whoever sits there. "You are a foreigner; an exile from your own place. You came only yesterday. Shall I make you wander with us today — when I am going I do not know where? Go back. Take your brothers with you. Kindness and truth go with you."

**ITTAI**
"As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives: in whatever place my lord the king shall be — for death or for life — there your servant will be."

*(DAVID looks at this Philistine; at the children behind him.)*

**DAVID**
Then cross.

*(ITTAI and his little ones pass. All the land is weeping aloud as the people go over. And now ZADOK the priest, and ABIATHAR, and the Levites with them — carrying the Ark of the Covenant of God. They set it down.)*

*(DAVID stops. He looks at it: the thing he danced before; the thing he was not allowed to house.)*

**DAVID**
"Carry the Ark of God back into the city."

**ZADOK**
My lord — it should go with the king.

**DAVID**
It is not mine, to carry about for my protection.

"If I find favor in the eyes of the LORD, He will bring me back, and let me see it, and the place where it dwells."

*(He steadies himself.)*

"And if He says: I have no delight in you — here I am. Let Him do to me as seems good in His eyes."

**ABIATHAR**
*(who came to him once with nothing but an ephod)* And you?

**DAVID**
"See — I will wait at the fords of the wilderness, until word comes from you." You have two sons who can run. Go back in peace.

*(The Ark is lifted, and carried back toward the city. DAVID watches it go. Then he turns his face to the hill.)*

*The ascent of the Mount of Olives. DAVID goes up — weeping as he goes; his head covered; barefoot. All the people with him cover their heads, and go up weeping. It is not a march.*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 3 — then "MIMA'AMAKIM" (Psalm 130). Version of the song still to be settled.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, on the climb, without stopping.
**Addressed to:** God.
**Physical action:** bare feet on stone; the column climbing; later, stones falling near him.
**Why song, here:** it changes his breathing, not his duty to keep the column moving.
**What changes:** the others' steps and voices come in under his; the music is built out of the walking.
**Note:** Psalm 3 carries this flight in its own heading. Psalm 130 is a theatrical use; it is not attributed to this moment.]**

**DAVID**
*(climbing)*
"LORD, how many are my foes.
Many are rising against me.
Many are saying of my soul:
There is no salvation for him in God."

*(The column climbs. His voice joins their steps.)*

"But You, LORD, are a shield about me —
my glory, and the lifter of my head."

*(At the summit, where one bows down to God, a man comes to meet him: HUSHAI the Archite, the king's friend — an old man; coat torn, earth on his head.)*

**HUSHAI**
I am coming with you.

**DAVID**
"If you cross over with me, you will be a burden to me."

*(HUSHAI is stricken. DAVID grips his arm.)*

Listen. Ahithophel is with him. You know what Ahithophel's counsel is worth. Go back to the city. Say to Absalom: I will be your servant, O king; as I was your father's servant, so now I am yours. And undo Ahithophel for me. Zadok and Abiathar are there; whatever you hear in the king's house, tell them. Their sons will run it to me.

**HUSHAI**
You are asking me to go and bow to him.

**DAVID**
I am asking you to buy me a night.

*(HUSHAI embraces him; cannot let go; lets go. He goes down toward the city.)*

*(A little past the summit, ZIBA — the servant of Mephibosheth — with a pair of saddled donkeys: two hundred loaves, raisins, summer fruit, a skin of wine.)*

**DAVID**
Where is your master's son?

**ZIBA**
"He is staying in Jerusalem. For he said: Today the house of Israel will give me back the kingdom of my father."

*(DAVID closes his eyes. The seat at his table.)*

**DAVID**
"Then all that was Mephibosheth's is yours."

*(ZIBA bows low. The column goes on.)*

*Bahurim. On the ridge above the road, keeping pace: a man of the house of Saul — SHIMEI son of Gera. He is cursing as he comes, and throwing stones.*

**SHIMEI**
"Get out! Get out! Man of blood! Worthless man!"

*(A stone strikes near DAVID.)*

"The LORD has brought back on you all the blood of the house of Saul, in whose place you have reigned! And the LORD has given the kingdom into the hand of Absalom your son! Look at you — in your own evil! For you are a man of blood!"

*(ABISHAI has stopped. His hand is on his sword.)*

**ABISHAI**
"Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me go over, and take off his head."

**DAVID**
"What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah?"

*(Not in anger; in a great weariness. He has been saying this to Abishai, in one form or another, since a cave.)*

"If he curses — and if the LORD has said to him: Curse David — who is to say: Why have you done so?"

*(To ABISHAI, and to all of them.)*

"Look. My own son — who came out of my own body — is seeking my life. How much more, now, this man of Benjamin? Let him alone. Let him curse."

"It may be that the LORD will look upon my affliction — and that the LORD will repay me good for his cursing this day."

*(ABISHAI takes his hand from the hilt. He falls in again. The stones go on falling; the dust. DAVID walks. Barefoot, without halting the column, he begins:)*

**DAVID**
*(sung, walking)*
"Out of the depths I call to You, LORD.
Lord, hear my voice."

*(A stone. ABISHAI starts toward the ridge; DAVID's arm bars his way without his breaking stride. Voices in the column take it up.)*

**DAVID AND THE PEOPLE**
"Out of the depths I call to You, LORD.
Lord, hear my voice.
Let Your ears be attentive
to the voice of my pleading."

*(They come, exhausted, to the fords. There, for an hour, he breathes.)*

---

## Scene 43 — Counsel, and a Night

*Jerusalem. The king's hall. ABSALOM sits in his father's seat. AHITHOPHEL at his right hand — spare, exact, unhurried. The elders of Israel. HUSHAI enters, and bows to the ground.*

**HUSHAI**
"Long live the king! Long live the king!"

**ABSALOM**
*(amused; and not a fool)* "Is this your loyalty to your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"

**HUSHAI**
"No. The one whom the LORD has chosen, and this people, and all the men of Israel — his I will be, and with him I will stay." And besides: whom should I serve? Should it not be his son? As I served in your father's presence, so I shall be in yours.

*(ABSALOM studies him. He lets him stay. He turns to AHITHOPHEL.)*

**ABSALOM**
Give your counsel. What shall we do?

**A VOICE**
"And Ahithophel said to Absalom: Go in to your father's concubines, whom he left to keep the house. And all Israel will hear that you have made yourself hateful to your father; and the hands of all who are with you will be strengthened. So they pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof. And it was done before the eyes of all Israel."

*(It is not shown. HUSHAI hears it; an old man keeping his face still. Somewhere, far off, a prophet's sentence: "before all Israel, and before the sun.")*

**AHITHOPHEL**
*(quick, cold, complete)*
"Let me choose twelve thousand men. I will rise and pursue David — tonight. I will come upon him while he is weary, and his hands are slack. I will throw him into panic. All the people with him will run. And I will strike down the king — alone."

*(He lets the last word stand.)*

"Then I will bring all the people back to you. The man you seek is worth the return of them all. One death; and all the people will have peace."

*(It is good counsel. Everyone in the room knows it. ABSALOM is opening his mouth to say so —)*

**ABSALOM**
"Call Hushai the Archite as well. Let us hear what is in his mouth, too."

*(HUSHAI comes forward. He does not hurry. He will not hurry once in the whole of what follows.)*

**HUSHAI**
"The counsel that Ahithophel has given is not good — this time."

*(A stir. AHITHOPHEL does not move.)*

"You know your father. You know his men. They are warriors. And they are bitter of soul — like a bear in the field that has been robbed of her cubs."

*(He lets ABSALOM see the bear.)*

"And your father is a man of war. He will not spend the night with the people. Even now he is hidden — in one of the pits; in one of the places. And when some of ours fall at the first onset, whoever hears it will say: There has been a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom. And then even the brave man — the man with the heart of a lion — will melt. For all Israel knows that your father is a mighty man."

*(On the far side of the stage, in darkness: DAVID's people, lying where they dropped by the fords.)*

"So this is my counsel. Let all Israel be gathered to you — from Dan to Beersheba — like the sand that is by the sea, for multitude."

*(Slowly; to the young man in his father's chair.)*

"And you. Your own presence. Going into the battle."

**ABSALOM**
*(seeing it)* I will ride at their head.

**HUSHAI**
"We shall come upon him in whatever place he is found. We shall settle on him as the dew falls on the ground. And of him and of all the men with him, not one will be left. And if he withdraws into a city — all Israel will bring ropes to that city, and we shall drag it down into the valley, until not a pebble of it is found there."

*(Silence. It is a picture. ABSALOM is inside it.)*

**ABSALOM AND THE ELDERS**
"The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel."

*(AHITHOPHEL looks at the young man. He has seen the end of this as clearly as he sees everything. He rises; bows to no one; gathers his cloak; and goes out.)*

**A VOICE**
"And when Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not followed, he saddled his donkey, and went home, to his own city. He set his house in order. And he hanged himself."

*(HUSHAI, leaving the hall — not fast; never fast — until he is out of sight of it. Then he runs. ZADOK and ABIATHAR; a serving-girl; two young men at a spring, who run; pursuers; a well in a courtyard at Bahurim, a cloth spread over its mouth and grain scattered on the cloth; the pursuers pass.)*

*The fords. Night. DAVID's people asleep. Two young men come in, soaked and filthy: AHIMAAZ son of Zadok, and another.*

**DAVID**
Who are you?

**YOUNG MAN**
Jonathan —

*(DAVID's head comes up.)*

— son of Abiathar, my lord.

*(A beat. DAVID nods. Of course.)*

**AHIMAAZ**
"Rise, and cross the water quickly. Do not spend this night at the fords of the wilderness." Ahithophel counselled them to fall on you tonight. Hushai has bought you until they gather. No longer.

*(DAVID makes no speech. He goes among the sleepers, waking them with a hand. He picks up a child who will not wake, carries it to the water, puts it into its father's arms. Family by family they go over. By the light of morning there is not one left who has not crossed the Jordan. DAVID goes into the water after the last of them.)*

---

## Scene 44 — "Deal Gently"

*Mahanaim, beyond the Jordan. The people come in, starving. Three men meet them with loaded animals: SHOBI of Ammon; MACHIR of Lo-debar — in whose house a lame boy once was hidden; and BARZILLAI the Gileadite, eighty years old.*

**BARZILLAI**
Beds. Basins. Clay pots. Wheat, barley, flour, roasted grain; beans, lentils; honey, curds, sheep, cheese.

*(To DAVID, gruff.)*

"The people are hungry, and weary, and thirsty in the wilderness."

*(For the first time since Jerusalem, heads are laid down on something. DAVID watches them eat.)*

*Morning. The gate of Mahanaim. DAVID numbers his people and sets captains over them, in three bodies: JOAB; ABISHAI; ITTAI the Gittite.*

**DAVID**
"I will surely go out with you myself."

**A SOLDIER**
"You shall not go out."

*(Others take it up; they are not being courteous.)*

"If we run, they will not care about us. If half of us die, they will not care about us. But you are worth ten thousand of us. It is better that you be ready to help us from the city."

*(DAVID looks at them. It is hard for him. He was always first down the hill.)*

**DAVID**
"What seems good in your eyes, I will do."

*(He stands at the side of the gate. The companies begin to go out, by hundreds and by thousands. JOAB comes to him for his orders.)*

**JOAB**
Well?

**DAVID**
End it. Today, if you can. Break them and scatter them, and let them go home to their tents.

**JOAB**
And him?

*(DAVID does not answer at once.)*

You are sending twenty thousand men into a forest to fight for you. Some of them will not come out. And you want me to tell them that the one man who caused it is to be kept safe.

**DAVID**
I am asking them to end a rebellion. I am not asking them to kill my son.

**JOAB**
It may come to the same thing.

**DAVID**
Then find the difference. You are my commander. Finding it is what I keep you for.

*(JOAB looks at him. He promises nothing. DAVID turns from him, and speaks — to the three commanders; but loudly, so that every man passing through the gate will hear it.)*

**DAVID**
"Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man — with Absalom."

*(The soldiers go through the gate with those words in their ears: "the young man." "Absalom." JOAB goes out without having answered.)*

*The forest of Ephraim. The battle comes apart among the trees into a hundred pursuits. The forest devours more that day than the sword.*

*(ABSALOM, riding a mule, alone, meets servants of DAVID. The mule goes in under the thick branches of a great oak. His head is caught fast in the oak. The mule goes on from under him. He hangs between heaven and earth.)*

*(A SOLDIER sees it. He stops. He runs — to JOAB.)*

**SOLDIER**
"I saw Absalom — hanging in an oak."

**JOAB**
"You saw him! Then why did you not strike him to the ground, there? I would have owed you ten pieces of silver and a belt."

**SOLDIER**
"If I were weighing a thousand pieces of silver in my hand, I would not put out my hand against the king's son. In our hearing the king commanded you, and Abishai, and Ittai: Watch over the young man, Absalom, for my sake."

*(JOAB says nothing.)*

And if I had done it behind his back — nothing is hidden from the king — you yourself would have stood aside and let me answer for it.

**JOAB**
And if he goes back in at that gate alive? What happens then?

**SOLDIER**
What the king ordered. The young man is kept.

**JOAB**
The king kisses him. Again. And in three years I am standing in another forest, counting another twenty thousand, for the same head of hair.

*(He looks at the SOLDIER a long moment. He has understood the order perfectly. He is deciding to break it.)*

I have buried enough of Israel for that boy.

"I will not wait here like this in front of you."

*(He takes three darts in his hand. He goes. What follows is sound and movement, not anatomy: three blows; then ten young men, his armor-bearers, closing in. Then JOAB lifts the ram's horn — as he did at Gibeon, long ago — and blows.)*

**JOAB**
Call the people back from the pursuit! Spare them!

*(The soldiers halt. They take ABSALOM down, and cast him into a great pit in the forest, and raise over him a very great heap of stones. All Israel flees, every man to his tent.)*

*(AHIMAAZ son of Zadok — young, the fastest runner in the host — comes to JOAB.)*

**AHIMAAZ**
"Let me run, and carry the news to the king: that the LORD has judged for him against his enemies."

**JOAB**
"You are not the man to carry news today. Another day you shall carry it. Today you shall carry none — for the king's son is dead."

*(To a CUSHITE.)*

Go. Tell the king what you have seen.

*(The CUSHITE bows, and runs.)*

**AHIMAAZ**
Whatever comes of it — let me run too.

**JOAB**
Why, my son? There is no reward in this news.

**AHIMAAZ**
Whatever comes of it — let me run.

**JOAB**
*(tired)* Run.

*(AHIMAAZ runs — by the way of the plain. He overtakes the CUSHITE.)*

## Scene 45 — Two Runners

*Mahanaim. DAVID is sitting between the two gates — the inner and the outer. Above him, on the roof of the gate, a WATCHMAN. A stair goes up to the chamber over the gate.*

**WATCHMAN**
A man! Running alone!

**DAVID**
"If he is alone, there is news in his mouth."

*(If it were a rout, there would be many. He holds on to that. The man comes on. The WATCHMAN shades his eyes.)*

**WATCHMAN**
Another man! Running alone!

**DAVID**
He too brings news.

**WATCHMAN**
"The running of the first looks to me like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok."

**DAVID**
"He is a good man. He comes with good news."

*(AHIMAAZ arrives — and cries out before he has his breath.)*

**AHIMAAZ**
"Peace!"

*(He falls on his face before the king.)*

"Blessed is the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who lifted their hand against my lord the king."

**DAVID**
"Is it well with the young man — with Absalom?"

*(AHIMAAZ looks up at the king's face. He ran all this way to say it. He cannot.)*

**AHIMAAZ**
"I saw a great tumult, when Joab sent the king's servant, and me, your servant. I did not know what it was."

**DAVID**
"Turn aside. Stand here."

*(AHIMAAZ stands aside. The CUSHITE comes in.)*

**CUSHITE**
"Let my lord the king receive good news. For the LORD has judged for you this day against all who rose up against you."

**DAVID**
"Is it well with the young man — with Absalom?"

**CUSHITE**
*(he believes he is saying a gracious thing)*
"May the enemies of my lord the king — and all who rise against you for evil — be as that young man is."

*(DAVID does not cry out. He looks about him, as a man looks for something to hold. He turns to the stair that goes up to the chamber over the gate. He begins to climb. Halfway up, his body stops.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "AVSHALOM" / "BNI AVSHALOM" — the setting associated with Moti Fleisher is the working reference; version to be chosen. No protected lyric is used. The verse itself, 2 Samuel 19:1, is the text.**

**Addressed to:** his son.

**DAVID**
"My son —"

*(Nothing more comes. He hauls himself up one stair.)*

"My son, Absalom. My son, my son, Absalom."

*(He is weeping as he goes.)*

"Would that I had died instead of you —
Absalom, my son, my son."

*(Below, the army is coming home. They have won. They hear the voice from over the gate. One by one they cover their faces. They come into the city by stealth, as men steal in who are ashamed, having fled from a battle. A soldier holds a bandage ready for his wounded comrade, and stands at the foot of the stair, not knowing whether he may come in.)*

## Scene 46 — The Gate, and the Return

*The chamber over the gate. DAVID, his face covered. From below: the shuffle of men going quietly to their tents. JOAB comes up the stair. He does not bow. There are no court manners in what follows.*

**JOAB**
"Today you have covered with shame the faces of all your servants — who today saved your life, and the lives of your sons and your daughters, and the lives of your wives."

*(DAVID does not uncover his face.)*

"By loving those who hate you — and hating those who love you. For you have made it plain today that captains and servants are nothing to you. For today I know: if Absalom were alive, and all of us were dead this day — then it would be right in your eyes."

**DAVID**
*(uncovering his face)* I gave an order. In the gate.

**JOAB**
You did.

**DAVID**
It was heard.

**JOAB**
It was heard.

*(They look at each other. JOAB does not explain. He does not deny. DAVID knows.)*

**DAVID**
My son is dead.

**JOAB**
I know it.

**DAVID**
And they are asking me to mourn as though he had not been my son.

**JOAB**
No one can take your mourning from you. Mourn him all your life; I would not respect you if you did not. But your men are standing in that gate. They walked out of it this morning to die for you, and they have come back into it tonight like thieves — and they do not know whether you wish to see their faces.

*(DAVID looks toward the sound of them.)*

"Now. Rise. Go out. And speak to the heart of your servants. For I swear by the LORD: if you do not go out, not one man will stay with you this night. And that will be worse for you than all the evil that has come upon you from your youth until now."

*(DAVID tries to answer. He cannot. His hand closes on the mourning-cloth at his breast. JOAB does not withdraw, and does not press. Below, the footsteps are getting fewer.)*

*(DAVID hears that. He stands. He does not agree with JOAB; he never will. He goes down the stair — the stair he could not climb — one step, and another, to the gate. He sits down in the king's place. JOAB stays above until he is seated.)*

**A MAN**
*(below; passing it on)* The king is sitting in the gate.

*(It runs through the camp. The people come before the king. DAVID looks at them. He makes no speech. He sees the wounded man hanging back at the foot of the stair with his comrade — and moves along the bench, and makes room. That is the beginning of his coming back to them.)*

*The road home. The Jordan. The tribes are quarrelling over who shall bring the king back; DAVID sends to Judah.*

**DAVID**
*(to a messenger)* And say to Amasa — my sister Abigail's son, who led Absalom's host: "Are you not my bone and my flesh? God do so to me, and more, if you are not captain of my army from now on — in place of Joab."

*(JOAB, at the edge of the scene, hears it. His face does not change.)*

*(The ford. A man throws himself down before the king as he is about to cross: SHIMEI — with a thousand men of Benjamin behind him.)*

**SHIMEI**
"Let my lord not hold me guilty. Do not remember the wrong your servant did, on the day my lord the king went out from Jerusalem. For your servant knows that I have sinned. See — I have come today, the first of all the house of Joseph, to go down to meet my lord the king."

**ABISHAI**
"Shall Shimei not be put to death for this? He cursed the LORD's anointed."

**DAVID**
"What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah — that you should be my adversary today?"

"Shall any man be put to death in Israel today? Do I not know that today I am king over Israel?"

*(To SHIMEI.)*

"You shall not die."

*(MEPHIBOSHETH comes down to meet the king. He has not cared for his feet, nor trimmed his beard, nor washed his clothes, from the day the king went away. DAVID sees him. It is hard for DAVID to speak.)*

**DAVID**
"Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?"

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
"My lord the king — my servant deceived me. For your servant said: I will saddle a donkey, and ride on it, and go with the king — for your servant is lame. And he has slandered your servant to my lord the king."

*(DAVID looks at ZIBA, standing by with his fifteen sons. He looks at the unwashed man in front of him. He cannot tell.)*

"But my lord the king is like an angel of God. Do what is good in your eyes. All my father's house were nothing but dead men before my lord the king — and you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What right have I left, to cry out any more to the king?"

*(It is the table. DAVID has not the strength, this day, to find out the truth of it.)*

**DAVID**
"Why go on speaking of your affairs? I have said it: you and Ziba shall divide the land."

*(A silence. MEPHIBOSHETH looks at him.)*

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
"Let him take all of it — now that my lord the king has come home in peace."

*(DAVID looks at Jonathan's son. MEPHIBOSHETH, to get down the bank to the road, needs a hand. He holds his out. DAVID gives him his own.)*

*(BARZILLAI the Gileadite has come down to see the king over the Jordan.)*

**DAVID**
Cross over with me. I will provide for you beside me in Jerusalem.

**BARZILLAI**
"How many are the days of the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem? I am eighty years old today. Can I tell good from bad? Can your servant taste what I eat or what I drink? Can I still hear the voice of singing men and singing women?"

*(DAVID — who is a singer — takes that in.)*

"Let your servant turn back, and die in my own city, by the grave of my father and my mother. But here is your servant Chimham. Let him cross over with my lord the king."

*(DAVID kisses BARZILLAI, and blesses him. The old man turns back to his own place. The king crosses over — and all the people cross with him.)*

## Scene 47 — Another Breach, Another City

*[Provisional scene — retained pending Roei's review.]*

*Gilgal. The king has come home across the Jordan — and the homecoming has turned, within the hour, into a quarrel. MEN OF ISRAEL on one side of him; MEN OF JUDAH on the other.*

**MAN OF ISRAEL**
"Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away — and brought the king and his household over the Jordan?"

**MAN OF JUDAH**
"Because the king is our near kinsman. Why be angry over this? Have we eaten at the king's cost? Has he given us any gift?"

**MAN OF ISRAEL**
"We have ten shares in the king. In David, too, we have more than you. Why did you treat us with contempt? Was it not our word, first, to bring back our king?"

*(DAVID, between them, lifts his hands. Neither side is looking at him. The words of the men of Judah are harsher than the words of the men of Israel.)*

*(A ram's horn. A man of Benjamin has leapt onto a rock: SHEBA son of Bichri.)*

**SHEBA**
"We have no share in David! We have no inheritance in the son of Jesse! Every man to his tents, Israel!"

*(And they go. Just like that: all the men of Israel peel away from behind DAVID and follow SHEBA. Only Judah clings to its king. DAVID looks at the road that was full a moment ago.)*

*Jerusalem. DAVID; AMASA, the new captain; ABISHAI. JOAB stands among the officers, without a command.*

**DAVID**
*(to AMASA)* "Call out the men of Judah for me. Three days. And be here yourself."

*(AMASA goes. Three days pass over the stage. He has not come.)*

**DAVID**
*(to ABISHAI)* "Now Sheba son of Bichri will do us more harm than Absalom did. Take your lord's servants and pursue him — before he finds himself walled cities, and tears out our eye."

*(ABISHAI goes out. JOAB's men go out after him — and JOAB with them, though no one has told him to.)*

*The great stone at Gibeon. AMASA — late; hurrying — comes to meet them. JOAB steps forward, in his soldier's tunic, a sword belted over it.*

**JOAB**
"Is it well with you, my brother?"

*(His right hand takes AMASA by the beard, to kiss him. AMASA is not watching the other hand. The blow is hidden between their bodies. They part. AMASA falls.)*

*(JOAB does not strike twice. He and ABISHAI go on after SHEBA. One of JOAB's young men stands over the body.)*

**YOUNG MAN**
"Whoever favors Joab, and whoever is for David — follow Joab!"

*(But AMASA lies in the middle of the highway, and every man who comes up stops. So the YOUNG MAN drags him off the road into the field, and throws a garment over him. Then they pass. No one blesses the deed.)*

*Abel of Beth-maacah, far to the north. Siege: a ramp against the wall; the ram swinging. A woman's voice, from above — before we see her.*

**WISE WOMAN**
"Listen! Listen! Say to Joab: Come near, so that I may speak to you."

*(The ram stops. JOAB comes near. She is on the wall.)*

**WISE WOMAN**
"Are you Joab?"

**JOAB**
"I am."

**WISE WOMAN**
"Listen to the words of your servant."

**JOAB**
"I am listening."

**WISE WOMAN**
"In the old days they used to say: Let them ask at Abel. And so a matter was ended. I am one of the peaceable and the faithful in Israel. You are seeking to destroy a city — and a mother in Israel. Why would you swallow up the inheritance of the LORD?"

**JOAB**
*(and for once it is torn out of him; he means it)*
"Far be it — far be it from me, that I should swallow up or destroy. That is not the matter. A man of the hill country of Ephraim — Sheba son of Bichri is his name — has lifted his hand against the king, against David. Give him up, him alone, and I will go from the city."

**WISE WOMAN**
"His head shall be thrown to you over the wall."

*(She turns from the parapet to her own people. What she says to them, in her wisdom, we do not hear; what they decide is done out of sight. Something is thrown over the wall. JOAB blows the ram's horn. The siege comes apart; every man to his tent. On the wall, the WOMAN stays until the last soldier is gone.)*

*Jerusalem. JOAB before DAVID.*

**JOAB**
The revolt is over. One head. A city is standing that would not be.

**DAVID**
And Amasa?

**JOAB**
Amasa was three days late.

**DAVID**
He was my sister's son. As you are.

*(JOAB says nothing. DAVID looks at him for a long time — the man who buried Asahel; who killed Abner in a gate, and Absalom in an oak, and Amasa on a road; who brought back Absalom; who has just spared a city because a woman asked him to. Who is, once more, captain of the host.)*

**DAVID**
*(low; to himself, as once before)* Too hard for me.

## Scene 48 — Rizpah

*[Provisional scene — retained pending Roei's review. Its date within the reign is not given in the source; it is placed here without a claim that it follows Sheba.]*

*A bare rock. A woman sits beside a spread of sackcloth: RIZPAH daughter of Aiah — once the concubine of Saul. She will not move from this place for the whole of the scene. Around her, light and weather and other people change.*

*Elsewhere: the people, thin.*

**A MAN OF THE PEOPLE**
The third year. No rain.

*(DAVID, seeking the face of the LORD. GAD, or the priest with the ephod, gives the answer that is given.)*

**A VOICE**
"It is on account of Saul, and of his house of blood — because he put the Gibeonites to death."

*(The GIBEONITES are called: a remnant, not of Israel, to whom Israel once swore.)*

**DAVID**
"What shall I do for you? With what shall I make atonement — so that you may bless the inheritance of the LORD?"

**GIBEONITE**
"It is not a matter of silver or gold between us and Saul, or his house. And it is not for us to put any man to death in Israel."

**DAVID**
What you say, I will do.

**GIBEONITE**
"The man who consumed us, and planned to destroy us — let seven men of his sons be given to us."

*(It hangs in the air. DAVID is thinking of a cave's mouth, and a weeping king: Swear to me that you will not cut off my seed. And of a field, and an arrow.)*

**DAVID**
"I will give them."

*(No music. He spares MEPHIBOSHETH son of Jonathan, because of the oath of the LORD that was between them. Seven are given. Among them: the two sons of RIZPAH.)*

*(It is not shown. The light comes back to the rock. The sackcloth; RIZPAH. It is the beginning of the barley harvest: heat. A bird's shadow crosses her; she rises, drives it off, sits. Night: something moves at the edge of the dark; she rises, drives it off, sits. People come — with water, with a cloak; they try, gently, to lead her away. She does not go. The season turns over her.)*

*The king's house.*

**MESSENGER**
Rizpah daughter of Aiah has not left them. From the first of the harvest. By day she keeps the birds of the sky from them, and by night the beasts of the field.

*(DAVID rises. He goes out himself.)*

*The rock. DAVID stands before RIZPAH. She does not rise for the king.*

**DAVID**
Rizpah, daughter of Aiah.

*(Nothing.)*

I have come for them. For all seven. And I have sent to Jabesh in Gilead, for the bones of Saul, and of Jonathan his son. They will lie together, in the land of Benjamin, in the tomb of Kish.

*(She looks at him, then — for the first time.)*

**RIZPAH**
They have names.

**DAVID**
Tell me.

**RIZPAH**
Armoni. And Mephibosheth.

*(The same name as the man at his table. DAVID bows his head.)*

**DAVID**
Armoni. And Mephibosheth.

*(He kneels at the edge of the sackcloth, and with his own hands begins to gather what is there. After a moment, she helps him. The cloth is folded at last. The families walk behind. And as they go — the sound of rain.)*

**A VOICE**
"And after that, God let Himself be entreated for the land."

## Scene 49 — Water from Bethlehem

*[A recollection — not the next event. It should be unmistakable that we have stepped back in time.]*

*The king's table. DAVID, old now. A servant is pouring water into a cup. DAVID hears the sound of it — and holds up his hand. The pouring stops. The light changes: the lamps of the palace give way to a cave-fire. Out of the shadows of the standing courtiers step THREE YOUNG WARRIORS, in the gear of long ago. DAVID straightens; years fall off him.*

*The stronghold of Adullam. Harvest heat. Below, in the valley of Rephaim, the Philistines; and a Philistine garrison is sitting in Bethlehem.*

**FIRST WARRIOR**
There is water in the skin.

**DAVID**
It is warm, and it tastes of goat.

*(He is looking south — toward a town he can nearly see.)*

There is a well by the gate at Bethlehem. Cut down through the rock, so the water comes up cold on the hottest day of the harvest. My mother sent me to it every evening with the jar. I used to put my whole face in.

*(Half to himself; a homesick man, not a commander.)*

"Oh, that someone would give me a drink of water from the well of Bethlehem — the one by the gate!"

*(He turns back to his work. He does not see the THREE look at one another; or see them go.)*

*(A passage of danger, swift: three men through a sleeping camp; a gate; a rope; a shout; running. They are back — filthy, bleeding, grinning. One of them holds out a vessel in a gashed hand.)*

**FIRST WARRIOR**
From Bethlehem.

**SECOND WARRIOR**
By the gate.

**THIRD WARRIOR**
It is still cold.

*(DAVID takes it. He is overcome. He lifts it to his mouth — and sees the hand that gave it: the blood running down the wrist. He stops.)*

**DAVID**
You went through their camp.

**FIRST WARRIOR**
There and back.

**DAVID**
For a drink of water.

**SECOND WARRIOR**
You asked for it.

**DAVID**
I did not ask. I wished. A man should be careful what he wishes aloud, when there are men who love him standing by.

*(He lowers the vessel.)*

"Far be it from me, LORD, that I should do this. Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?"

*(He will not drink it.)*

**THIRD WARRIOR**
*(stung)* We brought it for you.

**DAVID**
I know. That is why I cannot. It cost too much to go down one man's throat.

*(He looks at the three of them.)*

There is only One it is fit to be given to.

*(He pours it out, slowly, onto the ground — before the LORD. The THREE watch their water sink into the dust. Then they understand what has been done with their gift. One by one, they kneel.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "MAYIM LEDAVID HAMELECH" — proposed placement; adaptation to be made to fit this version; no existing lyric used here. Under it, verses from David's song, 2 Samuel 22 (two separate passages, not one continuous quotation).**
**Who begins:** DAVID, as the water is poured.
**Addressed to:** God; and to the men around him.
**Physical action:** the pouring lasts the whole of the passage. The THREE carry the vessel; DAVID pours.
**What changes:** a gift refused becomes an offering shared. The action stays concrete; it is never a comic number.]**

**DAVID**
"The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer.
My God, my rock — in Him I take refuge."

**DAVID AND THE THREE**
"For You are my lamp, LORD;
and the LORD lights up my darkness."

*(The cave-fire fades. The THREE step back into the shadows and are courtiers again. DAVID, old, at his table, the cup of water untouched in front of him.)*

**DAVID**
*(quietly)* Men have been going to the wall for me all my life.

*(He does not drink.)*

## Scene 50 — The Census

*The same table. The cup is cleared; in its place the business of a kingdom at its height: lists of towns; the heads of tribes; tallies. JOAB, grey; the SCRIBES.*

**DAVID**
"Go through all the tribes of Israel — from Dan to Beersheba — and number the people. I would know the number of the people."

*(The SCRIBES take up their pens. JOAB does not move.)*

**JOAB**
"May the LORD your God add to the people a hundred times as many as they are — and may the eyes of my lord the king see it."

*(Bluntly.)*

"But why does my lord the king want this thing?"

*(DAVID does not answer. JOAB — of all men — presses.)*

You have never counted them. You have led them out and brought them in for forty years, and you never once asked how many. You used to know them. Now you want them on a tablet.

*(He takes a blank tablet from a SCRIBE and lays it on the table in front of the king.)*

I have done a great many things for you that you did not order. I am asking you not to order this.

*(DAVID looks at the blank tablet. He gives no reason. He takes the king's seal, and sets it on the tablet.)*

**DAVID**
Number them.

*(JOAB holds his eyes a moment longer. The king's word stands. JOAB bows, and goes out, and the captains of the host with him.)*

*(Nine months and twenty days. The voices of the SCRIBES, overlapping, passing place-names from mouth to mouth: Aroer… Gilead… Dan… round to Sidon… the fortress of Tyre… the south of Judah… Beersheba. Across the stage, the ENSEMBLE — the people — walk past a scribe one at a time. Each says a name. Each is given a number. The names stop being heard; only the numbers.)*

*(JOAB returns, and lays the sum before the king. DAVID looks at it. He is alone with the tablets. His own heart strikes him.)*

**DAVID**
"I have sinned greatly in what I have done. And now, LORD — take away, I pray, the guilt of Your servant. For I have acted very foolishly."

*Morning. GAD the prophet, David's seer.*

**GAD**
"Thus says the LORD: Three things I hold over you. Choose one of them, and I will do it to you."

*(DAVID waits.)*

"Shall famine come to you in your land? Or will you flee three months before your foes, while they pursue you? Or shall there be three days' pestilence in your land? Now consider, and see what answer I shall return to Him who sent me."

**DAVID**
"I am in great distress."

*(He is. But he does not hesitate over this.)*

"Let us fall into the hand of the LORD — for His mercies are great. Into the hand of man let me not fall."

*(The pestilence passes through the people — from Dan to Beersheba. It is given without spectacle.)*

**A VOICE**
"And there died of the people, from Dan to Beersheba, seventy thousand men."

*(The ENSEMBLE come back to the table. They do not carry tablets. One lays down a sandal. One a shawl. One a child's wooden toy. A mat. A water jar. The table of numbers fills with the things of particular people. DAVID stands over it. He sees them: one, and one, and one.)*

*(He comes down from the place where he gave the order.)*

*(The ENSEMBLE draw near him and stand — as the lambs stood, at the very beginning, pressing toward the gap. This time he has nothing in his hands to give them. He stands in front of them. He lifts his face.)*

**DAVID**
"See — it is I who have sinned. It is I who have done wrong. But these — the sheep — what have they done?"

*(He opens his arms: in the gap.)*

"Let Your hand be against me — and against my father's house."

## Scene 51 — The Threshing Floor

*The table is carried away. What is left is a floor: flat rock, on a height above the city. Sledges; a yoke of oxen; a heap of wheat. A Jebusite farmer at his threshing, and his sons: ARAUNAH. This is where the plague stopped.*

*GAD comes to DAVID.*

**GAD**
"Go up. Raise an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite."

*(DAVID goes up, as the LORD commanded. ARAUNAH looks down, sees the king and his servants climbing toward him, and comes out, and bows with his face to the ground.)*

**ARAUNAH**
"Why has my lord the king come to his servant?"

**DAVID**
"To buy the threshing floor from you — to build an altar to the LORD — so that the plague may be held back from the people."

**ARAUNAH**
*(at once; generous; a stranger who has watched his neighbors die)*
"Let my lord the king take it, and offer up whatever is good in his eyes. See: the oxen, for the burnt offering. The threshing sledges and the yokes of the oxen, for the wood."

"All of it, O king, Araunah gives to the king."

*(He means every word.)*

"May the LORD your God accept you."

**DAVID**
"No."

*(Gently. He has learned this somewhere — beside a brook; on a road near Carmel; over a cup of water.)*

"I will buy it from you, at a price. I will not offer to the LORD my God burnt offerings that cost me nothing."

*(He weighs out the silver into ARAUNAH's hand. ARAUNAH opens his palm, to give it back. DAVID does not put out his hand again. Instead he stoops, and takes up a stone from the floor.)*

*(He builds the altar himself. The sledges are moved; the wheat is cleared; it is all done by hand, and nothing about it looks like a palace. And as he sets each stone, he says a name — not loudly; to the stone, and to God.)*

**DAVID**
Samuel.

*(A stone.)*

Saul.

Jonathan.

*(His hand rests on that one.)*

Ahimelech, and the house of his father.

Asahel. Abner.

Uriah.

*(A stone with no name; he holds it a moment before he sets it.)*

The child.

Amnon.

Absalom. My son.

Amasa.

Armoni. Mephibosheth.

*(And the last, the largest: he needs both hands.)*

Seventy thousand, whose names I did not learn.

*(He offers burnt offerings and peace offerings. And the LORD lets Himself be entreated for the land; and the plague is held back from Israel.)*

*(Quiet. DAVID stands on the threshing floor, and looks at where he is.)*

**DAVID**
"This is the house of the LORD God. And this is the altar of burnt offering for Israel."

*(A boy has come up the hill behind him: SOLOMON. He sees no temple. He sees a floor, an altar of rough stones, and his father.)*

**SOLOMON**
Here?

**DAVID**
Here.

**SOLOMON**
There is nothing here.

**DAVID**
No.

**SOLOMON**
It is a floor. For threshing.

**DAVID**
It is where it stopped.

*(He makes room for the boy beside him. He does not sit; there is no throne here; he puts no crown on anyone. From inside his robe he takes the rolled plan — tied, as he tied it. He holds it a moment. He gives it to his son.)*

I drew it once. A long time ago. There is a mark by the door; you will see it. A bench. You need not keep the bench.

**SOLOMON**
*(unrolling a little)* You will not build it.

**DAVID**
No.

**SOLOMON**
Will you see it?

**DAVID**
No.

*(The boy looks up at him.)*

**SOLOMON**
Does that grieve you?

**DAVID**
Yes.

*(He looks at the altar; at the names in it; at the bare rock all round.)*

And I have never stood so near to it.

*(He speaks it as he spoke it in his father's courtyard, with the oil running into his eyes — but he is not looking now at a prophet, or at a horn. He is looking at a threshing floor and a child.)*

"One thing I have asked of the LORD,
that one thing I will seek:
to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life,
to gaze on the sweetness of the LORD
and to come early to His sanctuary."

*(The boy who asked for that wanted to live in the house of the LORD. The king does not see the house standing. He is standing in the place where it will begin.)*

## Scene 52 — Hallelujah

*DAVID stays where he is, on the threshing floor. There is still work to do: tools lying about the altar; the sledges; the yoke. He bends to it.*

*And music comes back — at first almost without sound. A single string. DAVID, working, listens. He straightens.*

**DAVID**
"Let everything that has breath praise the LORD."

**[MUSICAL FINALE: PSALM 150 — sung to the melody of Leonard Cohen's "Hallelujah," the chosen musical reference. None of Cohen's lyric is used. The distribution of the text, the meter, and the fitting of the psalm to the melody remain to be made, by singing it.**
**Who begins:** DAVID — one voice.
**Who joins:** the whole company, a few at a time. They return as the ENSEMBLE of the theatre — outside the time of the story. No one who has died comes back to life.
**Physical action:** while they sing, they clear the tools from around the altar, passing them from hand to hand, until there is an open place at the heart of the threshing floor. DAVID works with them. He and SOLOMON look at one another. He sings among them, and he listens.
**What changes:** the place where one man stood alone fills with voices. The climax is people meeting — not a coronation, and not a temple.]**

**DAVID**
"Hallelujah.
Praise God in His holy place;
praise Him in the vault of His power."

**DAVID AND OTHERS**
"Praise Him for His mighty acts;
praise Him for His abundant greatness."

**MORE VOICES**
"Praise Him with the blast of the ram's horn;
praise Him with harp and lyre."

*(A lyre, somewhere. A timbrel — women's hands, as on the road from Elah.)*

"Praise Him with timbrel and dance;
praise Him with strings and pipe."

**ALL**
"Praise Him with sounding cymbals;
praise Him with crashing cymbals."

*(The floor is clear. The altar stands in it, made of names. DAVID is one voice among the many; he has stopped leading; he is listening to them carry it.)*

"Let everything that has breath praise the LORD.
Hallelujah."

*(No words follow the song. Light holds on the open floor, the rough altar, the old man and the boy, and the people singing. Then it goes.)*

**THE END**
