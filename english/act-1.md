# ACT I

## From the Flock to the King's Table

---

## Scene 1 — The Flock and the Choosing

*Two places share the stage. A hillside pasture outside Bethlehem, in morning light. Jesse's courtyard, in shadow, waiting. When the light moves from one to the other, the work in the first does not stop.*

*PASTURE. DAVID, a boy, a small lyre slung on his back, stands in the gap of a stone fold and blocks it with his body. Behind him the flock presses.*

**DAVID**
No. Not you. You know it isn't you.

*(A big ewe shoves. He shoves back with his hip.)*

Levana. Levana. I have seen you eat a thornbush. You will live till noon.

*(He lets the lambs out one at a time, counting by touch.)*

One. Two. You — yes, you, go on. Three. Four. Where is —

*(He looks back into the fold.)*

Come. Nobody is going to step on you. I'm standing here.

*(The last lamb comes. He walks it to the low grass.)*

This is the tender grass. Down here, before the sun gets at it. You can't chew the other kind yet; you have hardly any teeth. So you go first. That's all it is. There's no honor in it.

*(The lambs eat. He crouches, pleased, watching them.)*

Slowly. There is enough.

"The eyes of all look to You in hope,
and You give them their food in its season."

*(One lamb stands apart, not eating.)*

What? What is it?

*(He goes to it, kneels, feels along the leg, looks in the mouth. Finds a burr in the lip. Draws it out.)*

There. All that sorrow for one thorn.

*(He sets the lamb down among the others. It eats.)*

"The LORD is my shepherd; I lack nothing."

*(To the lamb.)*

That one is mine. You can have it.

*Light: BETHLEHEM. SAMUEL enters, old, travel-worn, leading a heifer on a rope. The ELDERS of the town come out to meet him and stop short.*

**ELDER**
Seer. That is a long road from Ramah.

**SAMUEL**
It is.

**ELDER**
We were not told to expect you.

**SAMUEL**
No.

*(The ELDER looks to the others. No one helps him.)*

**ELDER**
"Do you come in peace?"

**SAMUEL**
"In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Make yourselves holy, and come with me to the sacrifice."

*(The ELDERS breathe.)*

**ELDER**
Forgive us. When the prophet comes unannounced, a town begins to count its sins.

**SAMUEL**
Let it count. It does a town no harm. Where is Jesse?

*(JESSE comes forward with seven sons behind him: ELIAB, ABINADAB, SHAMMAH and four more. At the edge of the courtyard, the women of the house — among them DAVID'S MOTHER and ZERUIAH.)*

**JESSE**
Here, my lord.

**SAMUEL**
You and your sons will stand with me at the sacrifice. All of them.

**JESSE**
They are here.

*(He puts a hand on the eldest.)*

Eliab. My firstborn.

**ELIAB**
My lord. My father's house is honored. Tell us what is needed — wood, water, the knife — and my brothers and I will see to it. You have walked far enough today.

*(SAMUEL looks at him: the height, the shoulders, the ease.)*

**SAMUEL**
You have carried arms.

**ELIAB**
When the king calls Judah, I go. My brothers behind me.

**SAMUEL**
*(low, to himself)* "Surely the LORD's anointed stands before Him."

*(He reaches toward the horn at his belt — and stops. He listens. No voice is heard. The family waits. It is a long time.)*

**JESSE**
My lord?

**SAMUEL**
*(not to them; giving back what he has been given)*
"Do not look at his appearance or at the height of his stature, for I have rejected him. It is not as a man sees. A man sees what is before his eyes. The LORD sees into the heart."

*(ELIAB has caught one word of it.)*

**ELIAB**
Have I given offense?

**SAMUEL**
No. You have done nothing. Stand with your brothers.

*(ELIAB steps back. He does not know what he has lost, only that he has lost it.)*

*Light: PASTURE. The lambs are down in the grass. DAVID has an old ewe by his side and is walking at her pace, which is slow.*

**DAVID**
Now the old ones. — Levana, I can see you. I can see you thinking.

*(To the old ewe.)*

No hurry. This patch has had a little sun; it's soft still, but it will hold you. Your teeth aren't what they were either. You and the little ones — the same trouble from two ends.

*(She stops. He stops.)*

You have carried a lamb every spring since before I could walk. Nobody is driving you today.

*(She lowers her head and eats. He stays beside her until he is sure.)*

"The LORD is good to all,
and His mercies are over all that He has made."

*Light: BETHLEHEM. JESSE brings ABINADAB before SAMUEL. SAMUEL does not study him. He waits, listening, and is answered.*

**SAMUEL**
"Nor has the LORD chosen this one."

*(SHAMMAH.)*

"Nor has the LORD chosen this one."

**JESSE**
My lord — chosen for what?

**SAMUEL**
For what I was sent to do.

*(The fourth son. The fifth. SAMUEL shakes his head each time.)*

**JESSE**
If my lord would tell me what he is looking for, I could spare him the trouble. I know my sons.

**SAMUEL**
I am not the one looking. That was my mistake with your firstborn.

*(The sixth. The seventh. SAMUEL looks at the last of them, and then at JESSE.)*

"The LORD has not chosen these."

*(Silence. The meat is waiting. Nobody moves toward it.)*

"Are these all the boys?"

*(JESSE does not answer at once. ELIAB looks at his father. At the edge of the courtyard, DAVID'S MOTHER has lifted her head.)*

**JESSE**
"There is still the youngest. He is out tending the sheep."

**SAMUEL**
You have another son.

**JESSE**
He is with the flock.

**SAMUEL**
I said all your sons.

**JESSE**
Someone has to be with the flock.

**SAMUEL**
"Send and bring him. We will not sit down until he comes here."

**JESSE**
Eliab.

**ELIAB**
It is the far pasture. An hour there and back. The meat —

**SAMUEL**
Then we will stand an hour.

*(ELIAB goes. The family remains standing, exactly as they are.)*

*Light: PASTURE. The big ewe is pushing at the gap again. The lambs are still eating.*

**DAVID**
Wait. Let them finish.

*(She pushes. He plants his feet.)*

You are strong. You can shove through and take, and you would leave them the stalks. They can't shove back. So I am the one in the gate. You will have the hard grass on the ridge, the kind nobody else can chew, and you will like it better.

*(ELIAB arrives and hears the end of this. DAVID, his back to him, is still talking to the ewe.)*

Don't sulk. There is some for you too.

**ELIAB**
David.

**DAVID**
*(at once, before he turns)* Here I am.

*(He turns.)*

Eliab? What has happened? Is Father —

**ELIAB**
Father is well. He is calling you.

**DAVID**
Now? I can't leave them in the middle. The strong ones haven't —

**ELIAB**
Samuel is waiting.

**DAVID**
Samuel.

*(He takes this in.)*

The prophet is in Bethlehem for a sacrifice. You don't need me for a sacrifice.

**ELIAB**
No one said we needed you. He said he would not sit down until you came.

**DAVID**
Why?

**ELIAB**
He did not say. He looked at me. He looked at each of us, one after another. Then he asked whether there was anyone else.

*(A beat.)*

Father had to think.

*(DAVID receives this. He does not answer it. He turns back to the fold.)*

**DAVID**
There. Now. Your turn.

*(He steps out of the gap. The strong sheep pour through.)*

Up to the ridge. Go on, Levana — lead them. You have wanted to lead all morning.

*(He watches until the whole flock is feeding.)*

Is there a boy with you?

**ELIAB**
The servant's son followed me up.

**DAVID**
*(calling off)* Stand in the gap! Don't let the big ones come down until the little ones are lying in the shade!

*(He picks up his staff. ELIAB looks him over.)*

**ELIAB**
You smell of them.

**DAVID**
I know.

**ELIAB**
He is the prophet of the LORD.

**DAVID**
Then he has smelled sheep before.

*(They go. The two lights become one.)*

## Scene 2 — The Son of Jesse

*Jesse's courtyard. The family is standing as it was left. Places have been laid for the meal: one for the prophet, one for Jesse, seven for the sons.*

*DAVID enters behind ELIAB and stops at the edge, where the women are. He bows.*

**DAVID**
My lord. Father.

*(SAMUEL looks at him for a long time. Then he listens — and his hand goes to the horn of oil.)*

**JESSE**
My lord. What is the oil for?

**SAMUEL**
You know what oil is for, Jesse.

**JESSE**
For a priest. For a king. We are neither, in this house.

**SAMUEL**
The LORD has seen, among your sons, the one He wants.

*(He draws the horn from his belt.)*

**JESSE**
Then before you pour it, there is a thing you must know. I will not have the prophet of the LORD deceived under my roof.

**SAMUEL**
Speak.

**JESSE**
We come from Ruth the Moabite. My father's father was her son. All my life there have been men at the gate who say, quietly, that the Torah shuts a Moabite out of the assembly of the LORD, and that the house of Boaz was built on a mistake. I am not a learned man. There came a year when I feared they were right — that I had no right to a daughter of Israel for my wife. I separated from her.

**SAMUEL**
"A Moabite man — not a Moabite woman." The law speaks of the men. Ruth was permitted. So are you. So are your sons.

**JESSE**
I know it now. I did not know it then. And it is not what I have to tell you.

*(He cannot look at DAVID.)*

This boy was born after I had separated from her.

*(Silence. The brothers do not look at DAVID. They have never needed it explained. Only DAVID is hearing it for the first time.)*

**DAVID**
*(quietly)* Is that why.

*(DAVID'S MOTHER steps out from among the women.)*

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
Jesse.

**JESSE**
This is not for you to —

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
The night you sent for the servant woman, it was I who was with you.

**JESSE**
You.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
She came to me and told me what you had asked of her. She did not want it. I did not want it. We changed places. It was dark, and you did not speak. He is your son.

*(JESSE looks at her. At the boy. At her.)*

**JESSE**
All these years. You let me think — you let them think —

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
You had already judged yourself. Was I to stand in the gate and tell Bethlehem what Jesse had intended, and how his wife had deceived him? I would have saved my name and broken yours. So I kept it. And I kept him.

*(To SAMUEL.)*

My lord, I would have kept it until I died. But you are holding the oil.

**DAVID**
Mother. You knew.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
I knew whose son you are. Every day.

**DAVID**
You put bread in my hand every morning and sent me up the hill and you said nothing.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
I said your name. I said it as often as I could.

*(JESSE crosses to DAVID. His hand rises toward the boy's shoulder and stops in the air.)*

**JESSE**
David. My son.

*(At last the hand comes down. DAVID stands under it. He does not know yet what to do with it.)*

**DAVID**
Father.

*(SAMUEL has waited. Now ELIAB speaks — carefully.)*

**ELIAB**
My lord. You have seen all of us. I am the eldest. My brothers have carried spears for Israel. He has carried lambs. I ask it with respect: what does the LORD want with him?

**SAMUEL**
I asked the same question when I saw you. I was answered. I have stopped looking.

*(To DAVID.)*

Come here.

*(DAVID does not move.)*

**DAVID**
My lord — what is the oil for?

**SAMUEL**
The LORD has chosen you to be king over His people Israel.

**DAVID**
King?

*(He looks around the courtyard as if the man might be standing in it.)*

Israel has a king.

**SAMUEL**
It has. I poured this same oil on his head.

**DAVID**
Then what am I to do? Stand up against him?

**SAMUEL**
I was not told that. I was told: fill your horn and go to Jesse of Bethlehem. I am an old man, and I have learned to say no more than I am given. Saul is king of Israel today.

*(A beat. This costs him.)*

What I can tell you, I tell you not as a prophet but as a man who has not finished mourning him: do not raise your hand against Saul.

**DAVID**
I would never. He is the LORD's anointed.

**SAMUEL**
In a moment, so are you. Remember that you said it.

*(To all of them.)*

And remember this. If Saul hears what was done in this courtyard, he will kill me, and he will not stop with me. I came with a heifer on a rope so that I could say I came to sacrifice, and say it truly. No one here will speak of this. Not in the gate. Not in the camp.

*(He looks at ELIAB until ELIAB lowers his eyes.)*

**DAVID**
I did not ask to be king.

**SAMUEL**
What did you ask?

**DAVID**
"One thing I have asked of the LORD,
that one thing I will seek:
to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life,
to gaze on the sweetness of the LORD
and to come early to His sanctuary."

*(Plainly.)*

That is all of it. I wanted to be where He is. To be let in. I would play there, I would pray there, I would sweep the floor if they'd have me. And not be sent out again.

**SAMUEL**
I was given to that house as a child. I slept beside the Ark. I know what you are asking for.

*(Gently; and then not gently.)*

And Israel? Who will care for Israel?

**DAVID**
Someone who knows how.

**SAMUEL**
You can seek His face from wherever He sets you down. I have had to.

*(DAVID turns — not to his father, not to his mother. To his sister.)*

**DAVID**
Zeruiah. You always say the true thing. What do I know about being a king?

**ZERUIAH**
Nothing.

*(A beat.)*

Neither did Saul. Ask the one holding the oil. He has done this before.

**DAVID**
*(to SAMUEL)* Who will teach me?

**SAMUEL**
You do not need to know everything today. Today you need to come here.

*(DAVID looks once toward the hill, where the flock is. Then he comes, and kneels. SAMUEL listens. When he speaks, he is repeating an order.)*

"Arise, anoint him; for this is he."

*(He anoints DAVID in the midst of his brothers. The oil runs. Nobody speaks. DAVID is very still; then he lifts his eyes.)*

**DAVID**
"Your face, LORD, I seek."

*(SAMUEL closes the horn.)*

**SAMUEL**
Now we may sit.

*(The family goes to the places. Seven sons, seven places. DAVID is left standing. DAVID'S MOTHER brings an eighth bowl and holds it, not knowing where to set it.)*

**JESSE**
Beside me.

*(She sets it there. DAVID sits beside his father. ELIAB passes him the bread without looking at him. They eat.)*

*(While the others eat, DAVID rises quietly, takes his lyre, and goes to the edge of the courtyard that opens on the pasture. He raises the lyre. He does not yet play.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: THE "I WANT" SONG — original song to be written by Roei Barak.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, alone, at the edge between the courtyard and the pasture; the family still at the meal behind him.
**Addressed to:** God — in the same voice he used with the lambs.
**Physical action:** the oil is still on his head; he is walking back up to the flock; he arrives among the sheep before the song ends.
**Why song, here:** he has been told what he is to become, and forbidden to tell anyone. The lyre is the only place it can go.
**What it must accomplish:** state the one desire (Psalm 27:4 — to dwell in God's house and be near Him); let the audience fall in love with his joy in the sheep and the music; carry him, verse by verse, from the private wish toward the first acceptance that he will have to carry others. He does not resolve it. He does not ask for the throne.
**Exit:** on the hill, among the flock. The last phrase is left open; SAUL's sleeplessness answers it.**]**

## Scene 3 — The Musician

*Gibeah. The king's hall, deep in the night. SAUL — tall, a soldier's body that cannot find a way to sit. A spear leans beside his seat. An OLD SERVANT and a YOUNG MAN keep their distance.*

**SAUL**
Is it morning?

**OLD SERVANT**
Not yet, my lord.

**SAUL**
It was "not yet" an hour ago.

*(He stands. Sits. Stands.)*

Has anyone come from Ramah?

**OLD SERVANT**
No one, my lord.

**SAUL**
He never used to send word. You would look up and he would be standing in the door, furious about something.

*(A beat.)*

No one.

**OLD SERVANT**
My lord — may your servants speak?

**SAUL**
My servants have been speaking for a month. I hear it stop when I come in.

**OLD SERVANT**
We are afraid for you. A dark spirit comes on the king, and we stand at the wall and can do nothing. Let our lord command us. Let us look for a man who knows how to play the lyre. When the spirit comes, he will play, and it will go easier with you.

**SAUL**
A harper.

*(Dry.)*

I have led Israel against Moab, and Ammon, and Edom, and the Philistines, and my servants have put their heads together and prescribed me a harper.

*(No one answers. He is too tired to keep it up.)*

"Find me a man who plays well, and bring him to me."

**YOUNG MAN**
"I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play. A brave man, a fighter, careful in his speech, good to look at. And the LORD is with him."

*(SAUL has heard only the last of it.)*

**SAUL**
Is He.

*(A beat.)*

Send for him.

*Light: BETHLEHEM. Jesse's courtyard. A MESSENGER of the king. JESSE, ELIAB, DAVID'S MOTHER. DAVID has just come down from the hill.*

**MESSENGER**
Thus says the king: "Send me your son David, who is with the flock."

*(Nobody breathes. JESSE does not look at ELIAB. ELIAB does not look at anyone.)*

**JESSE**
The king knows my son's name.

**MESSENGER**
The king was given it.

**JESSE**
What does the king want with him?

**MESSENGER**
To play. The king does not sleep.

*(The courtyard lets go.)*

**DAVID**
Me?

**MESSENGER**
If you are the one with the lyre.

**JESSE**
He is the one. Bring the donkey.

*(JESSE loads it himself: bread, a skin of wine, a young goat.)*

You carry these to the king from my hand. You play what he asks. You answer what you are asked, and no more.

**ELIAB**
*(low)* Not a word.

**JESSE**
He knows.

*(To DAVID.)*

David —

**DAVID**
I know, Father.

**JESSE**
I was going to say: eat something on the road.

*(It is the first time he has said such a thing. They both hear it. DAVID nods, and goes.)*

*Light: GIBEAH. DAVID enters the hall with the lyre in one hand and the kid's lead-rope in the other. SAUL, at the far end, lifts a hand.*

**SAUL**
Stand there.

*(DAVID stands there.)*

**DAVID**
My lord the king.

**SAUL**
You are Jesse's son.

**DAVID**
Yes, my lord.

**SAUL**
They told me a fighter. A man of war. You are a boy.

**DAVID**
I have only ever fought for sheep, my lord.

**SAUL**
And what is that?

**DAVID**
Bread. Wine. A young goat. From my father, for the king.

**SAUL**
Jesse of Bethlehem sends the king a goat.

*(Not unkindly.)*

Put it down. What do you play?

**DAVID**
What I play for the flock, my lord. I have never played for anyone who could tell me to stop.

*(Something in SAUL's face nearly moves.)*

**SAUL**
I will tell you. Play.

**[MUSICAL CUE: "LEMA'AN ACHAI VERE'AI" — Psalm 122:8–9. Melody and arrangement to be settled.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, lyre alone, from the place where SAUL has stood him.
**Addressed to:** SAUL. This is not a performance; he is trying to change another man's breathing.
**Physical action:** SAUL is on his feet, braced. DAVID plays to the body, not the face.
**Why song, here:** it is what he was sent for — and speech between a shepherd and a king has run out.]**

**DAVID**
*(sung)*
"For the sake of my brothers and my friends,
let me speak peace upon you.
For the sake of the house of the LORD our God,
I will seek your good."

*(SAUL stands rigid. Then the hand on the back of the seat loosens. DAVID lets the phrase come to its end and holds his hand over the strings.)*

**SAUL**
Go on.

*(DAVID plays the phrase again. SAUL sits. Under the music:)*

"For the sake of my brothers." You have brothers.

**DAVID**
Seven, my lord.

**SAUL**
And they are your friends?

**DAVID**
*(a beat)* They are my brothers.

*(SAUL laughs — once, surprised out of him. The servants look at each other: they have not heard it in a month.)*

**SAUL**
I have a tribe like that.

*(He listens.)*

"Peace upon you." Upon whom?

**DAVID**
Whoever is listening, my lord. This morning it was an old ewe.

**SAUL**
And tonight?

**DAVID**
Tonight, upon the king.

**SAUL**
Come closer. I cannot hear the low string from there.

*(DAVID comes half the distance. He plays. The light turns — night to day, day to night. DAVID goes out and comes in again, each time with the same phrase a little further along, each time standing a little nearer.)*

**[The cue continues as passage of time: the same musical sentence, shortened and returning, over several visits. Between visits DAVID is glimpsed on the hill with the flock. No number of months or years is stated.]**

*A DAY. SAUL is easy. He is eating. DAVID sits on the step below him, retuning.*

**SAUL**
Who taught you?

**DAVID**
No one, my lord. There is a great deal of time, with sheep.

**SAUL**
And do they listen?

**DAVID**
Sometimes. The old ones. The lambs don't care for it.

*(They share it — a small smile, the same on both faces.)*

**SAUL**
I never kept sheep. Donkeys. My father's donkeys strayed, and he sent me after them, and I went three days through the hill country and never found a hoof. I found Samuel instead.

*(DAVID's hands have stopped on the strings.)*

He had already found the donkeys. He knew everything, that man. He sat me at the head of thirty guests and had the cook set the thigh in front of me — the portion they had been keeping. And at daybreak he walked me to the edge of the town, where no one could see, and poured oil on my head.

*(DAVID does not move.)*

I was looking for donkeys.

**DAVID**
*(carefully)* And then, my lord?

**SAUL**
Then they gathered all Israel to draw the lot for a king, and the lot fell on Benjamin, and on my clan, and on me — and I was not there. I was hiding in the baggage.

*(He laughs at himself.)*

I am a head taller than any man in Israel. It is not easy to hide me. They pulled me out from between the packs.

**DAVID**
Why did you hide?

**SAUL**
Why does anyone?

**DAVID**
Did you want it?

*(SAUL looks at him. No one asks the king this.)*

**SAUL**
Nobody put that question to me. Not then. Not since.

*(He lets it go by.)*

What do you want, son of Jesse? No boy walks into a king's house wanting nothing. A field? A place in the guard? Say it. I am in a giving mood, and it does not come often.

**DAVID**
To live in the house of the LORD, my lord. All my days.

**SAUL**
That is a priest's wish. You are of Judah.

**DAVID**
I know it. I would carry the water.

*(SAUL is quiet for some time.)*

**SAUL**
I would have liked that. To be let off.

*(A beat.)*

Samuel does not come to me anymore.

*(DAVID opens his mouth. There is nothing he is permitted to say.)*

**DAVID**
I am sorry, my lord.

**SAUL**
Why should you be sorry? You have done nothing. Play.

*(DAVID plays. The light turns.)*

*A NIGHT. A bad one. SAUL is on his feet with the spear in his hand, moving. The servants are flat against the wall. DAVID comes in fast, straight from the road, the lyre already off his shoulder.*

**SAUL**
Do not come near me.

*(DAVID stops — at the first distance, the place where he was stood on the first night. He does not wait to be told. He plays.)*

**SAUL**
It says I am finished. It says it in his voice.

*(DAVID plays.)*

"The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day, and has given it to your neighbor, who is better than you."

*(He turns on the room.)*

My neighbor. Which neighbor? I look at every man who comes through that door. I look at my captains. I look at my own —

*(He cannot say "son.")*

Who is he? Where is he standing?

*(DAVID's hand falters on the strings. For one breath there is no music.)*

**DAVID**
My lord, I —

**SAUL**
What?

*(Silence. DAVID looks at the king: the spear, the shaking hand, the face. He decides.)*

**DAVID**
I will play until it is light.

*(He plays. And, low, almost under it:)*

*(sung)*
"For the sake of my brothers and my friends,
let me speak peace upon you…"

*(SAUL's arm comes down. The spear-butt touches the floor. He leans on it; then he is sitting; then his head is back against the wall. His breathing slows. He sleeps.)*

*(DAVID plays on, softer, and stops. He crosses to the king — nearer than he has ever been. Gently he draws the spear out of the sleeping hand and stands it against the far wall. He comes back, sits on the floor at SAUL's feet, and takes up the lyre.)*

**[Cue ends: the phrase thins to a single string and stops with the dawn.]**

*MORNING. SAUL wakes. DAVID is asleep where he sat, the lyre across his knees.*

**SAUL**
Has he been here all night?

**OLD SERVANT**
He would not go, my lord.

*(SAUL looks at the boy; at his own empty hand; at the spear standing across the room. He says nothing about it.)*

**SAUL**
Send to Jesse. Say: "Let David remain in my service, for he has found favor in my eyes."

*(DAVID stirs, sees the king awake, scrambles up.)*

**DAVID**
My lord — forgive me —

**SAUL**
You will carry my armor.

**DAVID**
I do not know how to carry armor, my lord.

**SAUL**
It is not heavy when there is no war. It means that you stand near me.

**DAVID**
And my father's flock?

*(SAUL stares; then, for the second time, the laugh.)*

**SAUL**
The king offers him the place at his side, and he asks after the sheep.

*(Then, seriously — he has understood something about the boy.)*

Go, and come. When I send, you come. When I am well, go back to them.

*(A beat. It is hard for him.)*

Come when I do not send.

**DAVID**
As the king wishes.

**SAUL**
No. Not "as the king wishes."

*(He cannot find the other way to say it.)*

Come.

**DAVID**
I will come.

*(A RUNNER enters, dust to the knees.)*

**RUNNER**
My lord the king. The Philistines have mustered. They are camped between Socoh and Azekah — on our side of the valley, in Judah.

*(SAUL rises. It is a different man: the back straightens, the voice drops. He crosses the hall and takes up the spear himself.)*

**SAUL**
Call out Israel.

*(The servants scatter. DAVID has slung the lyre and is looking for something to carry.)*

Not you.

**DAVID**
I am the king's armor-bearer.

**SAUL**
In this hall. Your father has three sons marching with me already; he will keep the youngest. A valley full of Philistines is no place for a harp. Go home to your sheep.

**DAVID**
My lord —

**SAUL**
*(not harshly)* Go home, son of Jesse.

*(SAUL goes out toward the war, the spear in his hand. DAVID stands alone in the hall with the lyre. Then he goes the other way, toward Bethlehem. Somewhere the phrase begins again, and breaks off.)*
