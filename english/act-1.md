# ACT I

## From the Flock to the King's Table

---

## Scene 1 — The Flock and the Choosing

*Two places share the stage. A hillside pasture outside Bethlehem, in morning light. Jesse's courtyard, in shadow, waiting. When the light moves from one to the other, the work in the first does not stop.*

*PASTURE. DAVID, a boy, a small lyre slung on his back, stands in the gap of a stone fold and blocks it with his body. Behind him the flock presses.*

**DAVID**
No. Not you. You know it isn't you.

*(A big ewe shoves. He shoves back with his hip.)*

Levana. Levana. I have seen you eat a thornbush. You will live till noon.

*(He lets the lambs out one at a time, counting by touch.)*

One. Two. You — yes, you, go on. Three. Four. Where is —

*(He looks back into the fold.)*

Come. Nobody is going to step on you. I'm standing here.

*(The last lamb comes. He walks it to the low grass.)*

This is the tender grass. Down here, before the sun gets at it. You can't chew the other kind yet; you have hardly any teeth. So you go first. That's all it is. There's no honor in it.

*(The lambs eat. He crouches, pleased, watching them.)*

Slowly. There is enough.

"The eyes of all look to You in hope,
and You give them their food in its season."

*(One lamb stands apart, not eating.)*

What? What is it?

*(He goes to it, kneels, feels along the leg, looks in the mouth. Finds a burr in the lip. Draws it out.)*

There. All that sorrow for one thorn.

*(He sets the lamb down among the others. It eats.)*

"The LORD is my shepherd; I lack nothing."

*(To the lamb.)*

That one is mine. You can have it.

*Light: BETHLEHEM. SAMUEL enters, old, travel-worn, leading a heifer on a rope. The ELDERS of the town come out to meet him and stop short.*

**ELDER**
Seer. That is a long road from Ramah.

**SAMUEL**
It is.

**ELDER**
We were not told to expect you.

**SAMUEL**
No.

*(The ELDER looks to the others. No one helps him.)*

**ELDER**
"Do you come in peace?"

**SAMUEL**
"In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Make yourselves holy, and come with me to the sacrifice."

*(The ELDERS breathe.)*

**ELDER**
Forgive us. When the prophet comes unannounced, a town begins to count its sins.

**SAMUEL**
Let it count. It does a town no harm. Where is Jesse?

*(JESSE comes forward with seven sons behind him: ELIAB, ABINADAB, SHAMMAH and four more. At the edge of the courtyard, the women of the house — among them DAVID'S MOTHER and ZERUIAH.)*

**JESSE**
Here, my lord.

**SAMUEL**
You and your sons will stand with me at the sacrifice. All of them.

**JESSE**
They are here.

*(He puts a hand on the eldest.)*

Eliab. My firstborn.

**ELIAB**
My lord. My father's house is honored. Tell us what is needed — wood, water, the knife — and my brothers and I will see to it. You have walked far enough today.

*(SAMUEL looks at him: the height, the shoulders, the ease.)*

**SAMUEL**
You have carried arms.

**ELIAB**
When the king calls Judah, I go. My brothers behind me.

**SAMUEL**
*(low, to himself)* "Surely the LORD's anointed stands before Him."

*(He reaches toward the horn at his belt — and stops. He listens. No voice is heard. The family waits. It is a long time.)*

**JESSE**
My lord?

**SAMUEL**
*(not to them; giving back what he has been given)*
"Do not look at his appearance or at the height of his stature, for I have rejected him. It is not as a man sees. A man sees what is before his eyes. The LORD sees into the heart."

*(ELIAB has caught one word of it.)*

**ELIAB**
Have I given offense?

**SAMUEL**
No. You have done nothing. Stand with your brothers.

*(ELIAB steps back. He does not know what he has lost, only that he has lost it.)*

*Light: PASTURE. The lambs are down in the grass. DAVID has an old ewe by his side and is walking at her pace, which is slow.*

**DAVID**
Now the old ones. — Levana, I can see you. I can see you thinking.

*(To the old ewe.)*

No hurry. This patch has had a little sun; it's soft still, but it will hold you. Your teeth aren't what they were either. You and the little ones — the same trouble from two ends.

*(She stops. He stops.)*

You have carried a lamb every spring since before I could walk. Nobody is driving you today.

*(She lowers her head and eats. He stays beside her until he is sure.)*

"The LORD is good to all,
and His mercies are over all that He has made."

*Light: BETHLEHEM. JESSE brings ABINADAB before SAMUEL. SAMUEL does not study him. He waits, listening, and is answered.*

**SAMUEL**
"Nor has the LORD chosen this one."

*(SHAMMAH.)*

"Nor has the LORD chosen this one."

**JESSE**
My lord — chosen for what?

**SAMUEL**
For what I was sent to do.

*(The fourth son. The fifth. SAMUEL shakes his head each time.)*

**JESSE**
If my lord would tell me what he is looking for, I could spare him the trouble. I know my sons.

**SAMUEL**
I am not the one looking. That was my mistake with your firstborn.

*(The sixth. The seventh. SAMUEL looks at the last of them, and then at JESSE.)*

"The LORD has not chosen these."

*(Silence. The meat is waiting. Nobody moves toward it.)*

"Are these all the boys?"

*(JESSE does not answer at once. ELIAB looks at his father. At the edge of the courtyard, DAVID'S MOTHER has lifted her head.)*

**JESSE**
"There is still the youngest. He is out tending the sheep."

**SAMUEL**
You have another son.

**JESSE**
He is with the flock.

**SAMUEL**
I said all your sons.

**JESSE**
Someone has to be with the flock.

**SAMUEL**
"Send and bring him. We will not sit down until he comes here."

**JESSE**
Eliab.

**ELIAB**
It is the far pasture. An hour there and back. The meat —

**SAMUEL**
Then we will stand an hour.

*(ELIAB goes. The family remains standing, exactly as they are.)*

*Light: PASTURE. The big ewe is pushing at the gap again. The lambs are still eating.*

**DAVID**
Wait. Let them finish.

*(She pushes. He plants his feet.)*

You are strong. You can shove through and take, and you would leave them the stalks. They can't shove back. So I am the one in the gate. You will have the hard grass on the ridge, the kind nobody else can chew, and you will like it better.

*(ELIAB arrives and hears the end of this. DAVID, his back to him, is still talking to the ewe.)*

Don't sulk. There is some for you too.

**ELIAB**
David.

**DAVID**
*(at once, before he turns)* Here I am.

*(He turns.)*

Eliab? What has happened? Is Father —

**ELIAB**
Father is well. He is calling you.

**DAVID**
Now? I can't leave them in the middle. The strong ones haven't —

**ELIAB**
Samuel is waiting.

**DAVID**
Samuel.

*(He takes this in.)*

The prophet is in Bethlehem for a sacrifice. You don't need me for a sacrifice.

**ELIAB**
No one said we needed you. He said he would not sit down until you came.

**DAVID**
Why?

**ELIAB**
He did not say. He looked at me. He looked at each of us, one after another. Then he asked whether there was anyone else.

*(A beat.)*

Father had to think.

*(DAVID receives this. He does not answer it. He turns back to the fold.)*

**DAVID**
There. Now. Your turn.

*(He steps out of the gap. The strong sheep pour through.)*

Up to the ridge. Go on, Levana — lead them. You have wanted to lead all morning.

*(He watches until the whole flock is feeding.)*

Is there a boy with you?

**ELIAB**
The servant's son followed me up.

**DAVID**
*(calling off)* Stand in the gap! Don't let the big ones come down until the little ones are lying in the shade!

*(He picks up his staff. ELIAB looks him over.)*

**ELIAB**
You smell of them.

**DAVID**
I know.

**ELIAB**
He is the prophet of the LORD.

**DAVID**
Then he has smelled sheep before.

*(They go. The two lights become one.)*

## Scene 2 — The Son of Jesse

*Jesse's courtyard. The family is standing as it was left. Places have been laid for the meal: one for the prophet, one for Jesse, seven for the sons.*

*DAVID enters behind ELIAB and stops at the edge, where the women are. He bows.*

**DAVID**
My lord. Father.

*(SAMUEL looks at him for a long time. Then he listens — and his hand goes to the horn of oil.)*

**JESSE**
My lord. What is the oil for?

**SAMUEL**
You know what oil is for, Jesse.

**JESSE**
For a priest. For a king. We are neither, in this house.

**SAMUEL**
The LORD has seen, among your sons, the one He wants.

*(He draws the horn from his belt.)*

**JESSE**
Then before you pour it, there is a thing you must know. I will not have the prophet of the LORD deceived under my roof.

**SAMUEL**
Speak.

**JESSE**
We come from Ruth the Moabite. My father's father was her son. All my life there have been men at the gate who say, quietly, that the Torah shuts a Moabite out of the assembly of the LORD, and that the house of Boaz was built on a mistake. I am not a learned man. There came a year when I feared they were right — that I had no right to a daughter of Israel for my wife. I separated from her.

**SAMUEL**
"A Moabite man — not a Moabite woman." The law speaks of the men. Ruth was permitted. So are you. So are your sons.

**JESSE**
I know it now. I did not know it then. And it is not what I have to tell you.

*(He cannot look at DAVID.)*

This boy was born after I had separated from her.

*(Silence. The brothers do not look at DAVID. They have never needed it explained. Only DAVID is hearing it for the first time.)*

**DAVID**
*(quietly)* Is that why.

*(DAVID'S MOTHER steps out from among the women.)*

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
Jesse.

**JESSE**
This is not for you to —

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
The night you sent for the servant woman, it was I who was with you.

**JESSE**
You.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
She came to me and told me what you had asked of her. She did not want it. I did not want it. We changed places. It was dark, and you did not speak. He is your son.

*(JESSE looks at her. At the boy. At her.)*

**JESSE**
All these years. You let me think — you let them think —

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
You had already judged yourself. Was I to stand in the gate and tell Bethlehem what Jesse had intended, and how his wife had deceived him? I would have saved my name and broken yours. So I kept it. And I kept him.

*(To SAMUEL.)*

My lord, I would have kept it until I died. But you are holding the oil.

**DAVID**
Mother. You knew.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
I knew whose son you are. Every day.

**DAVID**
You put bread in my hand every morning and sent me up the hill and you said nothing.

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
I said your name. I said it as often as I could.

*(JESSE crosses to DAVID. His hand rises toward the boy's shoulder and stops in the air.)*

**JESSE**
David. My son.

*(At last the hand comes down. DAVID stands under it. He does not know yet what to do with it.)*

**DAVID**
Father.

*(SAMUEL has waited. Now ELIAB speaks — carefully.)*

**ELIAB**
My lord. You have seen all of us. I am the eldest. My brothers have carried spears for Israel. He has carried lambs. I ask it with respect: what does the LORD want with him?

**SAMUEL**
I asked the same question when I saw you. I was answered. I have stopped looking.

*(To DAVID.)*

Come here.

*(DAVID does not move.)*

**DAVID**
My lord — what is the oil for?

**SAMUEL**
The LORD has chosen you to be king over His people Israel.

**DAVID**
King?

*(He looks around the courtyard as if the man might be standing in it.)*

Israel has a king.

**SAMUEL**
It has. I poured this same oil on his head.

**DAVID**
Then what am I to do? Stand up against him?

**SAMUEL**
I was not told that. I was told: fill your horn and go to Jesse of Bethlehem. I am an old man, and I have learned to say no more than I am given. Saul is king of Israel today.

*(A beat. This costs him.)*

What I can tell you, I tell you not as a prophet but as a man who has not finished mourning him: do not raise your hand against Saul.

**DAVID**
I would never. He is the LORD's anointed.

**SAMUEL**
In a moment, so are you. Remember that you said it.

*(To all of them.)*

And remember this. If Saul hears what was done in this courtyard, he will kill me, and he will not stop with me. I came with a heifer on a rope so that I could say I came to sacrifice, and say it truly. No one here will speak of this. Not in the gate. Not in the camp.

*(He looks at ELIAB until ELIAB lowers his eyes.)*

**DAVID**
I did not ask to be king.

**SAMUEL**
What did you ask?

**DAVID**
"One thing I have asked of the LORD,
that one thing I will seek:
to dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life,
to gaze on the sweetness of the LORD
and to come early to His sanctuary."

*(Plainly.)*

That is all of it. I wanted to be where He is. To be let in. I would play there, I would pray there, I would sweep the floor if they'd have me. And not be sent out again.

**SAMUEL**
I was given to that house as a child. I slept beside the Ark. I know what you are asking for.

*(Gently; and then not gently.)*

And Israel? Who will care for Israel?

**DAVID**
Someone who knows how.

**SAMUEL**
You can seek His face from wherever He sets you down. I have had to.

*(DAVID turns — not to his father, not to his mother. To his sister.)*

**DAVID**
Zeruiah. You always say the true thing. What do I know about being a king?

**ZERUIAH**
Nothing.

*(A beat.)*

Neither did Saul. Ask the one holding the oil. He has done this before.

**DAVID**
*(to SAMUEL)* Who will teach me?

**SAMUEL**
You do not need to know everything today. Today you need to come here.

*(DAVID looks once toward the hill, where the flock is. Then he comes, and kneels. SAMUEL listens. When he speaks, he is repeating an order.)*

"Arise, anoint him; for this is he."

*(He anoints DAVID in the midst of his brothers. The oil runs. Nobody speaks. DAVID is very still; then he lifts his eyes.)*

**DAVID**
"Your face, LORD, I seek."

*(SAMUEL closes the horn.)*

**SAMUEL**
Now we may sit.

*(The family goes to the places. Seven sons, seven places. DAVID is left standing. DAVID'S MOTHER brings an eighth bowl and holds it, not knowing where to set it.)*

**JESSE**
Beside me.

*(She sets it there. DAVID sits beside his father. ELIAB passes him the bread without looking at him. They eat.)*

*(While the others eat, DAVID rises quietly, takes his lyre, and goes to the edge of the courtyard that opens on the pasture. He raises the lyre. He does not yet play.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: THE "I WANT" SONG — original song to be written by Roei Barak.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, alone, at the edge between the courtyard and the pasture; the family still at the meal behind him.
**Addressed to:** God — in the same voice he used with the lambs.
**Physical action:** the oil is still on his head; he is walking back up to the flock; he arrives among the sheep before the song ends.
**Why song, here:** he has been told what he is to become, and forbidden to tell anyone. The lyre is the only place it can go.
**What it must accomplish:** state the one desire (Psalm 27:4 — to dwell in God's house and be near Him); let the audience fall in love with his joy in the sheep and the music; carry him, verse by verse, from the private wish toward the first acceptance that he will have to carry others. He does not resolve it. He does not ask for the throne.
**Exit:** on the hill, among the flock. The last phrase is left open; SAUL's sleeplessness answers it.**]**

## Scene 3 — The Musician

*Gibeah. The king's hall, deep in the night. SAUL — tall, a soldier's body that cannot find a way to sit. A spear leans beside his seat. An OLD SERVANT and a YOUNG MAN keep their distance.*

**SAUL**
Is it morning?

**OLD SERVANT**
Not yet, my lord.

**SAUL**
It was "not yet" an hour ago.

*(He stands. Sits. Stands.)*

Has anyone come from Ramah?

**OLD SERVANT**
No one, my lord.

**SAUL**
He never used to send word. You would look up and he would be standing in the door, furious about something.

*(A beat.)*

No one.

**OLD SERVANT**
My lord — may your servants speak?

**SAUL**
My servants have been speaking for a month. I hear it stop when I come in.

**OLD SERVANT**
We are afraid for you. A dark spirit comes on the king, and we stand at the wall and can do nothing. Let our lord command us. Let us look for a man who knows how to play the lyre. When the spirit comes, he will play, and it will go easier with you.

**SAUL**
A harper.

*(Dry.)*

I have led Israel against Moab, and Ammon, and Edom, and the Philistines, and my servants have put their heads together and prescribed me a harper.

*(No one answers. He is too tired to keep it up.)*

"Find me a man who plays well, and bring him to me."

**YOUNG MAN**
"I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play. A brave man, a fighter, careful in his speech, good to look at. And the LORD is with him."

*(SAUL has heard only the last of it.)*

**SAUL**
Is He.

*(A beat.)*

Send for him.

*Light: BETHLEHEM. Jesse's courtyard. A MESSENGER of the king. JESSE, ELIAB, DAVID'S MOTHER. DAVID has just come down from the hill.*

**MESSENGER**
Thus says the king: "Send me your son David, who is with the flock."

*(Nobody breathes. JESSE does not look at ELIAB. ELIAB does not look at anyone.)*

**JESSE**
The king knows my son's name.

**MESSENGER**
The king was given it.

**JESSE**
What does the king want with him?

**MESSENGER**
To play. The king does not sleep.

*(The courtyard lets go.)*

**DAVID**
Me?

**MESSENGER**
If you are the one with the lyre.

**JESSE**
He is the one. Bring the donkey.

*(JESSE loads it himself: bread, a skin of wine, a young goat.)*

You carry these to the king from my hand. You play what he asks. You answer what you are asked, and no more.

**ELIAB**
*(low)* Not a word.

**JESSE**
He knows.

*(To DAVID.)*

David —

**DAVID**
I know, Father.

**JESSE**
I was going to say: eat something on the road.

*(It is the first time he has said such a thing. They both hear it. DAVID nods, and goes.)*

*Light: GIBEAH. DAVID enters the hall with the lyre in one hand and the kid's lead-rope in the other. SAUL, at the far end, lifts a hand.*

**SAUL**
Stand there.

*(DAVID stands there.)*

**DAVID**
My lord the king.

**SAUL**
You are Jesse's son.

**DAVID**
Yes, my lord.

**SAUL**
They told me a fighter. A man of war. You are a boy.

**DAVID**
I have only ever fought for sheep, my lord.

**SAUL**
And what is that?

**DAVID**
Bread. Wine. A young goat. From my father, for the king.

**SAUL**
Jesse of Bethlehem sends the king a goat.

*(Not unkindly.)*

Put it down. What do you play?

**DAVID**
What I play for the flock, my lord. I have never played for anyone who could tell me to stop.

*(Something in SAUL's face nearly moves.)*

**SAUL**
I will tell you. Play.

**[MUSICAL CUE: "LEMA'AN ACHAI VERE'AI" — Psalm 122:8–9. Melody and arrangement to be settled.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, lyre alone, from the place where SAUL has stood him.
**Addressed to:** SAUL. This is not a performance; he is trying to change another man's breathing.
**Physical action:** SAUL is on his feet, braced. DAVID plays to the body, not the face.
**Why song, here:** it is what he was sent for — and speech between a shepherd and a king has run out.]**

**DAVID**
*(sung)*
"For the sake of my brothers and my friends,
let me speak peace upon you.
For the sake of the house of the LORD our God,
I will seek your good."

*(SAUL stands rigid. Then the hand on the back of the seat loosens. DAVID lets the phrase come to its end and holds his hand over the strings.)*

**SAUL**
Go on.

*(DAVID plays the phrase again. SAUL sits. Under the music:)*

"For the sake of my brothers." You have brothers.

**DAVID**
Seven, my lord.

**SAUL**
And they are your friends?

**DAVID**
*(a beat)* They are my brothers.

*(SAUL laughs — once, surprised out of him. The servants look at each other: they have not heard it in a month.)*

**SAUL**
I have a tribe like that.

*(He listens.)*

"Peace upon you." Upon whom?

**DAVID**
Whoever is listening, my lord. This morning it was an old ewe.

**SAUL**
And tonight?

**DAVID**
Tonight, upon the king.

**SAUL**
Come closer. I cannot hear the low string from there.

*(DAVID comes half the distance. He plays. The light turns — night to day, day to night. DAVID goes out and comes in again, each time with the same phrase a little further along, each time standing a little nearer.)*

**[The cue continues as passage of time: the same musical sentence, shortened and returning, over several visits. Between visits DAVID is glimpsed on the hill with the flock. No number of months or years is stated.]**

*A DAY. SAUL is easy. He is eating. DAVID sits on the step below him, retuning.*

**SAUL**
Who taught you?

**DAVID**
No one, my lord. There is a great deal of time, with sheep.

**SAUL**
And do they listen?

**DAVID**
Sometimes. The old ones. The lambs don't care for it.

*(They share it — a small smile, the same on both faces.)*

**SAUL**
I never kept sheep. Donkeys. My father's donkeys strayed, and he sent me after them, and I went three days through the hill country and never found a hoof. I found Samuel instead.

*(DAVID's hands have stopped on the strings.)*

He had already found the donkeys. He knew everything, that man. He sat me at the head of thirty guests and had the cook set the thigh in front of me — the portion they had been keeping. And at daybreak he walked me to the edge of the town, where no one could see, and poured oil on my head.

*(DAVID does not move.)*

I was looking for donkeys.

**DAVID**
*(carefully)* And then, my lord?

**SAUL**
Then they gathered all Israel to draw the lot for a king, and the lot fell on Benjamin, and on my clan, and on me — and I was not there. I was hiding in the baggage.

*(He laughs at himself.)*

I am a head taller than any man in Israel. It is not easy to hide me. They pulled me out from between the packs.

**DAVID**
Why did you hide?

**SAUL**
Why does anyone?

**DAVID**
Did you want it?

*(SAUL looks at him. No one asks the king this.)*

**SAUL**
Nobody put that question to me. Not then. Not since.

*(He lets it go by.)*

What do you want, son of Jesse? No boy walks into a king's house wanting nothing. A field? A place in the guard? Say it. I am in a giving mood, and it does not come often.

**DAVID**
To live in the house of the LORD, my lord. All my days.

**SAUL**
That is a priest's wish. You are of Judah.

**DAVID**
I know it. I would carry the water.

*(SAUL is quiet for some time.)*

**SAUL**
I would have liked that. To be let off.

*(A beat.)*

Samuel does not come to me anymore.

*(DAVID opens his mouth. There is nothing he is permitted to say.)*

**DAVID**
I am sorry, my lord.

**SAUL**
Why should you be sorry? You have done nothing. Play.

*(DAVID plays. The light turns.)*

*A NIGHT. A bad one. SAUL is on his feet with the spear in his hand, moving. The servants are flat against the wall. DAVID comes in fast, straight from the road, the lyre already off his shoulder.*

**SAUL**
Do not come near me.

*(DAVID stops — at the first distance, the place where he was stood on the first night. He does not wait to be told. He plays.)*

**SAUL**
It says I am finished. It says it in his voice.

*(DAVID plays.)*

"The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day, and has given it to your neighbor, who is better than you."

*(He turns on the room.)*

My neighbor. Which neighbor? I look at every man who comes through that door. I look at my captains. I look at my own —

*(He cannot say "son.")*

Who is he? Where is he standing?

*(DAVID's hand falters on the strings. For one breath there is no music.)*

**DAVID**
My lord, I —

**SAUL**
What?

*(Silence. DAVID looks at the king: the spear, the shaking hand, the face. He decides.)*

**DAVID**
I will play until it is light.

*(He plays. And, low, almost under it:)*

*(sung)*
"For the sake of my brothers and my friends,
let me speak peace upon you…"

*(SAUL's arm comes down. The spear-butt touches the floor. He leans on it; then he is sitting; then his head is back against the wall. His breathing slows. He sleeps.)*

*(DAVID plays on, softer, and stops. He crosses to the king — nearer than he has ever been. Gently he draws the spear out of the sleeping hand and stands it against the far wall. He comes back, sits on the floor at SAUL's feet, and takes up the lyre.)*

**[Cue ends: the phrase thins to a single string and stops with the dawn.]**

*MORNING. SAUL wakes. DAVID is asleep where he sat, the lyre across his knees.*

**SAUL**
Has he been here all night?

**OLD SERVANT**
He would not go, my lord.

*(SAUL looks at the boy; at his own empty hand; at the spear standing across the room. He says nothing about it.)*

**SAUL**
Send to Jesse. Say: "Let David remain in my service, for he has found favor in my eyes."

*(DAVID stirs, sees the king awake, scrambles up.)*

**DAVID**
My lord — forgive me —

**SAUL**
You will carry my armor.

**DAVID**
I do not know how to carry armor, my lord.

**SAUL**
It is not heavy when there is no war. It means that you stand near me.

**DAVID**
And my father's flock?

*(SAUL stares; then, for the second time, the laugh.)*

**SAUL**
The king offers him the place at his side, and he asks after the sheep.

*(Then, seriously — he has understood something about the boy.)*

Go, and come. When I send, you come. When I am well, go back to them.

*(A beat. It is hard for him.)*

Come when I do not send.

**DAVID**
As the king wishes.

**SAUL**
No. Not "as the king wishes."

*(He cannot find the other way to say it.)*

Come.

**DAVID**
I will come.

*(A RUNNER enters, dust to the knees.)*

**RUNNER**
My lord the king. The Philistines have mustered. They are camped between Socoh and Azekah — on our side of the valley, in Judah.

*(SAUL rises. It is a different man: the back straightens, the voice drops. He crosses the hall and takes up the spear himself.)*

**SAUL**
Call out Israel.

*(The servants scatter. DAVID has slung the lyre and is looking for something to carry.)*

Not you.

**DAVID**
I am the king's armor-bearer.

**SAUL**
In this hall. Your father has three sons marching with me already; he will keep the youngest. A valley full of Philistines is no place for a harp. Go home to your sheep.

**DAVID**
My lord —

**SAUL**
*(not harshly)* Go home, son of Jesse.

*(SAUL goes out toward the war, the spear in his hand. DAVID stands alone in the hall with the lyre. Then he goes the other way, toward Bethlehem. Somewhere the phrase begins again, and breaks off.)*

---

## Scene 4 — I Am Looking for My Brothers

*Bethlehem, before dawn. JESSE is packing a sack. DAVID comes down from the hill.*

**JESSE**
Forty days, and no word. Three sons in that valley and I hear more from the crows than from the king's camp.

*(He ties the sack.)*

Roasted grain. Ten loaves. Run them down to your brothers. And these ten cheeses are for the captain of their thousand — put them in his hand yourself, and let him see whose son brings them.

**DAVID**
I will go at first light.

**JESSE**
"See how your brothers are faring, and bring me some token from them."

*(A beat.)*

David. A token. Not a story. You go, you look at their faces, you come back.

**DAVID**
And the flock?

**JESSE**
I kept sheep before you were born.

*(DAVID almost smiles. He shoulders the sack.)*

**DAVID**
There is a boy who knows the gap. I will leave them with him.

*(He goes. JESSE watches the road after he has gone from it.)*

*The Valley of Elah. The camp of Israel on its ridge. Men sitting. A man mending a strap that does not need mending. A man looking at the valley. Nobody speaks above a murmur.*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "ET ACHAI ANOCHI MEVAKESH" — "It is my brothers I am looking for." Version to be identified.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, humming, the sack on his shoulder — a boy on an errand.
**Addressed to:** no one; then, more and more, to the camp that does not answer.
**Physical action:** he walks the lines the way a shepherd counts a flock, looking into each face.
**What changes:** the tune is easy while he is sure he will find them. It thins as he understands what he is walking through.
**Exit:** GOLIATH's voice cuts it in the middle of a breath. It does not come back.]**

**DAVID**
*(to a soldier)* Peace to you. The sons of Jesse, of Bethlehem — Eliab, Abinadab, Shammah?

*(The soldier lifts a hand vaguely along the ridge and lets it fall.)*

*(To another.)* The thousand of Judah?

*(Nothing. He walks on. He slows. A voice comes up out of the valley — enormous, unhurried, bored.)*

**GOLIATH**
*(off)* "Choose yourselves a man, and let him come down to me!"

*(The humming stops. Along the ridge, men draw back from the edge without standing up. DAVID gives the sack to the KEEPER OF THE BAGGAGE and goes to where he can hear.)*

**GOLIATH**
*(off)* "I defy the ranks of Israel this day. Give me a man, and we will fight it out together!"

*(Silence. DAVID looks at the men. He crouches beside a SOLDIER who sits with his spear across his knees.)*

**DAVID**
Why is nobody doing anything?

**SOLDIER**
Have you seen him?

**DAVID**
I heard him.

**SOLDIER**
We have heard him for forty days. Morning and evening. You could set the watch by him.

**DAVID**
What does he want?

**SOLDIER**
One man. If our man kills him, they serve us. If he kills our man, we serve them — all of us, and our sons. That is the bargain.

**DAVID**
And nobody has gone.

**SOLDIER**
*(turning on him)* Listen, boy. I am not afraid to die. I have a wife in Tekoa and two children, and I have stood in the line against Philistines before, and I will again. But the man who goes down there and loses does not only die. He sells Israel. By his own hand. Who wants to be that man?

*(DAVID has no quick answer. He looks at the ridge.)*

**DAVID**
So you hold the hill.

**SOLDIER**
It is a good hill. He cannot come up it. The king says hold.

**DAVID**
For how long?

**SOLDIER**
Until something changes.

**DAVID**
He is the only thing changing. Every day he comes a little nearer, and every day you are a little more used to him.

*(The SOLDIER does not deny it.)*

**SOLDIER**
The king has promised: whoever strikes him down, the king will make rich, and give him his daughter, and his father's house will go free in Israel.

**DAVID**
And still nobody.

**SOLDIER**
And still nobody.

*(DAVID stands.)*

**DAVID**
"Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the ranks of the living God?"

*(He has not lowered his voice. Heads turn.)*

**SOLDIER**
Boy. That is Goliath of Gath.

**DAVID**
And we are Israel.

**SOLDIER**
Would you go down to him?

**DAVID**
Yes.

*(A stir runs along the line. A man shifts to let DAVID see the valley. Behind him:)*

**ELIAB**
"Why have you come down? And with whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness?"

**DAVID**
*(turning, glad)* Eliab! Father sent me. I have brought you —

**ELIAB**
"I know your insolence, and the wickedness of your heart. You came down to watch the battle."

*(The soldiers go quiet. ELIAB takes DAVID by the arm and walks him three steps off. Low, fast:)*

You were told. Not in the gate, not in the camp. And you stand on the king's ridge with your voice up, and every man in Judah's thousand looking at you. Do you know what becomes of our father's house if the king looks at you twice?

**DAVID**
He has looked at me. I have slept on his floor.

**ELIAB**
As a harper. Let him go on thinking so.

*(A beat. This is harder to say.)*

I stood in front of that old man and he looked through me like water. I have not said a word. Not one. I am asking you for the same.

**DAVID**
"What have I done now? It was only a word."

**ELIAB**
With you it is never only a word.

*(DAVID looks at his brother; then past him, at the valley.)*

**DAVID**
There is bread in the sack. And grain. Father wants a token that you are well.

*(He turns back to the SOLDIER.)*

Where is the king?

*(ELIAB lets his hand fall. A KING'S MAN comes along the line.)*

**KING'S MAN**
Which of you is the one who has been talking? The king has heard of it. He will see you.

*(DAVID goes with him. ELIAB is left among the soldiers, who are looking at him now.)*

---

## Scene 5 — The King's Armor

*SAUL's tent on the ridge. His armor hangs on its stand. ABNER beside him. DAVID is brought in. SAUL looks up — and knows him at once.*

**SAUL**
Son of Jesse.

*(Almost angry with relief.)*

I sent you home.

**DAVID**
My father sent me back, my lord. With bread.

**SAUL**
And they tell me you have been making speeches on my ridge. That you mean to go down to the Philistine.

**DAVID**
"Let no man's heart fail because of him. Your servant will go and fight this Philistine."

**ABNER**
My lord. The terms. If the boy falls, we are their slaves by our own agreement.

**SAUL**
I know the terms, Abner.

*(To DAVID; gently, as to someone he does not want to lose.)*

David. I have heard you play. I know what is in your hands. This is a different thing. "You cannot go against this Philistine to fight him. You are a boy, and he has been a man of war since his youth."

**DAVID**
"Your servant has been a shepherd of his father's flock. When a lion came, or a bear, and carried off a lamb from the flock, I went out after it, and struck it, and pulled the lamb out of its mouth."

*(SAUL is listening.)*

"And when it rose against me, I took it by the beard, and struck it, and killed it."

**SAUL**
You went after a lion. For one lamb.

**DAVID**
It was in my keeping.

*(A silence. ABNER looks at the boy again.)*

"Your servant has killed both the lion and the bear. This uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them, for he has defied the ranks of the living God. The LORD, who saved me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear — He will save me from the hand of this Philistine."

**SAUL**
*(quietly)* Forty days I have waited for a man to say that to me.

**DAVID**
My lord — may I ask a thing?

**SAUL**
Ask.

**DAVID**
Why has no one gone?

*(ABNER stiffens. SAUL does not.)*

**SAUL**
You mean: why have I not gone. I am a head taller than any man on this hill.

**DAVID**
I did not say it.

**SAUL**
You are the first who has not. If I go down and fall, Israel has no king by evening, and the Philistines are in Gibeah by morning. A king is not his own to wager. I have sat here forty days learning that.

*(DAVID bows his head. He accepts it entirely.)*

**DAVID**
Then send one who is his own.

**SAUL**
You are not your own either. You are Jesse's. And you are mine.

*(He stands.)*

I cannot go down to him in your place. But you will not ask me to send you out like this, with nothing, and do nothing to cover you. Bring my armor.

*(DAVID has his sling in his hand. He looks at it; lays it down. Only then does SAUL go to the stand.)*

*(SAUL dresses DAVID himself, without haste — it is nearly a rite. The coat of mail. The bronze helmet. Last, he girds his own sword over the armor. He steps back.)*

*(The tent has gone silent. ABNER, the guards — all are looking at the same thing: the shepherd, standing in the armor of the king.)*

**SAUL**
No one has worn it but me.

*(He does not hear what he has said. ABNER does.)*

Now. Walk.

*(DAVID tries. One step. Another. He stops.)*

**DAVID**
"I cannot walk in these. I have never tried them."

*(He lifts off the helmet and puts it into SAUL's hands. Unbuckles the sword; into SAUL's hands. The coat of mail. Piece by piece the king receives his armor back, until they stand face to face, and only one of them is armed.)*

**SAUL**
And how will you stand against him?

*(DAVID picks up the sling. His staff. The shepherd's bag.)*

**DAVID**
I did not come to stand against him, my lord. I came to bring him down.

*(SAUL looks at him for a long moment.)*

**SAUL**
"Go. And the LORD be with you."

*(DAVID goes. SAUL stands holding his own sword. He watches the boy walk down toward the valley.)*

"Abner. Whose son is that boy?"

**ABNER**
Jesse's, my lord. Of Bethlehem. He has played in your hall a hundred nights.

**SAUL**
I know his father's name. I asked whose son he is.

*(ABNER looks at the king; at the armor in his arms; at the small figure on the slope.)*

**ABNER**
"By your life, O king — I do not know."

---

## Scene 6 — Goliath and Uriah

*The valley floor. A brook. The two armies are far apart on their hills, small; the distance between them is the whole stage. DAVID kneels at the water.*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "ZOCHRENI NA" — "Remember me." Version and lyric to be settled.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, kneeling, half-voiced.
**Addressed to:** God.
**Physical action:** he chooses five smooth stones from the brook, one at a time, weighing each, and puts them in the shepherd's bag. He is not set facing the audience to sing; the song *is* the choosing.
**Why song, here:** the noise of both camps has fallen away; this is the last moment he is alone.
**What changes:** on the fifth stone he stands.
**Exit:** he walks into GOLIATH's sight; the music stops when the giant sees him.**
**Note:** the title phrase is Samson's (Judges 16:28). It is a borrowed prayer, used theatrically; not presented as David's recorded words.]**

*(GOLIATH, armored from head to shin. Before him walks his SHIELD-BEARER with the great shield. GOLIATH sees the boy, the staff, the sling — and motions. The SHIELD-BEARER plants the shield in front of him.)*

**GOLIATH**
A stone? You have come to fight me with a stone?

*(He raps the shield.)*

This is a wall. You will not breach it.

*(He knocks on his helmet.)*

And over my head — a dome of iron. No stone comes through it.

*(He looks at the staff.)*

"Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?"

**DAVID**
"You come against me with sword and spear and javelin. I come against you in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the ranks of Israel, whom you have defied."

**GOLIATH**
"Come here to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field."

**DAVID**
"And all the earth shall know that there is a God in Israel. And all this assembly shall know that the LORD does not save by sword and spear. For the battle is the LORD's — and He will give you into our hands."

*(GOLIATH comes on, slowly, behind his wall. DAVID does not wait for him. He runs.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 23:4 — "GAM KI ELECH."**
**Who begins:** DAVID, on the run.
**Addressed to:** God — "You are with me."
**Physical action:** the walk becomes a run; a stone goes from the bag to his palm to the sling; the sling begins to turn.
**What changes:** the words stop before the release. The stone flies in complete silence.]**

**DAVID**
*(sung, running)*
"Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death,
I will fear no evil —
for You are with me —"

*(The sling opens. Silence. The stone strikes under the rim of the helmet. GOLIATH stands a moment longer. Then he falls on his face, and the ground takes it.)*

*(Nobody moves on either hill. DAVID runs to the body. He has no sword. He hauls at the giant's own — it is pinned. He goes for the neck: the collar of the armor is laced shut. His fingers cannot find the way in.)*

*(The SHIELD-BEARER has not run. He is standing with the shield in his hands, looking at the man it was supposed to cover.)*

**SHIELD-BEARER**
Not from there.

*(DAVID looks up.)*

**DAVID**
You know how it opens?

**SHIELD-BEARER**
He said no stone would get through.

**DAVID**
Where is the knot?

**SHIELD-BEARER**
*(not moving)* If I help you — will you give me a wife from Israel?

*(On the far hill, the Philistine line is beginning to understand. There is no time.)*

**DAVID**
Yes. Open it.

*(The SHIELD-BEARER lets the great shield fall. He kneels, reaches under the collar, and frees the lacing with one pull.)*

**DAVID**
What is your name?

**SHIELD-BEARER**
Uriah.

*(DAVID drags GOLIATH's sword out of its sheath, and with it cuts off his head. He lifts it. From the Philistine hill, a sound like a wall coming down: they break, and run.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 18:38 — "I PURSUE MY ENEMIES."**
**Who begins:** DAVID, facing the fleeing Philistines — forward, not out front.
**Second voice:** the SOLDIER from the ridge. Then, phrase by phrase, the camp.
**Physical action:** men get to their feet for the first time in forty days and come down the hill.
**What changes:** one man's charge becomes an army's. By the end DAVID is not alone at the center; all Israel is moving.
**Exit:** the pursuit carries the stage empty.]**

**DAVID**
*(sung)*
"I pursue my enemies and overtake them —"

**SOLDIER**
*(rising, his spear in his hand at last)*
"— and I do not turn back until they are finished!"

**THE CAMP**
"I pursue my enemies and overtake them,
and I do not turn back until they are finished!"

*(Israel pours down into the valley. URIAH stands where he was, beside the fallen shield, and watches them pass.)*

## Scene 7 — Covenant

*Evening. The ridge. ABNER brings DAVID before SAUL; the Philistine's head is still in the boy's hand, wrapped in his cloak. JONATHAN stands at his father's side. Soldiers crowd behind.*

**SAUL**
*(for all to hear)* "Whose son are you, young man?"

*(DAVID understands that it is being asked for the army.)*

**DAVID**
"The son of your servant Jesse, of Bethlehem."

**SAUL**
Then let it be heard in the camp: the house of Jesse of Bethlehem is free in Israel.

*(A roar. Somewhere in it, ELIAB's face.)*

Today you stay with us. You do not go back to your father's house.

**DAVID**
My father is waiting to hear that I am alive.

**SAUL**
I will send to him. He has had enough of you; it is my turn.

*(He grips DAVID's shoulder — the old warmth, whole — and is drawn away by ABNER and the captains. The crowd goes with the king. JONATHAN does not.)*

**JONATHAN**
I am Jonathan.

**DAVID**
The king's son.

**JONATHAN**
Yes.

*(He is a soldier; he does not know how to begin.)*

I watched you walk down. I kept waiting for you to stop.

**DAVID**
I kept waiting for someone to go.

**JONATHAN**
And when no one did?

**DAVID**
I could not go on listening to him.

*(JONATHAN nods. It lands somewhere sore.)*

**JONATHAN**
I heard him for forty days. I want you to know that I am not a man who is frightened of a hill. At Michmash I climbed a cliff on my hands and knees, with one boy to carry my shield, and we took a Philistine post between us, because I said to him: nothing stops the LORD from saving, by many or by few.

**DAVID**
I know. In Bethlehem we sing it.

**JONATHAN**
Then where was I these forty days?

*(DAVID waits.)*

My father forbade it. The heir is not his own to wager. I obeyed him. Every morning that voice came up the valley, and I obeyed my father, and I told myself that obeying was the harder thing.

**DAVID**
It may have been.

**JONATHAN**
Do not be kind to me. I said the words at Michmash. You did them here.

**DAVID**
You said them first. I only had to remember them.

*(JONATHAN looks at him — and laughs; he has his father's laugh.)*

**JONATHAN**
They told me you were careful in your speech.

*(He unfastens his robe and holds it out.)*

**DAVID**
That is yours.

**JONATHAN**
I am giving it to you.

**DAVID**
I gave your father's armor back to him this morning.

**JONATHAN**
I saw. His was lent. This is given.

*(He puts the robe on DAVID's shoulders. Then his tunic of mail; his sword; his bow; his belt. He stands in his shirt. They both take in the sight: the shepherd dressed as the prince, the prince unarmored.)*

Does it fit?

**DAVID**
Nobody has asked me to walk in it yet.

**JONATHAN**
Walk.

*(DAVID walks. It fits.)*

Let us make a covenant, David. You and I, before the LORD. Whatever comes to you comes to me. When I am king in Israel —

*(DAVID's face changes. JONATHAN sees it and misreads it.)*

— no, hear me — when I am king, you will stand at my right hand, and no one will say again that the king's house sat on a hill while a boy went down.

*(DAVID opens his mouth. The oil; the oath in the courtyard; this man's father. He cannot.)*

**DAVID**
Jonathan —

**JONATHAN**
What?

**DAVID**
Whatever comes — I will not be the one who breaks it. Not with you. Not with your house after you.

**JONATHAN**
*(lightly; he does not know what he has been given)* My house can look after itself.

*(He holds out his hand. DAVID takes it.)*

**JONATHAN**
Covenant.

**DAVID**
Covenant.

## Scene 8 — Thousands and Ten Thousands

*The road home. Out of every town the WOMEN come with timbrels and dancing to meet the army. SAUL at the head; DAVID behind, among the captains, in JONATHAN's robe. At the edge of the dance, MICHAL — the king's younger daughter — is watching one man.*

**[MUSICAL CUE: THE WOMEN'S SONG — 1 Samuel 18:7.**
**Who begins:** the WOMEN, to the whole returning army.
**Physical action:** one circle of dancing, king and soldiers alike inside it.
**What changes:** on the repeat, the circle divides in two without anyone deciding it. The music does not stop when SAUL steps out of it.]**

**WOMEN**
"Saul has struck down his thousands —"

*(SAUL lifts his head, smiling.)*

"— and David his ten thousands!"

*(The soldiers cheer. JONATHAN pulls DAVID into the dance. DAVID, laughing, protests; dances. The women take it up again, louder.)*

"Saul has struck down his thousands,
and David his ten thousands!"

*(SAUL has slowed. The dance flows past him and closes around DAVID. He is standing alone at the side of the road.)*

**SAUL**
"To David they have given ten thousands. To me they have given the thousands."

*(He watches the boy in his son's robe.)*

"What more can he have — but the kingdom?"

*(The song goes on, distant. SAUL does not take his eyes from DAVID. Light closes to:)*

*The next day. The hall at Gibeah. SAUL in his seat, the spear in his hand. DAVID enters with the lyre and goes, as always, to the nearer place. SAUL lifts a finger.*

**SAUL**
Stand there.

*(The far place. The first night's distance. DAVID goes to it.)*

Play.

*(DAVID plays the phrase — "Lema'an Achai." It does not do what it used to do.)*

"For the sake of my brothers and my friends." The women have a new song. Have you heard it?

**DAVID**
I heard it, my lord. I did not ask them to sing it.

**SAUL**
No. You never ask for anything. You wanted to carry water in the house of the LORD.

*(A beat.)*

Ten thousands.

**DAVID**
They were counting Philistines, my lord. Not kings.

**SAUL**
They were counting.

*(DAVID plays. SAUL's hand opens and closes on the shaft.)*

I told you a thing once, in this room, at night. Do you remember it?

**DAVID**
I remember everything the king has told me.

**SAUL**
"Your neighbor, who is better than you." I asked you where he was standing.

*(DAVID's hands go on. His face does not move.)*

You said you would play until it was light.

**DAVID**
I will play until it is light, my lord.

**SAUL**
Enough.

*(DAVID stops.)*

**DAVID**
Would the king have something else?

*(SAUL throws the spear. DAVID twists aside. The spear stands quivering in the wall where his chest was.)*

*(Neither man moves. The servants are stone. DAVID is breathing hard. He looks at the spear; at the king. He goes to the wall and pulls the spear free.)*

*(He carries it back across the hall and lays it on the floor at SAUL's feet. He returns to his place. He picks up the lyre. He plays.)*

*(SAUL stares at him. Something in his face is close to breaking — toward the boy, or against him; for a breath it could be either. Then he snatches up the spear and throws it again.)*

*(DAVID evades it. This time he does not go to the wall. He stands with the lyre hanging from his hand.)*

**DAVID**
*(very quietly)* My lord. I do not know what I have done.

**SAUL**
*(not looking at him)* Nothing. You have done nothing. That is what I cannot —

*(He masters himself. When he speaks again it is the king, cold.)*

You wanted to carry my armor in the field. You will do better. I give you a thousand men. You will go out and come in at their head.

**DAVID**
As the king commands.

**SAUL**
And David.

*(DAVID turns at the door.)*

Do not come unless I send.

*(It goes into DAVID like the spear did not. He bows, and goes.)*

*(Outside: the thousand. DAVID walks out to them in JONATHAN's robe and they close around him, asking, listening, laughing at something he says. He goes out; he comes in; they follow. From the door of the hall SAUL watches all of it, with the spear in the wall behind him.)*

---

## Scene 9 — A Mission No One Returns From

*Two places, seen by turns. SAUL's chamber, where things are planned. The gate of the camp, where DAVID receives them. The audience hears both. DAVID hears only his own.*

*CHAMBER. SAUL. DAVID before him, a captain now, dust on Jonathan's robe.*

**SAUL**
"Here is my elder daughter, Merab. I will give her to you as a wife. Only be a man of valor for me, and fight the LORD's battles."

**DAVID**
"Who am I, and what is my life, or my father's family in Israel, that I should be son-in-law to the king?"

**SAUL**
You are the man who killed the Philistine. It was promised.

**DAVID**
I did not go down for a promise, my lord.

**SAUL**
No. That is what troubles everyone.

*(DAVID bows and goes. SAUL, alone:)*

"Let my hand not be on him. Let the hand of the Philistines be on him."

*(The OLD SERVANT enters.)*

**OLD SERVANT**
My lord. The day came for the wedding, and Merab has been given to Adriel of Meholah — as you ordered. The son of Jesse said nothing. He went out with his thousand the same morning.

**SAUL**
And came back.

**OLD SERVANT**
He comes back, my lord. Every time.

*(A beat.)*

There is another thing. Your daughter Michal. The women say she loves him.

*(SAUL is very still. Then something like pleasure.)*

**SAUL**
Does she.

"I will give her to him, and she shall be a snare to him."

Go to him quietly. Say: the king delights in you, all his servants love you; become the king's son-in-law.

*GATE. The OLD SERVANT and another find DAVID among his men.*

**OLD SERVANT**
The king delights in you, son of Jesse. The whole house loves you. He offers you Michal.

**DAVID**
"Is it a small thing in your eyes, to become the king's son-in-law? I am a poor man, and of no account."

**OLD SERVANT**
He means it.

**DAVID**
He meant Merab. I have no bride-price for a king's daughter. I have a lyre and a sling. Tell him so, with my thanks.

*CHAMBER.*

**OLD SERVANT**
He says he has nothing to give for her.

**SAUL**
Nothing in silver.

**OLD SERVANT**
My lord — may I speak as one who was in the room the night he first played?

**SAUL**
You will whether I permit it or not.

**OLD SERVANT**
He went to war in your name. He still calls you "my lord the king" when no one is listening. I have heard him. And the day you threw the spear, he carried it back and laid it at your feet.

**SAUL**
Yes.

*(Low.)*

Do you know what it is, to be forgiven by a boy? In your own hall, with your servants watching?

*(The OLD SERVANT understands: they are no longer speaking of David's loyalty, but of Saul's fear.)*

**SAUL**
He has no silver. He has a sword. I gave him a command to be rid of him, and the men love him for it. The women sing his name louder than mine. A little longer, and they will say the seat is owed to him as well.

**OLD SERVANT**
What does the king command?

**SAUL**
When a man endangers the kingdom, there is no need to put him to death in the courtyard. You send him where the enemy will do it.

**OLD SERVANT**
David?

**SAUL**
If he lifts his eyes to the king's seat, let him show how faithful he is to the king.

**OLD SERVANT**
And the errand?

**SAUL**
"The king wants no bride-price but a hundred foreskins of the Philistines."

**OLD SERVANT**
For Michal.

**SAUL**
That is what you will tell him.

**OLD SERVANT**
A hundred. He would have to go in among them a hundred times, and bring proof of each.

**SAUL**
Yes.

**OLD SERVANT**
And if he returns?

*(SAUL does not answer that.)*

**SAUL**
Before him — not a word of this.

*GATE.*

**OLD SERVANT**
The king asks no silver. He asks a hundred Philistines. Proof of each.

**DAVID**
A hundred.

**OLD SERVANT**
*(he cannot help it)* It is a great many, son of Jesse. No one would think less of you —

**DAVID**
Tell the king I will bring them.

*(He turns to his men. The OLD SERVANT watches him go, and says nothing.)*

*(Battle and return, swift: a passage of drums; men going out, fewer coming in; DAVID among them.)*

*CHAMBER — the two places are now one. DAVID stands before SAUL, straight from the field. The OLD SERVANT has counted.*

**SAUL**
You have brought a hundred?

**DAVID**
Two hundred, my lord the king.

*(SAUL looks to the OLD SERVANT.)*

**OLD SERVANT**
Two hundred.

*(A long silence. SAUL looks at the man he sent to die.)*

**SAUL**
Call Michal.

*(MICHAL enters — every inch a king's daughter. SAUL joins their hands, briefly, and leaves them; he cannot stay in the room. They are alone. She looks him over.)*

**MICHAL**
You were asked for a hundred.

**DAVID**
I did not want the king to think I had counted closely.

**MICHAL**
A king's son-in-law should not look as if he counts at all.

*(She comes closer. She straightens the robe on his shoulders.)*

That is my brother's. It hangs better on him. Stand straighter.

*(He does. She steps back to see.)*

You came to my father's hall with the road still on you.

**DAVID**
I came the moment I was back.

**MICHAL**
I know. Everyone will know. Next time, wash first. Let them wait an hour for the king's son-in-law. They will respect it.

**DAVID**
*(amused)* And you? Would you have waited an hour?

**MICHAL**
No.

*(It is the only unguarded thing she has said. She hears it; lifts her chin.)*

I watched you dance on the road from Elah. Like a shepherd at a shearing.

**DAVID**
I am a shepherd.

**MICHAL**
Not any longer. That is what I am telling you.

*(She holds out her hand. He takes it. From the doorway, unseen, SAUL watches: his daughter's face.)*

---

## Scene 10 — "As the LORD Lives, He Shall Not Die"

*The hall. SAUL, JONATHAN, the servants. DAVID's place is empty.*

**SAUL**
*(to all of them)* The son of Jesse is to be put to death.

*(Nobody speaks.)*

**JONATHAN**
What has he done?

**SAUL**
You have heard me.

*(He goes. JONATHAN stands a moment, then leaves by the other door — fast.)*

*Outside. JONATHAN finds DAVID.*

**JONATHAN**
"My father Saul is seeking to kill you." He has said it before the whole house. Tomorrow do not come before him. "Stay in a secret place, and hide."

**DAVID**
And you?

**JONATHAN**
I will speak to him.

**DAVID**
He said it before the whole house, and you will speak to him.

**JONATHAN**
He is my father. He has thrown things at me since I was a boy, and the next morning walked the fields with me as if he had dreamed it. I know which hour to choose.

*(He grips DAVID's arm.)*

Hide. Where I can find you.

*Morning. A field. SAUL walking; JONATHAN beside him. The attendants fall back out of hearing.*

**JONATHAN**
The barley is early this year.

**SAUL**
You did not come out here to talk about barley.

**JONATHAN**
No.

*(He takes a breath.)*

"Let the king not sin against his servant David. He has not sinned against you. Everything he has done has been greatly to your good."

**SAUL**
To my good.

**JONATHAN**
"He took his life in his hand and struck down the Philistine, and the LORD worked a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it — and you rejoiced."

*(SAUL stops walking.)*

**SAUL**
I rejoiced.

**JONATHAN**
I was standing beside you. You wept. You took him by the shoulder, and you said: it is my turn.

**SAUL**
And since then? You have heard the roads. You have seen the men follow him out of the gate like sheep.

**JONATHAN**
They follow him because you set him over them. He goes out because you send him. Father — he brought you two hundred when you asked a hundred, and never asked why you asked.

*(SAUL looks at his son sharply. JONATHAN holds it.)*

I am not a fool. Neither is he. And he came back to your hall, and bowed.

**SAUL**
The whole house is talking about the spear.

**JONATHAN**
The whole house is talking about how he carried it back.

*(Silence.)*

I am asking you as your son. I have fought for you since I could lift a sword. I have never asked you for anything. Do not make me the son of a man who killed the innocent. "Why would you sin against innocent blood, and put David to death for nothing?"

*(SAUL looks out over the barley for a long time. Something in him comes up out of deep water.)*

**SAUL**
"As the LORD lives — he shall not be put to death."

**JONATHAN**
May I bring him to you?

*(SAUL nods. He cannot speak.)*

*The hall. JONATHAN brings DAVID in. DAVID stops at the far place. SAUL sees where he has stopped.*

**SAUL**
Come closer.

*(DAVID comes — to the near place, the old one. They look at each other.)*

I sent. And you came.

**DAVID**
You sent, my lord.

*(SAUL puts out a hand; lays it, for a moment, on the boy's head, as a father would. JONATHAN watches the two of them. He lets out his breath.)*

*(DAVID sits at the king's feet, and takes up the lyre. The phrase begins again. For a little while it is as it was.)*

---

## Scene 11 — The Window

*The hall, another night. War again; DAVID has returned from it victorious — it is in the way the servants look at him. SAUL in his seat, the spear in his hand. DAVID playing.*

*(Without a word, SAUL throws. The spear drives into the wall. DAVID is already gone — out the door, running.)*

**SAUL**
*(to his men; flat)* To his house. Watch it through the night. In the morning, kill him.

*DAVID's house. MICHAL. DAVID comes in and shuts the door behind him and stands against it.*

**MICHAL**
What has happened?

**DAVID**
He threw the spear.

**MICHAL**
Again.

**DAVID**
He did not say a word first. Before, there was always a word.

*(MICHAL goes to the window, stands to one side of it, looks down.)*

**MICHAL**
My father's men. Two at the gate. More in the lane.

*(She turns.)*

"If you do not save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be dead."

**DAVID**
He swore. To Jonathan. "As the LORD lives."

**MICHAL**
I am his daughter. I know how long his oaths hold. Until the evening.

**DAVID**
If I go out by a window in the night, I am what he says I am. A man with something to run from.

**MICHAL**
And if you stay, you are a dead man with a clear name. I would rather have a husband.

*(She is already moving: bolting the door, clearing the sill.)*

**DAVID**
Michal. If I go, I cannot come back for you. Not tomorrow. I do not know when.

**MICHAL**
I know that.

**DAVID**
They will come up in the morning, and it will be you standing here.

**MICHAL**
Yes.

**DAVID**
They will ask you where I am.

**MICHAL**
Let me answer them. I have been answering my father all my life.

*(She opens the window. Knots a cloth to the frame.)*

Now. Down.

*(DAVID looks at her. He takes her face in his hands.)*

**DAVID**
Stand straight.

**MICHAL**
*(nearly a laugh; nearly not)* Go.

*(He goes over the sill. She pays out the cloth, bracing against the wall, until the weight leaves it. She looks down into the dark; one moment only.)*

*(Then she works. She drags the household image to the bed and lays it in; a net of goat's hair where the head would be; a garment over all. She steps back. In the dim light, a man is sleeping there.)*

*(She sits down beside it, and waits for morning.)*

*Morning. Pounding at the door.*

**MESSENGER**
*(off)* Open, in the king's name! The king sends for David!

**MICHAL**
*(not rising)* "He is ill."

*(Footsteps go. Silence. Footsteps return — more of them.)*

**MESSENGER**
*(off)* The king says: "Bring him up to me in his bed."

*(The door gives. They come in, go to the bed, pull back the garment. The image. The goat's hair.)*

*The hall. MICHAL before SAUL.*

**SAUL**
"Why have you deceived me like this — and let my enemy go, so that he has escaped?"

**MICHAL**
Your enemy. He is my husband. You gave me to him with your own —

*(She sees his face: he is past hearing that. She is alone in this room, and she knows what is true of it. She changes course, without a tremor.)*

"He said to me: Let me go. Why should I kill you?"

*(SAUL looks at her. He chooses to believe it.)*

---

## Scene 12 — Naioth

*Ramah. SAMUEL — much older than at Bethlehem. DAVID arrives on foot, with nothing.*

**DAVID**
My lord.

*(SAMUEL looks at him: no robe, no sword, no lyre.)*

**SAMUEL**
Sit. Tell me.

**DAVID**
Saul is seeking my life. Jonathan spoke to him, and he swore, and I went back to him. I went out to war for him. When I came home I sat down to play, as I always do. I saw him lift the spear. Then he sent men to my house. Michal let me down from the window.

**SAMUEL**
She stayed.

**DAVID**
Yes. I have no way back to her.

*(SAMUEL lets him come to the end of it.)*

**SAMUEL**
What have you come to ask me?

**DAVID**
You told me I did not need to know everything that day. I know less now than I did then.

*(He is not accusing; he is trying to understand his orders.)*

You said: do not raise your hand against him. I have not. When he threw the spear, I carried it back to him. I have gone where he sent me. I have called him my lord and meant it. And now he wants me dead, and I may not touch him, and I may not stand still. What is left for a man to do?

**SAMUEL**
Stay alive. And do not touch him.

**DAVID**
That is not an answer.

**SAMUEL**
It is all I have.

*(Gently.)*

I am not being hard with you. I was given one sentence in Bethlehem and I spent it. I was not given this road. I think you will have to walk it without a prophet telling you where to put your feet.

**DAVID**
For how long?

**SAMUEL**
I do not know.

*(A beat.)*

I can tell you what I have seen. I have seen a man made king who could not bear to wait seven days for me at Gilgal. He was afraid the people would scatter, so he did not wait. You — wait.

**DAVID**
I am not asking for the kingdom. I am asking where to sleep tonight.

**SAMUEL**
*(rising, with difficulty)* That I can answer. Come with me.

*Naioth. A company of PROPHETS, prophesying — movement, breath, sound without words. SAMUEL stands over them. DAVID near him.*

*SAUL's hall.*

**INFORMER**
"David is at Naioth, in Ramah."

**SAUL**
Bring him.

*(A FIRST PARTY of messengers comes up the path to Naioth. Their leader sees DAVID; then the prophets.)*

**FIRST MESSENGER**
David son of Jesse, in the king's name —

*(It stops in his mouth. The spirit comes on him and on his men. They are drawn into the movement. No one lays a hand on DAVID.)*

**SAUL**
*(hall)* Where is David?

**INFORMER**
At Naioth.

**SAUL**
And my men?

**INFORMER**
Prophesying, my lord.

**SAUL**
All of them?

*(To others.)* You. Go.

*(A SECOND PARTY: the same path, the same stride. They halt a little further on. The same.)*

**SAUL**
*(to a THIRD PARTY; spacing each word)* David. Him. You will bring him to me.

*(The THIRD PARTY: the same path. A little further still. The same.)*

*(SAUL stands. He goes himself. He walks the same path, alone. At the great cistern in Secu he stops a MAN.)*

**SAUL**
"Where are Samuel and David?"

**MAN**
"At Naioth, in Ramah."

*(He goes on. While he is still walking, it comes on him too: his stride changes; he goes prophesying up the path. Before SAMUEL he strips off the royal garments — robe, belt, band — and stands in a plain shirt, like any of the young men. He falls. He lies there.)*

*(DAVID looks at the king on the ground. The king's clothes are at his feet. The king's men are scattered about the hill like sleepers. No one is between them.)*

*(DAVID takes one step toward SAUL. SAMUEL's hand on his arm.)*

**SAMUEL**
Go.

**DAVID**
He is lying in the cold.

**SAMUEL**
A door has been opened for you. Do not stand in it. Go now, while he lies there.

**DAVID**
And you?

**SAMUEL**
I will stay with him.

*(DAVID looks at the old man — who anointed them both. He bows to SAMUEL; and then, lower, to the man on the ground. He goes.)*

*(SAMUEL lowers himself, slowly, to sit beside SAUL. A day passes over them, and a night.)*

**A VOICE**
"Is Saul, too, among the prophets?"

---

## Scene 13 — Tomorrow Is the New Moon

*A field outside Gibeah. A heap of stones. DAVID comes on JONATHAN before JONATHAN has seen him.*

**DAVID**
"What have I done? What is my guilt, and what is my sin before your father, that he is seeking my life?"

**JONATHAN**
"God forbid! You shall not die."

**DAVID**
His men stood round my house all night.

**JONATHAN**
I will speak to him again. "My father does nothing, great or small, without telling me." Why would he hide this from me?

**DAVID**
Because he knows you love me. He says to himself: let Jonathan not know, or it will grieve him.

"As the LORD lives, and as you live — there is one step between me and death."

*(JONATHAN looks at him. He has not seen him afraid before.)*

**JONATHAN**
"Whatever you say, I will do for you."

**DAVID**
Tomorrow is the new moon. My place is at the king's table. I will not be in it. I will hide in this field. If he asks, say: David begged leave to run to Bethlehem; his family has its yearly sacrifice. If he says "Good" — I can come home. If he burns — you will know that he has settled on it.

*(Then, hardest:)*

"And if there is guilt in me — kill me yourself. Why bring me to your father?"

**JONATHAN**
"God forbid." If I learn that my father means you harm, would I not tell you?

**DAVID**
And if you learn it?

*(JONATHAN does not answer at once.)*

**JONATHAN**
Then I will not ask you to come back to his house.

*(They both hear what that would be. JONATHAN walks a few steps into the field.)*

Come out here. Where no one hears.

*(DAVID follows.)*

On the hill, the day you killed the Philistine. I said: "When I am king." And your face changed.

**DAVID**
Jonathan —

**JONATHAN**
I have thought about that face for a long time. Through every feast where they sang your name. Through every night my father could not sleep.

**DAVID**
I gave my word that I would not —

**JONATHAN**
Then do not. If you tell me, I shall have to carry it to my father's table and look at him over the bread. Let me say it instead.

*(He turns to face him.)*

"The LORD be with you, as He was with my father."

*(DAVID cannot speak.)*

That is all. I have said it, and I am still standing. I thought it would be harder.

*(A breath. It was hard.)*

Now hear what I ask. "If I am still alive — show me the kindness of the LORD, that I may not die. And do not cut off your kindness from my house, ever. Not even when the LORD has cut off every one of David's enemies from the face of the earth."

**DAVID**
I will not cut it off.

**JONATHAN**
Swear it.

**DAVID**
By the LORD. To you, and to your house after you.

**JONATHAN**
You said that on the hill. I thought you were being courteous.

*(He nearly smiles. Then, practical — a soldier laying out a signal.)*

On the third day, come down to this place and stay behind the stone. I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as if at a mark, and send the boy for them. If I call to him, "The arrows are on this side of you — bring them," then come: it is peace. If I say, "The arrows are beyond you" — go. The LORD has sent you away.

"And for the word we have spoken, you and I — the LORD is between me and you forever."

*The king's table. New moon. SAUL in his seat by the wall; ABNER at his side; JONATHAN. DAVID's place — empty. SAUL looks at it, and says nothing.*

**SAUL**
*(to himself)* Something has befallen him. He is not clean. Surely he is not clean.

*The second day. The same table. The same empty place.*

**SAUL**
"Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal — not yesterday, not today?"

**JONATHAN**
He begged leave of me to go to Bethlehem. His family has a sacrifice in the town; his brother summoned him. He said: let me slip away and see my brothers. So he has not come to the king's table.

*(SAUL puts down his cup.)*

**SAUL**
"You son of a perverse, rebellious woman!"

*(The table freezes.)*

"Do I not know that you have chosen the son of Jesse — to your own shame, and the shame of your mother who bore you?"

**JONATHAN**
Father —

**SAUL**
"For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, you will not stand — not you, and not your kingdom."

*(He is on his feet. And under the rage, for those who can hear it: he is pleading.)*

Do you hear me? *Your* kingdom. Not mine. I am an old man; I have had mine. It is your seat he is sitting in. I am doing this for *you* — and you carry his messages to my table!

"Now send, and bring him to me. He is a dead man."

**JONATHAN**
"Why should he die? What has he done?"

*(SAUL seizes the spear and throws it — at his son. JONATHAN twists aside. It stands in the wall.)*

*(Silence. ABNER has half risen. SAUL is looking at his own hand.)*

*(JONATHAN rises from the table. He has not touched the bread. He looks at his father — grief, not anger, though it is burning. He walks out.)*

*Morning. The field. DAVID behind the stones. JONATHAN comes with his bow, and a small BOY.*

**JONATHAN**
"Run. Find the arrows I am going to shoot."

*(The BOY runs. JONATHAN shoots — far beyond him. He calls. His voice is steady because he makes it so.)*

"Is the arrow not beyond you?"

*(Behind the stones, DAVID closes his eyes.)*

"Quick! Hurry! Do not stand there!"

*(The BOY gathers the arrows and brings them, knowing nothing. JONATHAN gives him the bow and quiver.)*

Take these back to the town.

*(The BOY goes. When he is out of sight, DAVID comes out from behind the stones. He falls with his face to the ground and bows, three times. JONATHAN pulls him up. They hold each other and weep. JONATHAN is the first to try to let go. DAVID cannot.)*

**DAVID**
Then I cannot come back.

*(JONATHAN shakes his head.)*

Come with me.

**JONATHAN**
No.

**DAVID**
He threw a spear at you.

**JONATHAN**
He is my father. He will go out to war again, and when he does I will be at his side. That is where I belong. It was never one or the other, David. It is both — and I will carry both.

*(He holds him off at arm's length, to look at him.)*

"Go in peace. For we have sworn, the two of us, in the name of the LORD: the LORD shall be between me and you, and between my children and your children, forever."

*(DAVID goes. JONATHAN stays where he is until he can no longer see him. Then he turns, and walks back toward the town.)*

---

## Scene 14 — Nob

*Nob, the town of the priests. The tent of the LORD. PRIESTS at their work: one trims a lamp; two carry out a table of bread and carry in another. DAVID stands at the edge, alone, hungry, watching — a man looking at the one place he ever wanted to be.*

*AHIMELECH the priest sees him and comes quickly. He is alarmed.*

**AHIMELECH**
"Why are you alone, and no one with you?"

*(DAVID has to decide what he is going to be in this place. He decides.)*

**DAVID**
"The king has charged me with a matter." He said: let no one know anything of the errand on which I send you. My young men I have appointed to meet me at a certain place.

*(It comes out smoothly. He hates that it does.)*

"Now — what have you at hand? Give me five loaves of bread. Or whatever there is."

**AHIMELECH**
The king's son-in-law, on the king's business, without a crust in his bag.

*(He believes it; it only worries him.)*

"There is no common bread at hand. There is only holy bread." The bread of the Presence — it was taken from before the LORD this morning, when the fresh was set down. If your young men have at least kept themselves from women —

**DAVID**
"Women have been kept from us, as always when I go out."

*(AHIMELECH brings the bread, wrapped. He puts it into DAVID's hands himself.)*

**AHIMELECH**
You will stay the night. You are grey with the road.

**DAVID**
I cannot.

**AHIMELECH**
One night. You may sit at the door of the tent. I have heard that you play.

*(DAVID looks at the tent; the lamp; the door at which he might sit.)*

**DAVID**
There is nothing in the world I should like better.

*(He sees, then, a man standing apart by the wall: DOEG the Edomite, chief of Saul's herdsmen, detained here before the LORD. DOEG is looking at him. Their eyes meet. DOEG says nothing at all.)*

**DAVID**
*(turning back; quickly now)* Is there a spear here, or a sword, under your hand? "I brought neither my sword nor my weapons with me. The king's business was urgent."

**AHIMELECH**
"The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of Elah — it is here, wrapped in a cloth, behind the ephod. If you will take that, take it. There is no other here."

*(He uncovers it. DAVID lays his hand on the blade — the day at Elah, whole, in his palm.)*

**DAVID**
"There is none like it. Give it to me."

*(AHIMELECH gives it. DAVID stands with the bread of the LORD in one hand and the giant's sword in the other.)*

**AHIMELECH**
The LORD prosper the king's errand.

*(DAVID cannot answer that. He bows — deeply, to the priest — and goes. DOEG's eyes follow the bread and the sword out of sight. AHIMELECH goes back to his lamps.)*

---

## Scene 15 — Gath

*The gate of Gath — Goliath's town. DAVID comes in off the road, hooded, the long sword wrapped on his back. The SERVANTS OF ACHISH are on him almost at once. One pulls back the hood. One finds the sword.*

**FIRST SERVANT**
It is he.

**SECOND SERVANT**
*(the sword)* And look what he carries into Gath.

**FIRST SERVANT**
"Is this not David, the king of the land? Is this not the one they sing to in their dances —"

**SERVANTS**
*(mocking; they know the tune)*
"Saul has struck down his thousands,
and David his ten thousands!"

*(The song that made him, in his enemies' mouths. DAVID looks for the way out. There is none. He is very much afraid. They haul him before ACHISH, king of Gath.)*

**FIRST SERVANT**
Speak. The king is before you.

*(DAVID looks at ACHISH. Something leaves his eyes. His jaw goes slack. He turns away from the king as if the king were not there, goes to the doors of the gate, and begins to scratch marks on them with his nails — carefully, absorbed. A servant takes his shoulder; he slides out from under the hand and goes back to the door. Spittle runs down into his beard.)*

*(It is not comic. It is exact.)*

**ACHISH**
"Look at him. The man is mad. Why do you bring him to me?"

**FIRST SERVANT**
My lord, this is the man who —

**ACHISH**
"Am I short of madmen, that you have brought this one to rave in front of me? Is this to come into my house?"

*(They throw him out of the gate. DAVID goes on — the dragging foot, the scrabbling hand — down the road, until the gate is small behind him. Only then does it begin to leave him: the hands first; then the back; then the face. He wipes his beard on his sleeve. He stands in the road getting his breath.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 34.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, alone on the road outside Gath.
**Addressed to:** God — and then to anyone poor enough to need it.
**Physical action:** his body comes back under his own command. He sits; looks at his hands; gets up; gathers his things.
**Why song, here:** it starts as broken speech, the pulse still racing; the tune arrives only as the breathing steadies.
**What changes:** no orchestra announces a victory. He got out alive. That is the whole of it — and he blesses.]**

**DAVID**
*(spoken, in pieces)*
"I sought the LORD, and He answered me,
and delivered me from all my terrors."

*(He sits down at the side of the road. He looks at his hands — the nails that scratched the door.)*

"This poor man called, and the LORD heard,
and saved him out of all his troubles."

*(The breath is steady now. The line finds its tune.)*

*(sung)*
"I will bless the LORD at all times;
His praise shall be always in my mouth."

*(He stands. Takes up the sword.)*

"My soul shall glory in the LORD;
the humble shall hear it, and be glad."

*(He goes.)*

## Scene 16 — Adullam

*The cave of Adullam. DAVID alone at its mouth. Up the path comes his FATHER'S HOUSE — JESSE, DAVID'S MOTHER, ELIAB and the brothers, carrying what could be carried.*

**DAVID**
Come in. Come in out of the light. Is this everyone?

**ELIAB**
Everyone.

*(DAVID takes the bundle from his father's back. He makes a place for his parents against the wall.)*

**JESSE**
The king's men came asking at the gate. We did not wait for them to ask twice.

*(ELIAB and DAVID are left facing each other.)*

**ELIAB**
I said to you on the ridge: if the king looks at you twice.

**DAVID**
You did.

**ELIAB**
I am not saying it to be right.

*(It is the hardest thing he has ever said.)*

I am saying: tell me where you want me to stand.

*(DAVID looks at his eldest brother. Then he points.)*

**DAVID**
The mouth of the cave. You have a soldier's eye. Nobody comes up that path without your seeing him first.

*(ELIAB nods, and goes to stand there. Up the path: ZERUIAH, and behind her three young men — JOAB, ABISHAI, ASAHEL.)*

**ZERUIAH**
I have brought you my sons. They eat a great deal. They do not frighten.

**JOAB**
*(already looking the cave over)* One way in. Good for holding; bad for leaving. Is there water?

**DAVID**
A seep at the back. Not enough.

**JOAB**
Then that is the first thing.

**ABISHAI**
The first thing is: when do we go and get him?

**DAVID**
Get whom?

**ABISHAI**
Saul.

**DAVID**
We do not.

*(ABISHAI stares. ASAHEL — the youngest, light on his feet — grins.)*

**ASAHEL**
I told him you would say that. Uncle, I can outrun anything on these hills. Send me for the water.

**DAVID**
Take a skin. Take two. Come back before dark.

*(ASAHEL is gone. Others are coming up the path now: ragged, wary — the DEBTOR; the BITTER MAN; more behind them.)*

**DEBTOR**
Is David son of Jesse here?

**DAVID**
What do you want of him?

**DEBTOR**
A place to stay.

**DAVID**
Who is behind you?

**DEBTOR**
The man I owe. He has my ox and my cloak. He will have my sons next.

**DAVID**
Come in. Sit.

**BITTER MAN**
*(not sitting)* I had a field. The king's officer wanted a field. Now the king's officer has one. They say you are the one Saul is afraid of.

**DAVID**
They say many things.

**BITTER MAN**
Are you going to fight him?

**DAVID**
No.

*(A murmur among those arriving.)*

**BITTER MAN**
Then what is this? What are we all climbing up here for?

*(DAVID looks at them: the men in distress, the men in debt, the men bitter of soul. He does not make a speech. He answers the question.)*

**DAVID**
To stay alive. To keep one another alive. There are Philistine raiders in these hills and nobody is coming down from Gibeah to stop them; so we will. I cannot give you back your field. I can give you a place at the fire, and a share of whatever there is, and my word that I will not send you anywhere I do not go first.

**BITTER MAN**
And Saul?

**DAVID**
Saul is the king of Israel. If that is not what you came to hear, the path goes down as well as up.

*(Nobody goes down. The BITTER MAN, slowly, sits.)*

**A MAN**
There is not enough water for all of these.

**DAVID**
How much is left? — A boy has gone for more. Until he is back, share it out. Children first; then the old; then the rest of us.

*(JESSE, by the wall, looks up at that.)*

**ANOTHER**
And who watches the road?

**DAVID**
My brother has the mouth of the cave. Two more below him, where the path turns. I will relieve you before night.

*(They move to do it. Without anyone having said so, he is their captain. About four hundred men.)*

*(DAVID goes to his parents.)*

**DAVID**
You cannot stay here. This is no place for —

**JESSE**
For the old. Say it. You fed them second, as I recall.

*(DAVID is caught; he laughs. So, almost, does JESSE.)*

**DAVID**
I will take you across to Moab. The king there will not turn away the house of Ruth.

**JESSE**
Moab.

*(Dry.)*

My grandmother walked out of it. I am to walk back in.

*Mizpeh of Moab. DAVID, his parents behind him, before the KING OF MOAB.*

**DAVID**
"Let my father and my mother come and stay with you — until I know what God will do for me."

*(The KING OF MOAB inclines his head. DAVID turns to them. JESSE grips his arm; it is not a thing he does easily. DAVID'S MOTHER takes her son's face in her hands.)*

**DAVID'S MOTHER**
David.

*(That is all she says. He hears every morning of it. They go in.)*

*The stronghold. GAD the prophet comes to DAVID.*

**GAD**
"Do not stay in the stronghold. Go — get yourself into the land of Judah."

*(DAVID does not ask why.)*

**DAVID**
*(to the men)* Gather what you have. We are going back into Judah.

## Scene 17 — Abiathar

*Gibeah. SAUL sits under the tamarisk on the height, the spear in his hand, his servants standing round him — men of his own tribe.*

**SAUL**
"Hear me, men of Benjamin. Will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards? Will he make you all captains of thousands and captains of hundreds?"

*(No one answers.)*

"For all of you have conspired against me. No one tells me when my own son makes a covenant with the son of Jesse. Not one of you is sorry for me."

*(A silence in which men look at their feet. Then DOEG steps forward.)*

**DOEG**
"I saw the son of Jesse come to Nob, to Ahimelech son of Ahitub. He inquired of the LORD for him. He gave him provisions. And he gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine."

*(AHIMELECH and the PRIESTS of his father's house are brought — linen ephods, empty hands.)*

**SAUL**
"Why have you conspired against me, you and the son of Jesse? You gave him bread and a sword, and inquired of God for him, so that he might rise against me and lie in wait — as he does this day."

**AHIMELECH**
*(astonished; and then, simply, truthful)*
"And who among all your servants is as faithful as David? The king's son-in-law. Captain of your guard. Honored in your house."

*(SAUL's face. AHIMELECH does not see the danger; he sees only that the king has been misinformed.)*

"Was that the first day I inquired of God for him? God forbid. Let the king lay nothing to his servant's charge, or to all my father's house. Your servant knew nothing of all this — nothing, small or great."

**SAUL**
"You shall die, Ahimelech. You, and all your father's house."

*(To the RUNNERS at his side.)*

Turn and kill the priests of the LORD. Their hand is with David. They knew he was running, and did not tell me.

*(The RUNNERS do not move. Not one hand goes to a sword. It is the only mutiny of SAUL's reign, and it is silent.)*

**SAUL**
*(to DOEG)* "You. Turn, and fall upon the priests."

*(DOEG draws, and walks toward the white linen. The light leaves before he arrives.)*

*DAVID's camp. A young man comes in running, falls, is caught: ABIATHAR. He is clutching something to his chest — an ephod. Around the fire: DAVID, ABISHAI, the BITTER MAN, others.*

**DAVID**
Who are you?

**ABIATHAR**
Abiathar. Son of Ahimelech. Of Nob.

*(DAVID has gone very still.)*

The king sent for my father and all the priests. Doeg killed them. Eighty-five men. Then Nob — he put Nob to the sword. The women. The children. The infants. The oxen and the donkeys.

**DAVID**
Your father.

*(ABIATHAR shakes his head. Someone brings water. He cannot hold it.)*

**ABIATHAR**
I was with the flock on the far hill. I took this from the tent, and I ran.

*(DAVID lowers himself to sit on the ground, facing him.)*

**DAVID**
"I knew it that day. Doeg the Edomite was there, and I knew that he would surely tell Saul."

*(He does not look away.)*

"I am the cause of every death in your father's house."

**ABIATHAR**
You —

**DAVID**
Your father asked me why I was alone. I told him the king had sent me. I lied to him, in that place, and he believed me, and he gave me bread from the LORD's table with his own hands.

*(ABIATHAR looks at him. It might be hatred.)*

**ABIATHAR**
The king asked him why he had helped you. Do you know what he said? He said: "Who among all your servants is as faithful as David?" It was nearly the last thing he said.

*(DAVID takes it. He does not defend himself.)*

**ABISHAI**
*(on his feet)* Now. Now will you let us?

**BITTER MAN**
Eighty-five priests.

**ABISHAI**
He has murdered the LORD's priests in the LORD's linen. What is left to wait for?

**DAVID**
*(not rising)* Doeg did it. Saul commanded it. And I began it. Which of the three would you like to put your sword through first?

*(ABISHAI has no answer. He sits down, hard.)*

*(DAVID, to ABIATHAR:)*

"Stay with me. Do not be afraid. The one who seeks my life seeks yours. With me you are in safekeeping."

*(ABIATHAR does not answer. He does not leave. After a time, he takes the water.)*

*(DAVID's eyes go to the sword of Goliath, lying where he set it down — AHIMELECH's gift. He speaks to it.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 52.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, spoken, to the sword.
**Addressed to:** the "mighty man" — the informer; then, at the turn, to God, with ABIATHAR in his sight.
**Physical action:** at "a green olive tree" he turns his body from the sword to the survivor.
**What changes:** from indictment to the choice to shelter the one who is left. If set to music, the setting must show that turn, not only the denunciation.]**

**DAVID**
"Why do you boast of evil, mighty man?
The kindness of God lasts all the day.
Your tongue plots ruin;
like a sharpened razor, worker of deceit.
You love evil more than good,
a lie more than speaking what is right."

*(He turns from the sword to ABIATHAR.)*

"But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God.
I trust in the kindness of God, forever and ever.
I will thank You forever, for what You have done;
and I will wait on Your name — for it is good —
in the presence of those who love You."

*(ABIATHAR, the ephod in his lap, at DAVID's fire.)*

## Scene 18 — The Cave

*The wilderness of En-gedi. The back of a deep cave: DAVID and his men pressed into the dark — ABISHAI, the BITTER MAN, the DEBTOR, ABIATHAR, others. A lookout creeps back from the mouth.*

**LOOKOUT**
*(a breath)* Saul's men. Three thousand. On the goat-rocks.

*(They shrink further in. At the mouth of the cave a tall figure appears against the light — alone. SAUL. He comes a little way in, lays his robe aside on a rock, and goes to relieve himself, his back to the dark. He has no idea.)*

*(In the dark, everything is whispered. It is no less fierce for that.)*

**BITTER MAN**
"This is the day of which the LORD said to you: I am giving your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as seems good to you."

**ABISHAI**
There is no one with him. No one.

**DEBTOR**
One stroke. And we go home.

*(DAVID draws his sword. He goes forward, soundless. He stands over the robe — over SAUL's turned back, an arm's length away. The blade comes down — and cuts the corner from the robe. He comes back with the cloth in his hand.)*

*(He looks at it. His own heart strikes him.)*

**BITTER MAN**
Why have you come back? He is still there.

*(ABISHAI is already rising. DAVID's hand on his chest.)*

**DAVID**
"The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, to the LORD's anointed — to put out my hand against him. He is the anointed of the LORD."

**ABISHAI**
He is hunting you.

**DAVID**
I know.

**ABISHAI**
My mother has not slept under a roof in two years. Asahel has grown his beard in caves. That man —

*(He points with his whole arm.)*

— that man had eighty-five priests cut down for a loaf of bread. Ask the priest whether he is the anointed of the LORD.

*(They all look at ABIATHAR. ABIATHAR looks at the back of the king. He says nothing. It is worse than if he spoke.)*

**DEBTOR**
You are anointed too. We know it; all Judah knows it. One stroke — and Israel has one king, and no war.

**DAVID**
And how did he become king?

**DEBTOR**
What?

**DAVID**
The one king. When they ask it in the gate. How did the son of Jesse come by the seat?

*(No one answers.)*

With a knife. In a cave. While the old king had his robe off. And then every man in Israel who has a knife and knows of a cave is a king. I should sit in that seat for the rest of my days, watching you.

**BITTER MAN**
We would never —

**DAVID**
You are asking me to, now.

*(A beat.)*

And Benjamin. Do you think Benjamin follows the man who butchered their kinsman? You would not have one king. You would have Judah burying Benjamin and Benjamin burying Judah for a generation, and the Philistines sitting in Gath, patient, waiting to see what is left.

**ABISHAI**
So we go on running.

**DAVID**
I do not promise you that the hunt ends today.

**BITTER MAN**
Then what can you promise us?

**DAVID**
That we will not become the hunters.

*(One man lets his hand fall from his sword. Another keeps his where it is. They are not all persuaded. DAVID sees that.)*

You will not rise against him.

*(It is an order. ABISHAI sits back on his heels.)*

*(SAUL takes up his robe, puts it on without looking at it, and goes out into the light. DAVID waits; then follows, to the mouth of the cave, into the sun.)*

**DAVID**
"My lord the king!"

*(SAUL turns. DAVID bows, his face to the ground.)*

"Why do you listen to the words of men who say: David is seeking your harm? Today your own eyes have seen it. The LORD gave you into my hand in the cave. They told me to kill you. And I spared you. I said: I will not put out my hand against my lord, for he is the LORD's anointed."

*(He holds up the cloth.)*

"And see, my father — look. See the corner of your robe in my hand."

*(SAUL's hand goes to the hem. Finds the place.)*

"I cut the corner from your robe, and I did not kill you. Know, and see: there is no evil in my hand, and no rebellion. I have not sinned against you — and you are hunting my life, to take it."

"After whom has the king of Israel come out? Whom are you pursuing? A dead dog. A single flea."

"The LORD shall judge between me and you. The LORD may avenge me upon you. But my hand shall not be against you."

*(Silence. The wind on the rocks.)*

**SAUL**
"Is that your voice — my son David?"

*(He lifts up his voice, and weeps.)*

"You are more righteous than I. For you have repaid me good, and I have repaid you evil. When a man finds his enemy, does he send him safely on his way?"

*(He comes a step nearer.)*

I asked you once. In my hall, in the night. I asked you where he was standing.

**DAVID**
I remember.

**SAUL**
You said you would play until it was light.

**DAVID**
I played until it was light, my lord.

*(SAUL nods slowly. He has his answer; he has had it for years.)*

**SAUL**
"And now — I know that you will surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel will stand firm in your hand."

*(It is said. The men at the cave's mouth hear it.)*

"Swear to me, then, by the LORD: that you will not cut off my seed after me. That you will not wipe out my name from my father's house."

**DAVID**
I have sworn it already. To your son. I swear it to you.

*(SAUL looks at him a last moment — and turns, and goes down toward his three thousand. He does not say "Come home." DAVID does not ask.)*

*(DAVID stands in the mouth of the cave with the corner of the robe in his hand.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: PSALM 54 — here, at the END of the cave, and not before.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, alone in the cave's mouth.
**Addressed to:** God.
**Physical action:** his men file out past him and wait. He walks behind them, up toward the stronghold. He folds the cloth and keeps it.
**Why song, here:** only after the decision has been made and has cost what it costs does prayer come back to him.
**What changes:** nothing outside him. They are still fugitives. He gives thanks anyway.]**

**DAVID**
"God, by Your name, save me;
by Your might, judge my cause.
God, hear my prayer;
give ear to the words of my mouth."

*(The men come out, one by one. ABISHAI last. He looks at the cloth. He looks at DAVID. He goes on.)*

"See — God is my helper;
the LORD is with those who hold up my life."

*(He folds the cloth.)*

"Freely I will sacrifice to You;
I will thank Your name, LORD, for it is good.
For He has delivered me out of every trouble."

*(They climb toward the stronghold.)*

## Scene 19 — Nabal and Abigail

*DAVID's camp. A RUNNER.*

**RUNNER**
Samuel is dead.

*(DAVID sits down where he is. The camp goes quiet around him.)*

**A VOICE**
"And Samuel died. And all Israel gathered and mourned for him, and they buried him at his home in Ramah."

*(DAVID cannot go to Ramah. He sits facing that way. After a while:)*

**DAVID**
He told me I should have to walk it without a prophet.

*(He gets up.)*

*The wilderness of Paran, near Carmel. Shearing time: far off, the noise of a feast. DAVID with ten YOUNG MEN.*

**DAVID**
Go up to Carmel, to Nabal. He is shearing three thousand sheep, and there is not one missing, because all winter we stood between his shepherds and the raiders and took nothing. Greet him in my name. "Say: Long life to you. Peace to you, and peace to your house, and peace to all that is yours." It is a feast day. "Give, please, whatever comes to your hand — to your servants, and to your son David."

*(The YOUNG MEN go.)*

*NABAL's shearing-feast. NABAL — rich, loud, his cup in his hand. The YOUNG MEN deliver it, and wait.*

**NABAL**
"Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse? There are a great many servants nowadays breaking away from their masters."

*(Laughter at his table.)*

"Shall I take my bread, and my water, and my meat that I have slaughtered for my shearers — and give it to men who come from I do not know where?"

*DAVID's camp.*

**DAVID**
What did he give?

**YOUNG MAN**
Nothing.

**DAVID**
What did he say?

**YOUNG MAN**
*(reluctant)* "Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse." He said there are many servants nowadays who break away from their masters.

*(A long silence. It is the thing Saul's court says. It is the thing he will not let himself be. DAVID's face has changed in a way his men have not seen.)*

**DAVID**
"Every man: gird on his sword."

*(They do. DAVID girds on his own. ABISHAI is beside him at once, alight.)*

**ABISHAI**
Now *this* I understand.

*(Four hundred go; two hundred stay with the baggage.)*

*NABAL's house. A SHEPHERD runs to ABIGAIL.*

**SHEPHERD**
Mistress. David sent messengers out of the wilderness to greet our master, and he flew at them. And those men were very good to us. We lost nothing, all the time we were in the fields. "They were a wall around us, by night and by day." Now think what you will do — for harm is settled on our master and on all his house. And he is such a brute that no one can speak to him.

*(ABIGAIL does not waste one breath on dismay.)*

**ABIGAIL**
How many are with him?

**SHEPHERD**
Hundreds.

**ABIGAIL**
How long?

**SHEPHERD**
They will be on the mountain road by now.

**ABIGAIL**
Two hundred loaves. Two skins of wine. The five sheep that are dressed. Five measures of roasted grain. A hundred clusters of raisins, two hundred cakes of figs. On the donkeys — now.

*(Servants run.)*

Go ahead of me. I am coming behind you.

**SHEPHERD**
And the master?

**ABIGAIL**
Tell him nothing.

*The mountain road, a covert of the hill. DAVID coming down at the head of four hundred armed men.*

**DAVID**
"For nothing I guarded everything this man has in the wilderness. Not one thing of his went missing. And he has paid me evil for good."

"God do so to me, and more — if by morning I leave alive one male of all who are his."

**A MAN**
There is a train of donkeys on the road ahead. Do we go round?

**DAVID**
Keep to the road.

*(The donkeys. Then ABIGAIL, riding. She sees the blades — none of them is sheathed. She gets down quickly, and comes through the armed men, and falls at DAVID's feet with her face to the ground.)*

**ABIGAIL**
"On me, my lord — on me be the guilt. Let your servant speak in your hearing. And hear the words of your servant."

**DAVID**
Who are you?

**ABIGAIL**
Abigail. Nabal's wife.

*(His hand tightens on the hilt. She goes on, quickly — she has perhaps thirty breaths.)*

"Let my lord pay no mind to this worthless man, Nabal. He is what his name is. Fool is his name, and folly goes with him. And I, your servant — I did not see the young men my lord sent."

**DAVID**
I do not make war on women. Stand aside.

**ABIGAIL**
I know you do not. That is why I am standing here.

*(She gestures: the donkeys.)*

This is a gift your servant has brought to my lord. Let it be given to the young men who walk behind you. They are hungry. I can see it from here.

*(The men look at the bread. DAVID does not.)*

**DAVID**
He called me a runaway slave.

**ABIGAIL**
He did. And tomorrow, when he is sober, he will not remember that he said it. Will you remember that you killed for it?

*(That reaches him. She sees it reach him, and changes her ground: she is not pleading now; she is telling him who he is.)*

"The LORD will surely make my lord a lasting house — for my lord is fighting the battles of the LORD, and no evil has been found in you all your days."

They tell a thing about you in Carmel. That a king stood in a cave with his back to you, and you cut his robe, and would not cut him.

*(ABISHAI looks away.)*

Is it true?

**DAVID**
It is true.

**ABIGAIL**
Then will you spend on a fool what you would not spend on Saul?

*(Silence on the road.)*

"If a man rises to pursue you and seek your life, the life of my lord shall be bound up in the bundle of the living with the LORD your God. And the lives of your enemies He shall sling out, as from the hollow of a sling."

*(The sling. He hears it.)*

"And when the LORD has done for my lord all the good He has spoken of you, and has appointed you prince over Israel — let this not be a stumbling for you, or a grief of heart to my lord: to have shed blood without cause. For my lord to have saved himself with his own hand."

*(She has said it. She bows her head.)*

"And when the LORD has dealt well with my lord — remember your servant."

*(DAVID's hand comes off the hilt. Four hundred men see it.)*

**DAVID**
"Blessed is the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you this day to meet me. And blessed is your good sense. And blessed are you — who have kept me this day from coming into blood, and from saving myself with my own hand."

*(He lets out a breath that is nearly a laugh — at himself.)*

As the LORD lives: if you had not hurried, by the light of morning there would not have been one left to Nabal.

*(To the men.)*

Take what she has brought.

*(They sheathe their swords to carry bread. ABISHAI, last and slowest.)*

"Go up in peace to your house. See — I have listened to your voice. I have granted your request."

*(ABIGAIL rises, and goes. DAVID watches her out of sight.)*

## Scene 20 — Until the Morning Light

*NABAL's house. The feast — "like the feast of a king." NABAL very drunk, very merry. ABIGAIL comes in from the road. She looks at him. She says nothing, small or great, until the morning light.*

*Morning. NABAL, grey, the wine gone out of him. ABIGAIL standing.*

**ABIGAIL**
Yesterday David was on the road to this house. Four hundred men, with their swords drawn. I went out to him with bread. He listened to me, and turned back.

*(NABAL does not move. His heart dies within him. He becomes like a stone.)*

*(The house takes up its work again around a man who sits and does not stir. About ten days. Then they carry him out.)*

*DAVID's camp. The news.*

**DAVID**
"Blessed is the LORD, who has pleaded my cause against the insult I had from Nabal — and has held back His servant from evil."

*(He is quiet a moment.)*

I was an hour from it.

*(To his servants.)*

Go to Carmel. To Abigail.

*NABAL's house — hers now. DAVID's SERVANTS before ABIGAIL.*

**SERVANT**
David has sent us to you, to take you to him as his wife.

*(ABIGAIL rises. She bows, her face to the ground.)*

**ABIGAIL**
"See — your handmaid is a servant, to wash the feet of my lord's servants."

*(She gets up, and does not wait to be led: she lifts her own bundle. Five young women follow her.)*

*DAVID's camp. ABIGAIL arrives. A woman meets her at the edge of the firelight with a jar: AHINOAM of Jezreel — DAVID's wife, plain-spoken, a woman of the valley.*

**AHINOAM**
You will be the one from Carmel.

**ABIGAIL**
Abigail.

**AHINOAM**
Ahinoam. Of Jezreel. There is water. In this camp everyone carries their own; I thought, the first night, I would carry yours.

*(ABIGAIL takes it as it is meant.)*

**ABIGAIL**
Thank you.

**AHINOAM**
He is over there. He has had news from Gibeah, and he will not want anyone. I should go to him anyway, if I were you.

*(ABIGAIL goes to DAVID. He is sitting apart.)*

**ABIGAIL**
My lord.

**DAVID**
You came.

**ABIGAIL**
You sent.

*(She waits.)*

What is the news from Gibeah?

**DAVID**
Saul has given his daughter Michal to another man. Palti, son of Laish.

*(ABIGAIL takes that in without flinching.)*

She let me down from a window on a knotted cloth. She stayed in the room, to answer for it.

**ABIGAIL**
Then she is someone I should like to have known.

*(DAVID looks up at her. She sits down — not close; near.)*

## Scene 21 — "Once — I Will Not Strike Twice"

*Night. The hill of Hachilah. Below, SAUL's camp: three thousand men asleep in their circles; the king in the midst. DAVID on the height, looking down, with ABISHAI and AHIMELECH the Hittite.*

**DAVID**
He wept. At En-gedi. He called me his son. He said the kingdom would stand in my hand.

**ABISHAI**
And there he is. With three thousand.

*(DAVID says nothing. He is counting fires.)*

**DAVID**
"Who will go down with me to Saul, into the camp?"

**ABISHAI**
"I will go down with you."

*(DAVID looks at him — measuring exactly what he is taking with him.)*

**DAVID**
Come, then. My sister's son. Quietly.

*(They go down. They walk among the sleepers; no one stirs — a deep sleep lies on the whole camp. In the center: SAUL, asleep. His spear stands driven into the ground at his head. A jar of water beside it. ABNER sleeps an arm's length away.)*

*(They stand over the king. He is older than at the cave. He sleeps badly, even now.)*

**ABISHAI**
*(a whisper; reverent, almost)* "God has shut your enemy into your hand today."

*(He lays hold of the king's own spear.)*

"Let me pin him to the earth with the spear. Once. I will not strike him twice."

**DAVID**
"Do not destroy him. Who can put out his hand against the LORD's anointed, and be innocent?"

**ABISHAI**
You said that in the cave.

**DAVID**
I did.

**ABISHAI**
In the cave you did not know what it would buy. Now you know. It bought this.

*(His arm takes in the three thousand.)*

You spared him, and he wept, and he came again. How many times is a man allowed to weep?

**DAVID**
I do not know.

**ABISHAI**
Uncle. Listen to me. I am not the men in the cave. I do not want a field. I am your blood. I have watched you carry six hundred people from rock to rock for years, and I have never once seen you sleep the night through. Let me do it. Your hand will not be on him. Mine will. Let it be on me — the guilt, the name of it, all of it. You can weep for him afterward. You can tear your clothes and curse me in front of all Israel; I will stand there and be cursed. Only let it end.

*(It is love. DAVID knows that it is. He puts himself between ABISHAI and the sleeping man.)*

**DAVID**
And when I have cursed you in front of all Israel — who will believe me?

*(ABISHAI is silent.)*

If your hand does it, it is my hand. You are my sister's son; you came down this hill at my side. There is no one in Israel who would not know whose spear that was.

*(He looks down at SAUL.)*

In the cave, I was afraid of what I should become. I am not afraid of that tonight. Tonight I am afraid of what they would become.

*(The sleepers; the tribes.)*

That is Benjamin, lying there. Tomorrow they wake, and their king is nailed to the ground, and they come up this hill for us. And the day after, Judah comes for them. And it does not stop, Abishai. Brothers do not stop. A stranger you can beat and send home. A brother has nowhere to go.

**ABISHAI**
So he goes on living. And we go on —

**DAVID**
"As the LORD lives: the LORD Himself will strike him. Or his day will come, and he will die. Or he will go down into battle, and be swept away."

**ABISHAI**
When?

**DAVID**
I do not know. I have stopped asking when.

*(This is what is new, and what it has cost.)*

It may be that I grow old on these rocks. It may be that there is never any house, for me or for you. I have looked at that. I am telling you I have looked at it.

"The LORD forbid that I should put out my hand against the LORD's anointed."

*(He draws the spear out of the ground himself — the spear that was thrown at him three times; that was thrown at Jonathan; that he once took from this same sleeping hand, and once carried back. He holds it.)*

Take the jar of water. We are going.

*(ABISHAI looks at SAUL. He looks at DAVID. He bends, and takes the jar. They go up through the sleeping army, and no one sees, and no one knows, and no one wakes.)*

*The far hilltop. A wide space between. DAVID stands with the spear, and shouts.*

**DAVID**
"Abner! Will you not answer, Abner?"

*(Below: confusion; torches.)*

**ABNER**
"Who are you, that calls out to the king?"

**DAVID**
"Are you not a man? Who is like you in Israel? Then why did you not guard your lord the king? One of the people came in to destroy the king, your lord. It is not good, this thing you have done."

"Look — where is the king's spear? And the jar of water that was at his head?"

*(ABNER looks. The hole in the ground. SAUL is on his feet.)*

**SAUL**
"Is that your voice — my son David?"

**DAVID**
"It is my voice, my lord the king."

"Why does my lord pursue his servant? What have I done? What evil is in my hand?"

*(And now what he could not say at the cave — what the years have added.)*

"If it is the LORD who has stirred you against me, let Him accept an offering. But if it is men — cursed are they before the LORD. For they have driven me out this day from any share in the inheritance of the LORD. They say to me: Go. Serve other gods."

*(It is the worst thing that has been done to him, and he has never said it aloud.)*

"Do not let my blood fall to the earth far from the presence of the LORD. For the king of Israel has come out to hunt a single flea — as one chases a partridge in the mountains."

**SAUL**
"I have sinned."

*(Across the dark.)*

"Come back, my son David. I will do you no more harm — because my life was precious in your eyes this day. I have been a fool. I have erred, very greatly."

*(DAVID hears "Come back." He does not answer it. He holds up the spear.)*

**DAVID**
"Here is the king's spear. Let one of the young men cross over, and take it."

"The LORD will repay each man his righteousness and his faithfulness. The LORD gave you into my hand today, and I would not put out my hand against the LORD's anointed. As your life was great in my eyes this day — so may my life be great in the eyes of the LORD. May He deliver me out of every trouble."

*(A YOUNG MAN crosses the space. DAVID puts the spear into his hands. It goes back down the hill to SAUL.)*

**SAUL**
"Blessed are you, my son David. You will do great things. And you will surely prevail."

*(They look at each other across the valley — the tall old man with his spear; the fugitive with his empty hands. It is the last time. Neither knows it. SAUL turns back to his place. DAVID goes his way.)*

**ABISHAI**
*(when they are far)* He will come again.

**DAVID**
Yes.

**ABISHAI**
We shall have to go on running.

*(DAVID looks back once at the fires.)*

**DAVID**
Then we go.

## Scene 22 — Ziklag, and the House of Achish

*The wandering camp at dusk: six hundred men, and now their households — women, children, cooking fires. DAVID stands looking at them. ABIGAIL and AHINOAM are folding a tent that has been folded too many times.*

**DAVID**
*(to himself)* "One day I shall be swept away by the hand of Saul."

*(JOAB at his shoulder.)*

**JOAB**
You are counting children again.

**DAVID**
There are more of them every spring. I cannot run a nursery from rock to rock.

**JOAB**
Then stop running it in his country.

*(DAVID looks at him.)*

There is one place Saul will not follow. You know it. You have been there once already.

**DAVID**
I scratched on their gate and dribbled in my beard.

**JOAB**
This time you would come with six hundred swords. A king looks at that differently.

*(ABISHAI has overheard.)*

**ABISHAI**
Gath? We are to go and live under the Philistines?

**DAVID**
I have been driven off the LORD's own land by the LORD's own king. I said it to his face from the hilltop. Did you think I was making a speech?

*(Quietly.)*

"There is nothing better for me than to escape into the land of the Philistines. Saul will give up seeking me within the borders of Israel; and I shall escape out of his hand."

*Gath. ACHISH on his seat. DAVID before him — upright, armed, six hundred at his back. ACHISH studies him with some amusement.*

**ACHISH**
The last time, you ate my doorpost.

**DAVID**
The king was gracious to a madman. I have come to see whether he will be gracious to a sane one.

**ACHISH**
*(laughs)* Saul's son-in-law, with Saul hunting him. I like it. Stay.

**DAVID**
"If I have found favor in your eyes, let a place be given me in one of the towns of the open country, and I will live there. Why should your servant live in the royal city with you?"

**ACHISH**
Ziklag. It is yours.

*Ziklag. A home is built before our eyes: mats unrolled; water jars set in a row; garments hung; a child's wooden toy put on a shelf. ABIGAIL directs it as she once loaded donkeys. AHINOAM sets the jars. Children run through doorways — real doorways. The men stand about, not knowing what to do in a place with walls.*

**AHINOAM**
*(to the BITTER MAN, who is holding a mat)* It goes on the floor. You remember floors.

*(He lays it down. Slowly, he sits on it. His child climbs onto him.)*

**ABIGAIL**
*(to DAVID)* There is a well inside the wall. Two ovens. A roof over every family, if we are sensible about who can bear whom.

**DAVID**
You are pleased.

**ABIGAIL**
I am housed. It is not the same thing; but tonight I shall not quarrel with it.

*(She looks at him.)*

And you?

*(DAVID watches the children.)*

**DAVID**
It is a Philistine's gift, in a Philistine's country, and there is no house of the LORD within three days' walk. And I have not seen them run like that. Ever.

*(He picks up the wooden toy, turns it over, sets it carefully back on the shelf.)*

*(A year and four months, in a breath: DAVID and the men go out and come in. ACHISH, each time:)*

**ACHISH**
Where did you raid today?

**DAVID**
Against the south country of Judah.

*(It is not where he raided. JOAB, behind him, looks at nothing.)*

**ACHISH**
*(to his court, content)* "He has made himself a stench to his own people Israel. He will be my servant forever."

*Gath. Trumpets; the Philistines are mustering for war — against Israel. ACHISH, armed.*

**ACHISH**
"Know this well: you go out with me in the host. You and your men."

*(A stillness in DAVID that ACHISH does not see.)*

**DAVID**
"Then you shall know what your servant will do."

**ACHISH**
*(delighted)* Then I will make you the keeper of my head, for life.

*(He goes. ABISHAI is there instantly.)*

**ABISHAI**
Against Israel. Against Jonathan.

**DAVID**
I said he would know what I will do.

**ABISHAI**
And what will you do?

*(DAVID does not answer. He does not know. That is plain on his face, and ABISHAI has never seen it there before.)*

**DAVID**
Get them ready to march.

*(The men form up. The women stand in the doorways. DAVID looks back once: the mats, the jars, the toy on the shelf. A house, standing.)*

## Scene 23 — At Endor / The Dismissal

*Two roads in one night. On one, SAUL goes down into the dark. On the other, DAVID marches north in the Philistine rear.*

*Gilboa. SAUL on the height, looking across at the Philistine camp at Shunem — fire beyond fire. His heart is shaking.*

**SAUL**
I asked. I asked by dreams. I asked by the Urim. I asked the prophets.

*(To the OLD SERVANT.)*

Nothing. Not a word.

*(A beat.)*

"Find me a woman who is mistress of a spirit, and I will go to her, and inquire of her."

**OLD SERVANT**
My lord — you drove them out of the land yourself.

**SAUL**
I know what I did. Find me one.

**OLD SERVANT**
There is a woman at Endor.

*(SAUL takes off the royal robe and puts on another man's cloak. For a moment the OLD SERVANT sees the young man who hid among the baggage.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "AT ENDOR" — after the poem by Shaul Tchernichovsky. Text to be selected from the poem; version to be checked. Not reproduced here.**
**Who begins:** ANOTHER VOICE — not SAUL.
**Physical action:** SAUL walks through the night with two men; changes his garment; reaches the woman's door. He does not stop the action to sing: his movement breaks down inside the music.
**What changes:** a king becomes a man asking in the dark.
**Exit:** the VOICE yields to speech at the woman's door, and returns when SAUL goes out into the night.]**

*Endor. A low house. The WOMAN. SAUL, hooded, with two men.*

**SAUL**
"Divine for me by a spirit. Bring up for me the one I name to you."

**WOMAN**
"You know what Saul has done — how he has cut off the mediums and the wizards from the land. Why are you laying a snare for my life, to get me killed?"

**SAUL**
"As the LORD lives — no punishment shall fall on you for this."

*(She looks at the tall hooded man who swears by the LORD in her house.)*

**WOMAN**
"Whom shall I bring up for you?"

**SAUL**
"Bring up Samuel."

*(She turns to her work. Then — she cries out, and wheels on him.)*

**WOMAN**
"Why have you deceived me? You are Saul!"

**SAUL**
"Do not be afraid. What do you see?"

**WOMAN**
"An old man is coming up. He is wrapped in a robe."

*(SAUL knows the robe. He bows with his face to the ground.)*

**A VOICE**
"Why have you disturbed me, and brought me up?"

**SAUL**
"I am in great distress. The Philistines are making war on me, and God has turned away from me. He answers me no more — not by prophets, not by dreams. So I have called you, to tell me what I should do."

**A VOICE**
"Why do you ask me, when the LORD has turned from you? The LORD has done as He spoke by my hand. He has torn the kingdom out of your hand, and given it to your neighbor — to David."

*(The name, at last. SAUL closes his eyes. He has always known it.)*

"And the LORD will give Israel also, with you, into the hand of the Philistines. Tomorrow — you and your sons will be with me."

*(SAUL falls his full length on the ground. There is no strength in him; he has eaten nothing all day and all night. The WOMAN comes to him. She is not afraid of him now.)*

**WOMAN**
"Your servant listened to your voice. I put my life in my hand. Now you listen to mine. Let me set a piece of bread before you. Eat — so that you have strength when you go on your way."

**SAUL**
I will not eat.

**WOMAN**
You will.

*(The two men urge him. He sits up, on her bed. She kills the calf she has, kneads flour, bakes unleavened bread; sets it before the king of Israel. He eats. It is the last table at which anyone is kind to him.)*

*(He stands. He is steady. He puts the borrowed cloak about him.)*

**SAUL**
*(to the two men)* Say nothing to Jonathan.

*(He goes out into the night, toward tomorrow.)*

*The Philistine muster at Aphek. Hundreds and thousands passing. In the rear, with ACHISH: DAVID and his men. The PHILISTINE COMMANDERS see them.*

**COMMANDER**
"What are these Hebrews?"

**ACHISH**
"Is this not David — the servant of Saul king of Israel — who has been with me these days, these years? I have found no fault in him from the day he came over to me."

**COMMANDER**
"Send the man back! Let him return to the place you gave him. He shall not go down with us to battle — or in the battle he will turn on us. With what would this fellow win back his master's favor? With the heads of our men!"

**SECOND COMMANDER**
"Is this not David, of whom they sing in their dances —"

*(He does not need to finish. For the third time:)*

**COMMANDERS**
"Saul has struck down his thousands,
and David his ten thousands."

*(ACHISH, unhappy, comes to DAVID.)*

**ACHISH**
"As the LORD lives" — you see, I swear by yours — "you are upright, and your going out and coming in with me in the host is good in my eyes. But in the eyes of the lords you are not good. So turn back. Go in peace."

**DAVID**
"But what have I done? What have you found in your servant, from the day I came before you until this day — that I may not go and fight against the enemies of my lord the king?"

*(ACHISH hears it one way. ABISHAI, behind DAVID, hears that it could be heard another.)*

**ACHISH**
"I know. You are as good in my eyes as an angel of God." But the commanders have spoken. "Rise early in the morning — you and your master's servants who came with you — and as soon as it is light, go."

*(First light. DAVID and his men turn south, away from the battle. Nobody says anything for a long time.)*

**ABISHAI**
You are smiling.

**DAVID**
Am I?

*(He is not, quite. He looks back once, north, toward Gilboa — toward Jonathan. Then he sets his face for Ziklag.)*

**DAVID**
Home.

## Scene 24 — No More Strength to Weep

*The road to Ziklag, the third day. The men are walking faster — they are nearly home. Then one of them stops, and lifts his face.*

**A MAN**
Smoke.

*(They run. Ziklag: the walls black, the roofs fallen in. The mats, the jars, the garments — the things we watched set in their places — lie scattered, overturned, in the wrong places. There are no bodies. There is no one at all.)*

**BITTER MAN**
Where are they?

*(No answer. Men go from doorway to doorway. Stones are shifted. Names are shouted.)*

**DAVID**
Abigail! — Ahinoam!

*(Nothing. The BITTER MAN finds, in the ash, a child's wooden toy. He holds it. He begins to weep. It goes through them like fire through stubble: six hundred men, weeping aloud. DAVID is on his knees among them. He weeps as they do. He makes no speech.)*

**A VOICE**
"And David and the people who were with him lifted up their voices and wept — until they had no more strength to weep."

*(Silence: the weeping has worn itself out. DAVID gets up, and goes among them — a hand on a shoulder; turning over a fallen door with another man, to be sure. Then:)*

**BITTER MAN**
*(not loud)* We marched out with you. We left them here.

**DEBTOR**
For a Philistine. To carry a Philistine's shield.

**BITTER MAN**
You brought us here. You said: a place at the fire. You said: where I do not go first.

*(He has a stone in his hand. So have others. They are moving toward DAVID — slowly; they are too tired for rage; it is worse than rage.)*

*(ABISHAI and JOAB step in front of DAVID, swords half out.)*

**DAVID**
No.

*(He puts them aside. He does not draw. He looks at the men with the stones: he knows every name.)*

You are right. I brought you here.

*(He turns his back on the stones and walks a few paces apart. He stands. He is breathing like a man who has run.)*

**DAVID**
*(low; to God; plain)* I do not know where they are. You know.

*(He stands a moment longer. His breath steadies. His back straightens.)*

**A VOICE**
"But David strengthened himself in the LORD his God."

*(He turns round. His voice is the commander's.)*

**DAVID**
Abiathar. "Bring me the ephod."

*(ABIATHAR brings it — the thing he carried out of Nob. The men with stones stop where they are.)*

"Shall I pursue this band? Shall I overtake them?"

*(ABIATHAR, the ephod in his hands, is still. Then he gives the answer he has received.)*

**ABIATHAR**
"Pursue. For you shall surely overtake — and you shall surely rescue."

*(DAVID turns to the six hundred.)*

**DAVID**
We go. Now.

*(The stones fall. They go.)*

*The brook Besor. Two hundred men are down at the water's edge and cannot get up — men who marched three days from Aphek and have not eaten. DAVID goes among them.)*

**DAVID**
Stay. Guard the baggage. That is an order, not a kindness.

*(He goes on with four hundred.)*

*Open country. They find a YOUTH lying in a field, nearly dead.*

**DAVID**
Water. Give him bread.

**ABISHAI**
We have no time —

**DAVID**
Water.

*(They give him water; bread; a piece of fig cake; two clusters of raisins. He eats. His spirit comes back into him. Only then:)*

**DAVID**
"Whose are you? Where are you from?"

**EGYPTIAN YOUTH**
"I am an Egyptian boy — slave to an Amalekite. My master left me behind, because I fell sick, three days ago." We raided the south. And Ziklag — we burned with fire.

*(The men stir. DAVID's hand holds them.)*

**DAVID**
"Will you bring me down to this band?"

**EGYPTIAN YOUTH**
"Swear to me by God that you will not kill me, and will not hand me over to my master — and I will bring you down."

**DAVID**
I swear it.

*The Amalekite camp, at twilight: spread out over all the ground, eating, drinking, dancing, over the great spoil. And, roped at the edge: the women; the children.*

*(DAVID looks. He finds ABIGAIL's face; AHINOAM's. He signs to his men. They strike — from twilight until the evening of the next day. Four hundred young men on camels get away. No one else.)*

*(The ropes are cut. ABIGAIL and AHINOAM come out from among the captives. The families find one another: it is the weeping again, turned inside out. The BITTER MAN has his child in his arms, and the toy.)*

*(DAVID holds his wives. Then he goes through the whole crowd — counting, as he once counted lambs out of a fold: touching each head.)*

**DAVID**
Is anyone missing? Small or great? Son or daughter?

**JOAB**
None.

**A VOICE**
"Nothing of theirs was missing, small or great, sons or daughters, spoil or anything that had been taken. David brought back all."

*The brook Besor. The two hundred come out to meet them. DAVID goes to them first.*

**DAVID**
Peace to you. Have you eaten?

*(Behind him, hard voices among the four hundred.)*

**A FIGHTER**
"They did not go with us. We will give them none of the spoil we recovered. Let each man take his wife and his children, and go."

**DAVID**
"You shall not do so, my brothers — with what the LORD has given us. He has kept us, and given the band that came against us into our hand. Who would listen to you in this?"

*(He stands between the four hundred and the two hundred.)*

"As is the share of the one who goes down into the battle, so is the share of the one who stays by the baggage. They shall share alike."

*(The FIGHTER looks at him. Steps back. The dividing begins. DAVID sits down with the men who could not cross.)*

## Scene 25 — The Lament

*Mount Gilboa. Not a battle: its end. Israel in flight. JONATHAN falls, and his brothers. SAUL — arrow-struck, the spear still in his hand — sees his son go down. His ARMOR-BEARER beside him.*

**SAUL**
"Draw your sword, and run me through with it — before these uncircumcised come, and run me through, and make sport of me."

*(The ARMOR-BEARER cannot. He is too much afraid. SAUL takes the sword himself, and falls upon it. Dark.)*

*Ziklag, among the ruins; the third day after the return. DAVID and his men. A YOUNG MAN comes in from the north: clothes torn, earth on his head. He falls to the ground before DAVID. In his hands, a crown and an armlet.*

**DAVID**
Where have you come from?

**AMALEKITE**
"I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**DAVID**
"How did the matter go? Tell me."

**AMALEKITE**
"The people fled from the battle. Many of the people have fallen and are dead. And Saul and his son Jonathan are dead also."

*(DAVID does not look at the crown.)*

**DAVID**
"How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?"

**AMALEKITE**
"I happened to be on Mount Gilboa. And there was Saul, leaning on his spear, and the chariots and horsemen closing on him. He turned and saw me, and called to me. He said: Who are you? I said: I am an Amalekite. He said: Stand over me, and kill me — for agony has seized me, yet my life is still whole in me."

*(He offers it up — proudly; he expects to be paid.)*

"So I stood over him, and killed him. For I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. And I took the crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm — and I have brought them here, to my lord."

*(He holds them out. DAVID takes hold of his own garments, and tears them. Around him, one after another, all his men do the same.)*

**BITTER MAN**
*(under his breath, to ABISHAI)* He is dead. It is over. Why are we tearing our clothes?

*(ABISHAI does not answer. He is looking at the AMALEKITE, and thinking of a night on the hill of Hachilah.)*

*(They mourn, and weep, and fast until evening — for Saul; for Jonathan; for the people of the LORD; for the house of Israel, fallen by the sword. Evening.)*

**DAVID**
*(to the AMALEKITE)* "Where are you from?"

**AMALEKITE**
"I am the son of a sojourner. An Amalekite."

**DAVID**
"How were you not afraid to put out your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"

*(The AMALEKITE understands, too late, what room he has walked into.)*

"Your blood is on your own head. Your own mouth has testified against you, saying: I killed the LORD's anointed."

*(He is taken out. It is brief. ABISHAI watches him go.)*

*(The crown lies on the ground where it was set down. DAVID does not go to it. His men draw in around him — not as soldiers about a victor; as mourners.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: DAVID'S LAMENT — 2 Samuel 1:19–27. Musical reference: "Eretz Tzvi." The setting of the verses is to be written; the reference song's own lyric is not used.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, alone.
**Addressed to:** Israel; then SAUL and JONATHAN; then JONATHAN only.
**Physical action:** one by one the men lay their weapons on the ground. DAVID never takes the crown.
**What changes:** it widens — one verse alone, one with the mourners — and then narrows to a single dead friend. It must not become a coronation song.]**

**DAVID**
"Your glory, O Israel, lies slain upon your heights.
How the mighty have fallen."

"Tell it not in Gath.
Publish it not in the streets of Ashkelon —
lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice."

*(A weapon is laid down. Another.)*

"Mountains of Gilboa —
let there be no dew, and no rain, upon you.
For there the shield of the mighty was cast away:
the shield of Saul."

**DAVID AND THE MEN**
"Saul and Jonathan —
beloved and gracious in their lives;
and in their death they were not divided.
They were swifter than eagles.
They were stronger than lions."

**DAVID**
"Daughters of Israel — weep for Saul."

*(The crown, to one side. He does not look at it. The others fall silent. He is alone with one of them now.)*

"I am in anguish for you, my brother Jonathan.
You were very dear to me.
Your love was more wonderful to me
than the love of women."

*(He cannot go on. Then he does.)*

"How the mighty have fallen —
and the weapons of war have perished."

*(A long stillness. The men stay round him. No one touches the crown.)*

## Scene 26 — Hebron: The Three Brothers

*DAVID, and ABIATHAR with the ephod.*

**DAVID**
"Shall I go up into one of the towns of Judah?"

**ABIATHAR**
"Go up."

**DAVID**
"Where shall I go up?"

**ABIATHAR**
"To Hebron."

*Hebron. The MEN OF JUDAH. Oil — for the second time. They anoint DAVID king over the house of Judah. He receives it soberly. Behind him stand the three sons of Zeruiah, together: JOAB, ABISHAI, ASAHEL — and ZERUIAH, watching her sons.*

**A VOICE**
"But Abner son of Ner, the captain of Saul's host, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and made him king over all Israel. Only the house of Judah followed David. And there was war between the house of Saul and the house of David."

*The pool of Gibeon. ABNER and his men on one side; JOAB and his on the other. They sit down facing each other across the water — the same people, in the same clothes.*

**ABNER**
"Let the young men get up, and play before us."

**JOAB**
"Let them get up."

*(Twelve rise on each side. They meet. Each takes his fellow by the head and puts his sword in his fellow's side. Twenty-four fall together. Nobody has won. Then all of it breaks loose.)*

*The pursuit. ABNER running — a veteran, running without panic. Behind him, gaining: ASAHEL, light as a gazelle, looking neither right nor left.*

**ABNER**
*(over his shoulder)* "Is that you, Asahel?"

**ASAHEL**
"It is."

**ABNER**
Turn aside. Right or left. Take one of the young men; take his armor for your prize.

*(ASAHEL keeps coming.)*

"Turn aside from following me! Why should I strike you to the ground? How could I lift my face to your brother Joab?"

*(ASAHEL does not turn aside. He is the boy who could outrun anything on those hills. ABNER, without turning, drives the butt of his spear backward. ASAHEL falls where he stands. Every man who comes to that place stops.)*

*(JOAB and ABISHAI do not stop. They pursue until the sun goes down. On a hilltop, ABNER and the men of Benjamin gather into one band. ABNER calls down.)*

**ABNER**
"Shall the sword devour forever? Do you not know that it will be bitter in the end? How long before you tell the people to turn back from pursuing their brothers?"

*(JOAB, sword in hand, the dusk around him.)*

**JOAB**
"As God lives — if you had not spoken, the people would not have left off following their brothers until the morning."

*(He lifts the ram's horn, and blows. The pursuit stops. The mourning does not.)*

*Hebron. They have buried ASAHEL in his father's tomb at Bethlehem and walked all night. JOAB and ABISHAI stand before DAVID — and between the two of them there is a space, the width of one man, which neither closes. ZERUIAH sits apart.*

**DAVID**
Joab. Abishai.

*(ABISHAI grips his brother's arm. JOAB does not answer.)*

I sent him for water. At Adullam. He said he could outrun anything on those hills.

**JOAB**
He could.

**ABISHAI**
Abner warned him. Twice. I will give him that.

**JOAB**
You may give him what you like. I give him nothing.

*(DAVID goes to ZERUIAH. He does not know what to say to his sister. She looks up.)*

**ZERUIAH**
Do not say anything true to me today.

*(He sits down beside her and says nothing. JOAB is looking at the empty space.)*

---

## Scene 27 — Abner

**A VOICE**
"The war between the house of Saul and the house of David was long. And David grew stronger and stronger; and the house of Saul grew weaker and weaker."

*Mahanaim. ISH-BOSHETH, a frightened man on a throne, and ABNER, who put him there.*

**ISH-BOSHETH**
"Why have you gone in to my father's concubine?" To Rizpah?

**ABNER**
*(a cold fury)* "Am I a dog's head? To this day I show loyalty to the house of your father Saul — I have not let you fall into the hand of David — and you charge me with a fault over a woman?"

"God do so to Abner, and more: as the LORD has sworn to David, so I will do for him — to carry the kingdom over from the house of Saul, and set up the throne of David over Israel and over Judah, from Dan to Beersheba."

*(ISH-BOSHETH cannot answer him a word.)*

*Hebron. ABNER's MESSENGERS before DAVID.*

**MESSENGER**
Abner says: "Whose is the land? Make your covenant with me — and my hand will be with you, to bring all Israel round to you."

**DAVID**
"Good. I will make a covenant with you. One thing only I require of you: you shall not see my face unless you first bring Michal, Saul's daughter, when you come."

*A road. MICHAL is led away from a house. Behind her walks a man — PALTIEL — weeping as he goes; he follows her mile after mile, to Bahurim. ABNER turns in the saddle.*

**ABNER**
"Go. Turn back."

*(PALTIEL stops. He stands in the road. He is still standing there when they are out of sight.)*

*Hebron. MICHAL is brought in to DAVID. Years have gone over both of them. She is still every inch a king's daughter.*

**DAVID**
You came.

**MICHAL**
I was fetched.

*(She looks at him: the king of Judah; no dust on him now.)*

There is a man standing on the road at Bahurim. He wept the whole way. No one has ever wept for me before. I thought you should know that something was taken, to bring me here.

**DAVID**
I asked for my wife.

**MICHAL**
You asked for Saul's daughter. Abner is bringing you Israel, and when Israel comes you want the old king's daughter standing beside you.

*(He begins to answer. She lifts a hand.)*

I am not reproaching you. I would have advised it.

**DAVID**
And the window?

**MICHAL**
I have not forgotten the window.

*(A beat. Neither has he. It is not enough; it is not nothing. She straightens the fall of his robe at the shoulder — the old gesture — and goes in.)*

*DAVID's hall. A feast: ABNER and twenty men. DAVID and ABNER, who hunted him across the wilderness, at one table.*

**ABNER**
I stood beside him at Elah when you walked down. He asked me whose son you were. I said: by your life, I do not know.

*(He drinks.)*

I know now.

**DAVID**
You were asleep at Hachilah.

**ABNER**
I was. You were right to shout it. I have not slept well since.

*(He rises.)*

"Let me arise and go, and gather all Israel to my lord the king. They shall make a covenant with you — and you shall reign over all that your heart desires."

**DAVID**
Go in peace.

*(ABNER goes — in peace. JOAB comes in from a raid, with spoil, and hears.)*

**JOAB**
"What have you done? Abner came to you. Why did you send him away, so that he is gone — gone!"

**DAVID**
I sent him away in peace.

**JOAB**
"You know Abner son of Ner. He came to deceive you — to learn your going out and your coming in, to learn everything you do."

**DAVID**
He came to bring me Benjamin without a battle. He came so that no more Asahels need be buried.

*(JOAB's face shuts at the name. He goes out.)*

*The gate of Hebron. JOAB's messengers have brought ABNER back from the cistern of Sirah. DAVID does not know. JOAB draws ABNER aside into the middle of the gate — a hand on his shoulder, as if to speak with him quietly.*

**JOAB**
A word. Apart.

*(ABNER leans to hear it. JOAB strikes him in the belly. ABNER dies there — "for the blood of Asahel his brother.")*

*DAVID's hall. A RUNNER.*

**DAVID**
Abner?

**RUNNER**
Dead. In the gate. Joab struck him.

*(JOAB comes in. He does not hide. ABISHAI behind him.)*

**DAVID**
"I and my kingdom are guiltless before the LORD, forever, of the blood of Abner son of Ner. Let it whirl about the head of Joab, and all his father's house."

**JOAB**
Asahel was my brother.

**DAVID**
I know whose brother he was. I sent him for water.

**JOAB**
You call that man a prince and a great man. I watched my brother go down in front of his spear.

**DAVID**
In a battle. Running him down. After he had begged the boy twice to turn aside — your own brother says so.

*(ABISHAI looks at the floor.)*

And you killed him in a gate. In a time of peace. With your hand on his shoulder.

**JOAB**
Dead is dead. He was Saul's sword for twenty years. He would have had your head on a pole at En-gedi and thought it a good day's work. And tomorrow he would have ridden into Benjamin as the man who gave David his crown — and what he gave, he could take.

**DAVID**
I had seen him walk in at my gate under a covenant. He came to me in peace.

*(They face each other: the brother who has lost a brother; the king who could not keep his own guest alive.)*

Do you know what you have told Israel today? That my word at my own table is worth nothing. That the house of David makes peace with its mouth and settles accounts in the gate. Every elder in Benjamin who was packing to come to Hebron is unpacking tonight.

**JOAB**
Then I have kept you from a mistake.

**DAVID**
You have kept me from a kingdom that did not need a war.

*(He turns to all of them. To JOAB, by name.)*

"Tear your clothes. Put on sackcloth. And mourn before Abner."

*(JOAB stares. It is an order. Slowly, he tears his garment.)*

*The burial. The bier of ABNER; and the king walks behind it — on foot. All the people watch. DAVID lifts up his voice at the grave, and weeps.*

**DAVID**
"Should Abner die as a fool dies?
Your hands were not bound.
Your feet were not put in fetters.
As a man falls before the sons of wickedness — so you fell."

*(All the people weep again. They bring him bread while it is still day.)*

**DAVID**
"God do so to me, and more, if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down."

*(The people see it. It is good in their eyes. That day all Israel understands that it was not from the king, to kill Abner son of Ner.)*

*(DAVID, to those nearest him — and JOAB, standing off in his torn clothes, can hear.)*

"Do you not know that a prince and a great man has fallen this day in Israel?"

*(Quietly. It is the plain truth, and it shames him.)*

"And I am weak today, though anointed king. These men — the sons of Zeruiah — are too hard for me. The LORD repay the doer of evil according to his evil."

*(He does not hide his mourning from JOAB. He does not dismiss him either.)*

---

## Scene 28 — The City of David

*Hebron. Two men, RECHAB and BAANAH, travel-stained, triumphant. One of them holds a bundle.*

**RECHAB**
"Here is the head of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, your enemy, who sought your life. The LORD has given my lord the king vengeance this day on Saul and on his seed."

*(DAVID looks at the bundle. He has been here before.)*

**DAVID**
"As the LORD lives, who has redeemed my life out of every trouble —"

"When a man told me, Saul is dead, and thought he was bringing good news, I seized him and killed him at Ziklag. That was the reward I gave him for his news."

"How much more, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house, on his own bed? Shall I not now require his blood at your hands?"

*(They are taken out. The head of ISH-BOSHETH is buried in the tomb of Abner. The house of Saul has ended; no one in DAVID's hall is permitted to be glad of the manner of it.)*

*All the tribes of Israel — their ELDERS — come to DAVID at Hebron.*

**ELDER**
"Here we are. We are your bone and your flesh. Yesterday, and the day before, when Saul was king over us — it was you who led Israel out and brought Israel in."

**SECOND ELDER**
"And the LORD said to you: You shall shepherd My people Israel. You shall be prince over Israel."

*(DAVID makes a covenant with them before the LORD. Oil — for the third time. The shepherd of Bethlehem is king over all Israel.)*

*Jerusalem. The stronghold of Zion is taken; a city goes up before our eyes — the elders and the people in a line, passing stone from hand to hand; gates raised; cedar from Tyre. A seat has been set for the king. It is empty: the king is in the line, passing stone.*

**A VOICE**
"And David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel — and that He had exalted his kingdom for the sake of His people Israel."

*(ZERUIAH comes through the work, the people of her house behind her. She stops by DAVID.)*

**ZERUIAH**
I once told you that you knew nothing about being a king.

**DAVID**
"Nothing. Neither did Saul."

**ZERUIAH**
You know something now.

**DAVID**
I know what it has cost you.

*(She accepts that. She looks at the walls.)*

**ZERUIAH**
Well. Have you come home?

*(DAVID looks at the city: a gate with no doors yet; a wall half-high; and, on the height above it, a bare place with nothing built.)*

**DAVID**
There is more to build.

*(He goes back to the line and takes the next stone.)*

---

## Scene 29 — The Ark and the Dance

*The road from Baale-judah. A new cart, oxen; on it, the Ark of God. All the house of Israel before it with lyres, harps, timbrels, rattles, cymbals — fast, bright, festive. DAVID among them, playing.*

*(At the threshing floor of Nacon the oxen stumble. The cart lurches. UZZAH, walking beside it, puts out his hand to the Ark, and takes hold of it — and falls, and lies dead beside the Ark of God.)*

*(Every instrument stops. DAVID stands with the lyre hanging from his hand. He is angry. And then he is afraid.)*

**DAVID**
"How can the Ark of the LORD come to me?"

*(He will not take it into the city. It is turned aside into the house of OBED-EDOM the Gittite.)*

**A VOICE**
"And the Ark of the LORD stayed in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite three months. And the LORD blessed Obed-edom, and all his house."

*(DAVID has waited three months outside a door.)*

**DAVID**
Not on a cart.

*(He has understood something.)*

On their shoulders. A step at a time.

*The second procession. No cart: BEARERS, the poles on their shoulders. Six paces — they halt; a sacrifice. Six paces more.*

**[MUSICAL CUE: THE ASCENT OF THE ARK. Options: "Shir LaMa'alot" (placement still provisional) for the carrying; "Why Did Michal Laugh?" as the anchor for the sequence that follows. Versions and use to be settled.**
**Who begins:** the BEARERS — the rhythm is the rhythm of men carrying weight.
**Addressed to:** God, before whom they walk.
**Physical action:** the measured step loosens; DAVID's dance is born out of the bearers' walk, not out of a halt in the procession. He has laid aside the royal robe; he wears a plain linen ephod, like any of the servants of the tent.
**What changes:** a controlled ascent becomes the joy of a whole people.
**Exit:** sharp. The window. Then speech.]**

*(DAVID dances before the LORD with all his might — leaping, whirling — the boy at the shearing, the boy on the road from Elah. The ram's horn; the shout. The people take fire from him.)*

*(Above, at a window: MICHAL. She watches the king of Israel leap and whirl in a linen shift among the serving-girls. Her face.)*

*(The Ark is set in its place, inside the tent DAVID has pitched for it. He offers; he blesses the people in the name of the LORD of hosts. Then he goes among them himself, handing out to every man and woman a loaf of bread, a portion of meat, a cake of raisins. He looks into each face. He does not stop until the last have theirs.)*

*(The people go home. DAVID, sweat-soaked, shining, turns to bless his own house. MICHAL has come out to meet him.)*

**MICHAL**
"How the king of Israel has honored himself today — uncovering himself today before the eyes of his servants' maids, as one of the worthless fellows shamelessly uncovers himself."

*(The joy stops in him as the instruments stopped at Nacon.)*

**DAVID**
It was before the LORD.

**MICHAL**
It was before all Jerusalem. I was at the window. I saw what they saw.

**DAVID**
And what did they see?

**MICHAL**
A man with his robe off. Jumping.

*(She is not sneering now; she means to be heard.)*

My father never once let Israel see him without his robe. Whatever else he was — and I know what else he was — he was a king every hour that anyone was looking. Do you think that was vanity? A crown is a people's belief that somebody stands above the scramble. You hold that belief in trust. You spent it this afternoon, in the street, to make the kitchen-girls clap.

**DAVID**
I was not thinking of the kitchen-girls.

**MICHAL**
You were not thinking. That is what I am telling you. I told you once: like a shepherd at a shearing.

**DAVID**
And I told you that I am a shepherd.

**MICHAL**
And I told you: not any longer. I let you down out of a window so that you might live to be a king. Not a spectacle.

*(That reaches him. And he answers — hard; harder than he has ever spoken to her.)*

**DAVID**
"Before the LORD — who chose me above your father, and above all his house, to appoint me prince over the people of the LORD, over Israel. Before the LORD I will make merry."

*(Her father. All his house. She takes it like a blow.)*

**MICHAL**
You need not tell me whose house was put down. I am what is left of it.

*(Silence. He is still breathing from the dance. He does not take it back; he finishes it — quieter.)*

**DAVID**
"And I will be lighter still than this. I will be low in my own eyes."

*(He looks at his own hands — the linen, the sweat.)*

Michal. It was the only hour since I left the sheep that I was not carrying anything.

*(She hears that. It does not change her mind.)*

"And with the maids you spoke of — with them I shall be held in honor."

*(She looks at him a long moment: the man she loved; the king she wanted. She inclines her head — to the king; exactly as much as is owed — and goes in. He does not follow.)*

**A VOICE**
"And Michal, the daughter of Saul, had no child until the day of her death."

---

## Scene 30 — A House for His Name

*The house of cedar. A table; on it, unrolled, a plan — a drawing of a building. Through the door, across a court: the tent of the Ark, its curtains moving. DAVID and NATHAN the prophet.*

**DAVID**
"Look. I am living in a house of cedar. And the Ark of God is living inside a curtain."

*(He cannot keep it in any longer. He pulls NATHAN to the table.)*

Here. Come and see. I have been drawing it at night. The court — here; wide, so that no one is kept standing in the road. The singers on these steps, facing east, and the morning sun behind the altar. Cedar for the walls; I have the cedar; Hiram will send more. And here —

*(His finger on one small mark at the threshold.)*

— a place just inside the door. A man could sit there with a lyre and trouble no one.

**NATHAN**
*(smiling)* The king has drawn himself a doorkeeper's bench.

**DAVID**
I asked for one thing, Nathan. When I was a boy with a horn of oil running into my eyes, Samuel asked me what I had wanted, and I told him: to live in the house of the LORD all my days. He said: and who will look after Israel? So I looked after Israel. I have had thirty years of caves and camps and councils.

*(He lays his palm on the plan.)*

There has never been the house. Not for Him; not for me. Now I can build it.

*(NATHAN looks at the plan, and at the man.)*

**NATHAN**
"Go. Do all that is in your heart. For the LORD is with you."

*(DAVID's face. NATHAN goes. DAVID stays by the table, into the night, moving a line; moving it back.)*

*Morning. NATHAN returns. He has not slept. DAVID, at the table, looks up — glad; and then sees his face.*

**NATHAN**
The word of the LORD came to me in the night.

*(DAVID stands.)*

"Go and say to My servant David: Thus says the LORD. Shall *you* build Me a house to dwell in? I have not lived in a house from the day I brought the children of Israel up out of Egypt to this day. I have gone about in a tent. In all the places I have walked with Israel, did I ever say one word to any of the rulers of Israel: Why have you not built Me a house of cedar?"

*(DAVID does not move.)*

"I took you from the pasture — from following the sheep — to be prince over My people Israel. I have been with you wherever you went. I have cut off all your enemies before you; and I will make you a great name."

"And the LORD declares to you that the LORD will make *you* a house."

**DAVID**
*(carefully)* And the house that I would build for Him?

**NATHAN**
"When your days are fulfilled, and you lie down with your fathers, I will raise up your seed after you, who shall come from your own body; and I will establish his kingdom. *He* shall build a house for My name."

*(Silence. DAVID sits down. He looks at the plan.)*

**DAVID**
Not I.

**NATHAN**
Not you.

**DAVID**
Why?

*(It is not rebellion. It is a man asking his orders.)*

**NATHAN**
I was not told why. I was told who.

*(DAVID nods slowly. He has heard a prophet say that kind of thing before.)*

**DAVID**
My son.

**NATHAN**
"I will be a father to him, and he shall be a son to Me. When he does wrong, I will correct him with the rod of men; but My kindness shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul, whom I removed from before you. Your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before you. Your throne shall be established forever."

*(It is more than he ever asked. It is not what he asked. NATHAN waits; there is nothing he can add. He goes.)*

*(DAVID alone. He looks toward the tent across the court. He gets up, and crosses to it, and goes in — and sits down before the LORD.)*

**DAVID**
"Who am I, Lord GOD — and what is my house — that You have brought me as far as this?"

*(He is weeping; he is also — it is unmistakable — giving thanks. Both.)*

"And even this was a small thing in Your eyes, Lord GOD. You have spoken of Your servant's house for a long time to come."

"What more can David say to You? You know Your servant."

*(A breath.)*

"And now, LORD God: the word that You have spoken concerning Your servant and his house — establish it forever. And do as You have spoken."

*(He comes back to the table. He looks at the plan once more: the court; the singers' steps; the little mark by the door.)*

**[MUSICAL CUE: "SHIRO SHEL ABBA" — anchor for the dream of the house. Placement proposed here; setting and words not final.**
**Who begins:** DAVID, to the plan — to a son who is not yet born.
**Physical action:** he does not tear it. He rolls it, slowly; ties it; and lays it up where a son may one day find it. The melody is born from the unrolling and the rolling, not as a number apart.
**What changes:** the builder becomes the one who prepares for a builder.
**Exit:** he lays his hand on the rolled plan, and leaves it there. The same roll will be in his hand at the threshing floor.]**

**DAVID**
*(to no one; to God; almost smiling)* You did not say I may not gather the cedar.

## Scene 31 — A Place at the Table

*The king's hall in Jerusalem. A long table. The household gathering to eat: DAVID's sons, small still; ABIGAIL; AHINOAM; MICHAL, in her own place, apart. One seat near the king's is empty, and has been for as long as there has been a table.*

**A VOICE**
"And David reigned over all Israel. And David did justice and righteousness for all his people."

*(DAVID has not sat down. He is looking at the empty seat. ZIBA — once a servant of the house of Saul — is brought before him.)*

**DAVID**
You are Ziba.

**ZIBA**
Your servant.

**DAVID**
"Is there anyone still left of the house of Saul — that I may show him kindness, for Jonathan's sake?"

*(At the table, MICHAL lifts her head.)*

**ZIBA**
*(wary; this question has killed people, in other kingdoms)* "There is still a son of Jonathan. He is lame in both his feet."

**DAVID**
A son.

*(It goes through him.)*

Where is he?

**ZIBA**
"In the house of Machir son of Ammiel. In Lo-debar."

**DAVID**
Bring him.

*Later. The table as it was. MEPHIBOSHETH is brought in — a young man, crippled in both feet, moving with difficulty, and terrified. He gets himself down onto his face before the king.*

**DAVID**
"Mephibosheth."

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
"Here is your servant."

**DAVID**
"Do not be afraid. I will surely show you kindness, for the sake of Jonathan your father. I will give back to you all the land of Saul your father. And you — you shall eat bread at my table, always."

*(MEPHIBOSHETH does not believe it. He bows lower.)*

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
"What is your servant — that you should turn and look at a dead dog like me?"

*(DAVID has gone still. He knows that phrase. He called it up a hillside once, with a piece of a robe in his hand.)*

**DAVID**
I called myself that. To your grandfather. He was hunting me through the rocks of En-gedi, and I shouted after him: Whom are you chasing? A dead dog.

*(He comes down from his place and — with some effort; he is not young — sits on the floor, in front of the young man.)*

Look at me.

*(MEPHIBOSHETH looks.)*

All the way here, you asked the men why the king wanted you.

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
No one would answer.

**DAVID**
And you were afraid.

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
I am of the house of Saul. I was five years old when the news came from Jezreel. My nurse picked me up and ran, and she fell with me. I have been running from this room ever since — as well as I can run.

**DAVID**
You are of the house of Saul. And you are Jonathan's son.

*(He has to stop for a moment.)*

You have his forehead. Do you remember him?

**MEPHIBOSHETH**
I remember being carried. I do not remember his face.

**DAVID**
I do.

*(Simply.)*

He took the robe off his own back and put it on mine, when I was nobody. He stood up at his father's table and asked what I had done, and a spear went past his head for it. He shot an arrow over a boy, in a field, to tell me to run — and then would not come with me, because he belonged at his father's side, and he was not a man who chose between the people he loved. I will tell you all of it. Every day, at that table, if you want it.

*(MEPHIBOSHETH is weeping. So, quietly, at her place, is MICHAL.)*

*(DAVID gets up. To ZIBA:)*

"All that belonged to Saul and to all his house I have given to your master's son. You shall work the land for him — you, and your sons, and your servants — and bring in the harvest."

The field is his. And the bread is here.

*(He goes to the table. He does not order the young man to rise; he draws back the empty seat with his own hands, and stands by it, and waits.)*

*(MEPHIBOSHETH gets to his feet — slowly, badly. No one helps him until MICHAL rises from her place, comes to him, and gives him her arm.)*

**MICHAL**
Come. Son of my brother.

*(She brings him to the seat. He sits. Only then does DAVID sit down beside him.)*

**DAVID**
*(quietly)* I promised your father.

*(He takes the bread, breaks it, puts the first piece into MEPHIBOSHETH's hand. The bread goes round the table from hand to hand — the house of David, and the house of Saul, at one board, and no one afraid.)*

*(The light goes slowly. The last thing seen is the seat that was empty.)*

**END OF ACT ONE**
